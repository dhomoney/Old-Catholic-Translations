6. *non alius de nobis vult*: see the nit.

## Findings

### 1. n. 2 Augustine's sentence is cut in two, and the English says the opposite of Augustine (meaning)

- Latin: *recognoscens verba quæ in quæst. 1 lib. 2 ad Simpl. posuerat: Quamvis sit in cujusque potestate quid possit; ait ideo illa dixisse, quia non dicimus esse in potestate nostra, nisi quod cum volumus fit...*
- English: "reviewing the words which he had set down ...: *Although it be in every man's power what he can do;* saith that he spoke them for this cause, *because we say not that anything is in our power, save that which is done when we will...*"
- Augustine's text (augustinus.it, *Ad Simplicianum* II, q. 1, and *Retractationes* II, 1, which quotes it): *Quamvis sit in cuiusque potestate quid velit, non est tamen in cuiusquam potestate quid possit.* The words *quid velit, non est tamen in cujusquam potestate* are missing: an eye-skip from *potestate* to *potestate*. As printed, the sentence says that what a man can do is in his power; Augustine says it is NOT. It also contradicts the reason Suarez quotes next (only what happens when we will is in our power) and his own point (liberty lies in the active power over our willing).
- The OCR has *quid / possit* across a line break (lines 1469-1470), so the loss may be the print's or the OCR's; the Scribe's header does not mention it.
- Fix: (a) Mose checks p. 5 in the archive.org scan (rpfranciscisuare07suar). If the OCR lost a line, the Latin is corrected and the English follows. (b) If the print reads as the OCR does, it is a printed reading that contradicts the author's argument: translate by the sense, "*Although it be in every man's power what he willeth, yet it is not in any man's power what he can do;*", and give a translator's note with the printed words and Augustine's text. Mose lists it in the header.

### 2. Note 3 gives the wrong place in *To Simplician* (note accuracy)

- Note 3: "they stand in Book I of *To Simplician*, quest. 2, where his text has *quid velit*".
- Problem: the sentence stands in Book II, question 1 (on "And the spirit of the Lord came upon Saul"), exactly as Suarez cites it (*quæst. 1 lib. 2 ad Simpl.*); *Retractationes* II, 1, 1 introduces it as the first of the questions on Kings. So the Scribe's doubt is unfounded, and the note's correction is itself wrong. The note is right that the *Retractationes* place is Book II, chap. 1, not Book I as Suarez has it.
- Fix: rewrite note 3 (or fold it into the note of finding 1): Suarez's "Book I of the *Retractations*" is II, 1 in the usual numbering; the place in *To Simplician* (II, q. 1) is right; Augustine's full sentence as in finding 1.

### 3. Note 5 states a reference that has not been checked (note accuracy)

- Note 5: "the question whether the will moves itself is Quodlibet X, quest. 9."
- Problem: I could not confirm this in a quick search, and Henry's best-known question on whether the will moves itself is usually cited from another Quodlibet. A reference given from memory should not be stated as fact. What can be said: Henry left fifteen Quodlibets, so "40" is wrong; "10" is a likely reading, since OCR easily takes 1 for 4.
- Fix: "the figure 40 is marked unverified by the Scribe and cannot be right, for Henry of Ghent left fifteen *Quodlibets*; perhaps 10 was printed." Name Quodlibet X, q. 9 only once it is checked.

## Nit (optional)

- n. 2 (Augustine, *De div. quæst. 83*, q. 8, wording confirmed on augustinus.it) *non alius de nobis vult*: "it is not another that willeth concerning us" is literal but unclear. The sense is "no one else does the willing for us". Suggest "it is not another that willeth for us".
