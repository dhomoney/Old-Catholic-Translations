## Answers to the translator's points

1. n. 8 'Priori modo denominatur arbitrium liberum' = 'free will is so denominated': right; 'so' carries *priori modo* well. 'voluntatis arbitrium' = 'the choice of the will': right, and it keeps Augustine's phrase apart from the defined term.
2. n. 9 'ut procedat ..., ut ... retineat, ut ... sinatur operari': I agree the power (*potentia libera*) is the subject of *retineat* and *sinatur*; *potestate sua* and *in manu ejus* only make sense of the power. The Latin sets the three *ut* clauses side by side as requirements; "in such wise that this retain ..." makes the second depend on the first. The sense holds, so no finding.
3. Note 3: checked. In the numbering now usual (Denzinger), Baius's proposition 39 is *Quod voluntarie fit, etiamsi necessario fiat, libere tamen fit* and 66 is *Sola violentia repugnat libertati hominis naturali*. Both English renderings are right. Keeping '[?]' with the gap is correct.
4. Note 1: checked against the Latin text of *De peccatorum meritis et remissione* on augustinus.it (book II): the words *voluntatis arbitrium, quod huc atque illuc liberum flectitur atque in eis naturalibus bonis est, quibus et male uti malus potest* stand in II, 18, n. 30. The note is right. Suarez changes the last clause (*quibus homo bene et male uti potest*); that is a Father, not Scripture, so no note is needed for it.

## Findings

### 1. n. 8 *sustinere actionem* is "to hold back", not "to sustain" (meaning)

- Latin: *ita ut in manu ejus sit velle aut nolle exercere vel sustinere actionem.*
- English: "so that it is in its hand to will or not to will to exercise or to sustain an action."
- Problem: mistranslation. *Sustinere* here is the classical "to hold back, check, keep back" (Lewis and Short, *sustineo* II B), set against *exercere*: the faculty can put forth the action or withhold it. That is the indifference the whole chapter turns on (n. 9: "to work or not to work"). English "to sustain an action" means to keep it going, so both verbs read as positive and the alternative is lost.
- Fix: "to exercise or to hold back an action" (or "to withhold"). A GLOSSARY row would help, since the Psalms rows give *sustinere* = "to wait on": `| sustinere (actionem) | to hold back (an action) | set against exercere; not "sustain" | Suarez, De gratia, Proleg. I, cap. 1 (002) |`.

## Nit (optional)

- n. 9 *jam impeditur, et tollitur libertas actus*: *jam* is logical here ("then", "by that very fact"), not temporal. "forthwith" suggests time. Suggest "then the liberty of the act is hindered and taken away".
