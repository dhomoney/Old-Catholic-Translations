## Findings

1. **Nit, note 5 (n. 6).**
   Latin: *ut patet ex August., lib. 1 contra Gaudent., c. 25, et epist. 48*
   English note 5: the figures "chap. 25" and "epistle 48" are marked doubtful by the Scribe (OCR text).
   Problem: "epist. 48" is very probably sound. In the old (pre-Maurist) numbering, Augustine's letter to Vincentius on compelling the Donatists, now Ep. 93, was Ep. 48, and that letter is exactly about such "coaction" by penalties and fears. I have not checked *Contra Gaudentium* I, 25.
   Fix (optional): "the figures are marked doubtful by the Scribe (OCR text). Epistle 48 in the older numbering is the letter to Vincentius, now Ep. 93, which treats of this coaction."

2. **Nit, wording (n. 6, end).**
   Latin: *simpliciter et absolute coactio non est, sed alicujus mali prohibitio*
   English: *it is not coaction simply and absolutely, but the prohibition of some evil*
   Problem: *prohibitio* here is the holding back or restraint of someone from an evil (by penalties), not a law forbidding it. "Prohibition" in English reads as the latter.
   Fix (optional): "but the restraining from some evil" (or "the hindering of some evil").
