## Findings

1. **Minor, probable misprint without a note (p. 185, Rom. iv paragraph).**
   Latin: *sicut enim sol corporalis non expellit tenebras, nisi lucem infundat, sic etiam sol justitiæ, et Pater hominum non remittit peccata*
   English: so also the sun of justice, and Father of men, doth not forgive sins
   Problem: the scan (PDF 196, 300 dpi) does read *Pater hominum*, so the Latin file is right. But the image is of light (sun, darkness, pouring in light), and "Father of men" next to "sun of justice" is odd. It looks like a misprint for *Pater luminum*, "the Father of lights" (James i, 17). I cannot prove it from this edition.
   Fix: keep "Father of men" and add a translator's note: "So the print (*Pater hominum*); perhaps a misprint for *Pater luminum*, 'the Father of lights' (James i, 17), which fits the image of light." Or, if Clark judges the misprint certain, translate "Father of lights" with the note (STYLE: misprints translated by the sense, with a note).

2. **Minor, Douay-Rheims wording (v. 3 paragraph).**
   Latin: *Thren.* III: « Vetustam fecit pellem meam, »
   English: "He hath made my skin old,"
   Problem: Douay-Rheims Lam. iii, 4 reads "My skin and my flesh he hath made old". Bellarmine quotes only part of the verse, but the English should take the Douay-Rheims words.
   Fix: "My skin ... he hath made old," (or "My skin he hath made old,").

3. **Nit, wording.**
   Latin: *toto cœlo aberrant adversarii*
   English: the adversaries err by the whole heaven.
   Fix (optional): "the adversaries are as far astray as heaven is wide" or "err by the whole breadth of heaven".
