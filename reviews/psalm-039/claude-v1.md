## Checks made

- Block-for-block check: Latin 101 blocks, English 101 blocks, MATCH.
- Douay-Rheims: all 24 verse lemmas compared word for word with drbo.org (chapter 21039, DR verses 2-18): all match. Note 1 (his 24 against DR 18) checked verse by verse: correct.
- Title: the print has no *ipsi*, so "Unto the end, a psalm for David" without "himself" is right (the same handling as Ps 35).
- Clark's point 2 (v. 9, note 5): I agree that the lemma should keep the Douay-Rheims "thou hast pierced ears for me" under STYLE 3. Note 5 states plainly that this answers to the Hebrew *fodisti*, which Bellarmine sets aside. In the discussion the Vulgate reading keeps its Latin with "thou hast perfected ears for me" beside it, so the argument stays readable. In my view there is no need to ask god.
- Quotations checked: Prov. xviii, 3; I Cor. x, 4; John xiv, 6 (note 2); Ps. xviii, 2 (note 3); Wis. i, 7 (note 4); Heb. x, 4 (note 6), 10; IV Kings vi, 23 (note 7); Ps. xvii, 45 (note 8); John iv, 34, vi, 38, viii, 29; II Cor. v, 21; Gen. ii, 23-24; Eph. v, 32; Ps. i, 2; John i, 1 and 14; Luke xxiv, 27 and 44; John v, 39; Heb. viii, 1; Rom. x, 4; Ps. xxxvi, 31 (note 9); Matt. xvii, 5; Isa. liii, 6; Mark vi, 5-6; Ps. xxxiv, 25; Matt. xxvii, 40 (note 10). All right; notes 2-10 agreed.
- Clark's points 3-6: agreed. *forta-se*, *edit.o* and the placing of *Matth. III et XVII* are Latin matters and do not affect the English.
- Doctrine: preventing grace before the sinner's cry (v. 2), the figurative sacrifices fulfilled in the one oblation of Christ (v. 9-10), the Vulgate approved at Trent, Christ taking our sins as "my iniquities", Christ's knowledge and the improper sense of "could not" and "wondered" (v. 16-17): all rendered faithfully.
