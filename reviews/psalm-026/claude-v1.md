## Findings

1. **Minor: the Latin form is quoted wrongly** (v.18 commentary)
   - Latin: *petit enim non tradi in animam, id est, voluntatem et desiderium hostium*. The print (PDF 166) has *in animam*.
   - English: "to the soul (*in animas*), that is, the will and desire".
   - Problem: Bellarmine's gloss uses the singular. *in animas* is the verse's form, not his.
   - Fix: "(*in animam*)".

2. **Minor: misprint without a note** (v.12 heading)
   - Latin: **... QUA CLAMAVI AT TE ...**; the print (PDF 164) has *AT TE*, as the Scribe's header records.
   - English: "WITH WHICH I HAVE CRIED TO THEE" (right by the sense).
   - Problem: by STYLE, a misprint is translated by the sense and gets a note, as is done for *quum* (note 2).
   - Fix: add a note: "the print has *AT TE*, a misprint for *AD TE*."

3. **Nit: terms used inconsistently** (v.8 commentary)
   - Latin: *ex qua ineffabilis voluptas et delectatio sequitur*.
   - English: "from which unspeakable pleasure and delight followeth".
   - Problem: in this same paragraph and in the new glossary rows, *voluptas* = "delight" and *delectatio* = "delectation". Here *voluptas* becomes "pleasure", and "delight" stands for *delectatio*.
   - Fix: "from which unspeakable delight and delectation followeth".

4. **Nit: note 5 could name the simpler reading**
   - Latin: *ut a recta via divinæ nos deducant* (print confirmed, PDF 166).
   - The English supplies "law". That works, but a one-letter misprint *divinæ* for *divina* ("from the right divine way") needs no word to fall out, and the sense is the same.
   - Fix (optional): note 5: "the print has *a recta via divinæ*; either a noun such as *legis* has dropped out, or *divinæ* is a misprint for *divina*. Rendered 'the right way of the divine law'."
