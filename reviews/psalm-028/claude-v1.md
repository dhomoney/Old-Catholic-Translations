## Findings

1. **Nit: optional note** (v.3)
   - Latin: *« Spiritus Domini ferebatur super aquas »* (Gen. i, 2).
   - English: "The spirit of the Lord moved over the waters".
   - The English follows Bellarmine, which is right. The Vulgate (and the Douay-Rheims) have *Spiritus Dei*, "the spirit of God". If the house practice of noting such variants applies here, add: "Bellarmine has *Domini*; the Vulgate, *Spiritus Dei*."

2. **Nit: grammatical term** (v.8)
   - Latin: *in græco est nomen commune ἐλάφους*.
   - English: "in the Greek it is a common noun, ἐλάφους".
   - Problem: *nomen commune* here means a noun of common gender (masculine or feminine), as opposed to the Hebrew feminine. In modern English, "common noun" means something else.
   - Fix: "in the Greek it is a noun of common gender, ἐλάφους".

For the Scribe: v.2 *nominis* → *nomini* (twice, as above), and list *acilitate*.
