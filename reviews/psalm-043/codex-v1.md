1. **Verse 6 commentary: explain the printed contradiction (STYLE §§6, 8; doubtful reading/misprint).**
   - Latin: *nonnulli volunt supervacaneum esse illud, et ... et exponunt, tu es Rex meus et Deus meus.*
   - Current English: “some will have that *et* (and) to be superfluous ... and they expound, *tu es Rex meus et Deus meus* (thou art my King and my God).”
   - Problem: the proposed reading still contains the very conjunction these interpreters would remove. The scan, PDF p. 295, confirms the Scribe and Translator have reproduced the print; this is no transcription error. But without a note the reader cannot tell why the claimed alternative is identical to the reading defended. The translation needs to identify the apparent printed slip, as it already does for the title reference and the Mark quotation.
   - Suggested fix: retain the printed Latin and English, and add a brief note: “Translator’s note: so the print, retaining *et* in the alternative reading, although the preceding sentence calls it superfluous. The argument would require *tu es Rex meus, Deus meus* (thou art my King, O my God), with *Deus* in the vocative.” Alternatively translate that evident intended sense and document the printed wording in the note under STYLE §8. Do not silently emend the Latin source.

2. **Verse 10 commentary: supply the meaning of the Italian example (omission).**
   - Latin: *Similis est phrasis italica, quando dicimus: Mi lodo del tale.*
   - Current English: “Like to it is the Italian phrase, when we say: *Mi lodo del tale,*”.
   - Problem: the Italian has been copied without an English rendering. The example explains how a form of “praise” can express satisfaction or glory in another; an English reader cannot follow that comparison without its sense.
   - Suggested fix: “Like to it is the Italian phrase, when we say: *Mi lodo del tale* (I am well pleased with such a one)”. Keep the Italian because its form matters. The sense of *lodarsi di qualcuno* is confirmed by [Treccani, lodare](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/lodare/).
