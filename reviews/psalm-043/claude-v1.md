## Findings

1. **Misprint translated as it stands, no note (affects the argument).** v. 6 commentary (p. 284). Latin: *Hoc loco nonnulli volunt supervacaneum esse illud, et, ... et exponunt, tu es Rex meus et Deus meus.* Current English: "some will have that *et* (and) to be superfluous ... and they expound, *tu es Rex meus et Deus meus* (thou art my King and my God)." Problem: the sentence says these interpreters drop the *et*, then quotes their reading with the *et*. A few lines later he says the Seventy kept *et* to show that *Deus* "is not of the vocative case, but of the nominative", so the reading he reports is *tu es Rex meus, Deus* (vocative: "thou art my King, O God"), as in the Hebrew. The printed *et* is a misprint (confirmed on the page image, so not a Scribe slip). As it stands the English makes his argument contradict itself. STYLE: a misprint of the print is translated by the sense and gets a note. Fix: render the reading without *et*, e.g. "*tu es Rex meus, Deus meus* (thou art my King, O my God)", with a translator's note: "the print has *tu es Rex meus et Deus meus*, with the *et* which these interpreters leave out." Mose should list it in the Latin header as a misprint kept as printed.

2. **STYLE 4: Italian phrase without English (does not affect meaning).** v. 10 commentary (p. 284). Latin: *Similis est phrasis italica, quando dicimus: Mi lodo del tale.* Current English: "*Mi lodo del tale,*" with no English. He discusses the phrase as a phrase, so STYLE 4 asks for the English in brackets. Fix: "*Mi lodo del tale* (I am well pleased with such a one)", or a literal "(I praise myself of such a one)" if you want to show the reflexive he is pointing at.
