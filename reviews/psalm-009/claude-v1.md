## Findings

1. **Verse 6, commentary (minor, wording).**
   - Latin: *quia nihil nocere possunt armatis fide Jesu Christi.*
   - English: "because they can hurt nothing those who are armed with the faith of Jesus Christ."
   - Problem: *nihil* is adverbial ("in no way"). "Hurt nothing those who" reads as ungrammatical.
   - Fix: "because they can in no wise hurt those who are armed with the faith of Jesus Christ."

2. **Verse 21, commentary (minor, optional note).**
   - Latin: *Ego Constantinum intelligo, Jovinianum, Valentinianum, Theodosium, et similes.*
   - English: "I understand Constantine, Jovinian, Valentinian, Theodosius, and the like."
   - Problem: "Jovinian" is right as printed, but an English reader will take it for Jovinian the heretic whom St. Jerome wrote against. The list is of Christian emperors, so the Emperor Jovian (*Jovianus*, 363-364) is evidently meant. STYLE 6 allows a note for a doubtful reading in the source.
   - Fix (optional): keep "Jovinian" and add: "Translator's note: so printed (*Jovinianum*); the Emperor Jovian (*Jovianus*) is evidently meant."

3. **Verse 5, commentary (minor, register).**
   - Latin: *arguens « mundum de peccato, de justitia, et de judicio »*
   - English: convincing "the world of sin, of justice, and of judgment"
   - Problem: *arguens* is outside the quotation marks, so the verb is Bellarmine's own and "convincing" is allowed. But the drbo.org Challoner text of John xvi, 8 reads "he will convict the world of sin". A modern reader may take "convincing" to mean "persuading".
   - Fix (optional): "convicting".
