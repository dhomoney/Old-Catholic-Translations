## Findings

1. **Minor, print omission (v. 29 commentary), note suggested.**
   Latin: *« Quoniam, inquit, Dominus amat judicium, » id est, justos servos suos; ideo affirmo, quod « in æternum conservabuntur. »*
   English: *"For, saith he, the Lord loveth judgment," that is, his just servants; therefore I affirm that "they shall be preserved for ever."*
   Problem: the English follows the print, which is right. But the gloss "his just servants" plainly belongs to *sanctos suos* ("and will not forsake his saints"), which the print leaves out of the lemma; as it stands the reader is told that "judgment" means "his just servants". The text layer confirms the print reading, so this is Bellarmine's or the printer's skip, not the Scribe's.
   Fix (optional): a translator's note at "his just servants": "so the print. The gloss belongs to the words of the verse left out here, 'and will not forsake his saints'."

2. **Nit, wording (margin at v. 23).**
   Latin: *[Margin: Cur pauperes minus sæpe habent quam divites?]*
   English: *[Margin: Why do the poor less often have than the rich?]*
   Problem: the English is not idiomatic and hard to parse. The Latin allows *minus sæpe* "less often" (with *habent* absolute, "have enough"), which fits the paragraph (the rich sinner is always in debt; the just man, with a little, has wherewith to give).
   Fix (optional): "Why are the poor less often in want than the rich?", or, more literal, "Why have the poor less often than the rich?"

3. **Nit, addition (v. 27 commentary).**
   Latin: *ut vel ex misericordia donet, vel certe mutuo, aut commodato det proximis de facultatibus suis.*
   English: *that either he giveth out of mercy, or at least giveth to his neighbours of his means as a loan, whether of a thing to be repaid or of a thing to be used and returned.*
   Problem: "whether of a thing to be repaid or of a thing to be used and returned" explains the legal difference between *mutuum* and *commodatum* inside Bellarmine's sentence; it is the translator's gloss, not his words.
   Fix (optional): "or at least lendeth (*mutuo, aut commodato*) to his neighbours of his means", with the explanation in a note if wanted.

4. **Nit, note 4 (v. 7 commentary).**
   Latin: *ut idem S. Hieronymus eamdem vocem interpretatur in Psalm. XXV, vers. 12*
   Note 4: Ps. xxv, 12 does not have the word; Ps. xx, 12 is meant.
   Problem: Ps. xxv (Heb. xxvi), 10 has the same word in the singular, זמה, and Jerome's Hebrew Psalter there has, as far as I recall, *in quorum manibus scelera*; so the slip may be in the verse number rather than the Psalm number. I have not checked Jerome's text at either place.
   Fix (optional): "so the print. Ps. xxv, 12, does not have this word; it stands at Ps. xxv, 10, and Ps. xx, 12; one of these seems to be meant." Keep Ps. xx, 12 alone if you have checked Jerome there.

5. **Nit, wording (two places).**
   - v. 19-20 commentary: *Sed hæc interdum implentur in hoc sæculo, semper autem implebuntur in futuro.* English: "But these things are sometimes fulfilled in this world, but shall always be fulfilled in the world to come." Two "but"s; fix: "Now these things are sometimes fulfilled in this world, but shall always be fulfilled in the world to come."
   - v. 35 commentary: *cum ille ... judicandus erit*. English: "(when he shall be to be judged)". Fix: "(when he is to be judged)".
