## Findings

1. **Minor, addition (v. 9-10 paragraph).**
   Latin: *Salvasti etiam tunc animam meam, id est, de angustiis, in quibus necessario hærebam*
   English: Thou hast also then saved my soul "out of distresses" (*de necessitatibus*), that is, out of the straits...
   Problem: the words "out of distresses" (*de necessitatibus*) are not in Bellarmine's sentence; the Translator supplies the lemma words his "id est" seems to gloss. The sense is not changed, but it is an unmarked addition.
   Fix: either "Thou hast also then saved my soul, that is, out of the straits, in which I stuck fast of necessity..." or keep the supplied words and add a translator's note that they are supplied from the verse.

2. **Minor, wording (v. 30-31 paragraph).**
   Latin: *Retribuet, inquit, his qui satis operantur superbiam*
   English: (He will repay them that work pride enough)
   Problem: Jerome's *satis* here means "greatly, abundantly"; that is why Bellarmine cites it, as the match of *abundanter* joined to *facientibus*. "enough" reads as "sufficiently" and hides the point.
   Fix: "(He will repay them that work pride abundantly)" or "...exceedingly".

3. **Minor, Scripture quoted loosely (v. 23-24 paragraph).**
   Latin: *Psalm. XCIII: « Secundum multitudinem dolorum meorum consolationes tuæ lætificaverunt animam meam; »*
   English: "According to the multitude of my sorrows, thy comforts have given joy to my soul"
   Problem: the Vulgate (Ps. xciii, 19) has *in corde meo* after *dolorum meorum*; Douay-Rheims "the multitude of my sorrows in my heart". The English rightly follows Bellarmine, but the house practice is a translator's note naming the Vulgate reading when he quotes loosely (as note 7 does).
   Fix: add a note: "Bellarmine leaves out *in corde meo* (in my heart) of the Vulgate."

4. **Nit, wording (v. 3 paragraph).**
   Latin: *Dicuntur autem hæc omnia plane ad rem propositam aptissime.*
   English: Now all these things are said plainly most fitly to the matter in hand.
   Fix: "Now all these things are said most fitly, plainly, to the matter in hand", or "...are said quite plainly and most fitly to the matter in hand."

5. **Nit, for the Scribe, not the English.** v. 29 Latin file *differem* should be *diffiderem* (scan PDF 192, line split *diffi-/derem*). Already reported to Mose by Clark.
