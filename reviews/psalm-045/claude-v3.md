## Findings

### 1. v. 7: Ps. cii, 20 not in the Douay-Rheims wording

- Latin: "juxta illud *Psalm.* CII: « Potentes virtute facientes verbum illius. »"
- English: "Mighty in strength, executing his word."
- Problem: the Douay-Rheims (Ps. cii, 20) has "you that are mighty in strength, and execute his word". He only shortens the verse, so STYLE 3 asks for the Douay-Rheims wording of the words he keeps.
- Fix: "You that are mighty in strength, and execute his word." (or "Mighty in strength, and execute his word").

### 2. STYLE 3: two quotations in words that differ from the Vulgate, with no note

- (a) v. 5, II Pet. i, 19: "Habetis firmiorem propheticum sermonem": the Vulgate has *Et habemus* ("And we have"). The English follows him ("You have") but gives no note. Add one: "Bellarmine's text has *Habetis* (you have); the Vulgate, II *Pet.* i, 19, has *habemus* (we have)."
- (b) vv. 2-3, Wis. v, 2: "« impii terræ amatores turbabuntur timore horribili »": the Vulgate has *Videntes turbabuntur timore horribili*. "The wicked lovers of the earth" is his own wording inside the quotation marks, not a shortening. Add a note giving the Vulgate words (Douay-Rheims "These seeing it, shall be troubled with terrible fear").
