# Psalm 45 — Codex review v2

**CHANGES**

English: `d2a957a`, `english/psalm-045.md`. Latin: `d832916`, `latin/psalm-045.md`. This is the full initial review of v2, which superseded v1 before review; the English/header diff from v1 is header-only.

1. **Scribe finding — three closing guillemets omitted.** On printed p. 304 / PDF 315, right column, the scan reads *« Conturbatæ sunt, inquit, gentes, » quia propinquat interitus earum* (v. 6), and *« Dominus virtutum, » id est exercituum, « nobiscum est, susceptor noster, » id est* (v. 7). Current Latin lacks all three closing marks, giving *gentes, quia*, *virtutum, id est* and *noster, id est*. Verified each at 600 dpi: these are transcription omissions, not unclosed quotations in the print. Suggested fix: restore the three marks in the Latin. The present English quotation closures are correct; no Translator’s note is needed. Update its header to the final source commit.

2. **Verse numbering — concordance omitted (STYLE 3).** The source and English number eleven verses, while Douay-Rheims numbers twelve including the title, and also divides the words around verses 8–9 differently. Suggested fix: add a note: Bellarmine 1–7 correspond to Douay-Rheims 2–8; his 8 contains Douay-Rheims 9 and the opening “Making wars to cease...” of 10; his 9 contains the rest of 10; his 10–11 correspond to 11–12. Keep the present divisions. Checked against [Douay-Rheims Psalm 45](https://www.drbo.org/chapter/21045.htm).

3. **Readings discussed as readings — STYLE 4.** Latin *Adjutor in tribulationibus inventus est nimis* in v. 1 becomes only “A helper in tribulations he hath been found exceedingly”; *invenerunt* becomes “have found”. In vv. 2–3, *eorum / marium*, *in cor maris / in corda marium*, and the word *maris* are given only in English although the pronoun and number are the argument. Vv. 4–5 similarly give *fluminis rivi / divisiones*, *fluminis impetus* in the actual comparison, and the ancient *Adjuvabit eam Deus vultu suo* only in English; v. 8 gives Jerome’s *quantas posuerit desolationes in terra* only in English; v. 9 discusses *scuta / currus* only as “shields / chariots”; v. 10 gives the Hebrew rendering *cessate et videte* only as “cease and see”. Suggested fix: retain the Latin in italics with an English gloss at first mention in each comparison. The already supplied Latin for *virtus*, *fluminis impetus* in the introductory quotation, *Dominus virtutum*, *prodigia*, *arma* and *vacate* is helpful but does not cover these other grammatical/readings comparisons. Mere explanatory repetitions can remain English.

4. **Reference labels — STYLE 3.** Latin *Psalm.*, *Sap.*, *Apocalyp.*, *II Epist. cap. I* of Peter, and *Malachias cap. IV* appear as *Psalm*, *Wisd.*, *Apocal.*, II *Epist.* and unformatted “Malachias, chap. iv”. English also gives the reference *Actis Apost.* as *Acts of the Apostles* and leaves Ps. unitalicised in note 4. Suggested fix: use *Ps.*, *Wis.*, *Apoc.*, II *Pet.* i, *Mal.* iv, *Acts* ii and iii, and italic *Ps.* in note 4. Preserve the wrongly printed chapter numbers which the notes already correct.
