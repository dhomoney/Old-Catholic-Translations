## Findings

1. **Minor: attribution phrase** (p. 61)
   - Latin: *ut Guitmundus libro I contra Berengarium auctor est*
   - English: "as Guitmund is authority in Book I against Berengarius"
   - Problem: *auctor est* means "relates, is the source for". This is the same construction as *Irenæus auctor est* in unit 004, now "relateth".
   - Fix: "as Guitmund relateth in Book I against Berengarius".

2. **Minor: wrong name, no note** (p. 59; Clark's point 4)
   - Latin: *Julium Halicarnasseum* (print confirmed).
   - English: "Julius of Halicarnassus".
   - Problem: the Monophysite bishop of Halicarnassus (6th century) is Julian (*Julianus*). Under STYLE a wrong or doubtful name or reference is kept as printed and given a note. Otherwise the reader is left with a person who does not exist.
   - Fix: keep "Julius" and add a note: "so the print; the bishop meant is Julian (Julianus) of Halicarnassus." *Petrum Gnapheum* = "Peter Gnapheus" (Peter the Fuller) is fine as it stands.

3. **Nit: optional note** (p. 61; Clark's point 5)
   - Latin: *quæstiones ... quæ sunt de gratia hominis, de gratiæ amissione ...*
   - English: "of the grace of man" is right as printed.
   - Suggestion: the treatise this announces is Bellarmine's *De gratia primi hominis* (on the grace of the first man, before the Fall). That is why the loss of grace follows next. A short note would help the reader, but it is optional.

4. **Nit: wording** (p. 59)
   - Latin: *ut non nisi longo tempore, et multis multorum laboribus et sudoribus, tantis malis remedium adferri possit*
   - English: "that not but in a long time, and by the many labours ... can a remedy be brought"
   - Fix: "that only in a long time, and by the many labours and sweats of many, can a remedy be brought to so great evils".
