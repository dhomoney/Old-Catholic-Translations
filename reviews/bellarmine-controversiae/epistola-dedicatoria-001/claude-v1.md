## Translator's points

1. "thou", "thy Holiness", "thy Beatitude" to the Pope: follows STYLE 8.
2. Notes 2 and 3: both readings construe badly as printed, and the English follows the evident sense; notes are allowed for misprints of the edition (STYLE 8). Note 1 (*Eecclesiæ*) is right by the same rule.
3. *Pontificatum* (scan) for the file's *Pontificatus*: confirmed on the text layer of PDF p. 70; the English "that thy Pontificate be very long" follows the scan, with no note (STYLE 8).
4. "not one to be sought who might be chosen, but one to be chosen who stood pre-eminent": correct; it keeps the play on *legere* (to choose).
5. "that I should at length commit to the press ... compelled me": correct for the bare subjunctive after *compulit*.
6. "answering to thy name ... happy" with no note: acceptable. The pun on Felice Peretti is lost in English, but a note for it is not among the kinds STYLE 6 allows.
7. Footnote line (1)-(2) translated in place, with the split sentence kept split: follows STYLE 8.

## Findings

1. **Paragraph 2 (minor, wording).**
   - Latin: *operam atque industriam tuam Ecclesiæ Catholicæ ita probasti*
   - English: "hast in this spiritual battle so approved thy labour and industry to the Catholic Church"
   - Problem: *probare alicui* is "to prove (show) to someone", "to commend to". In present English "approved thy labour" reads as though the Pope gave his own approval to his own labour.
   - Fix: "hast ... so proved thy labour and industry to the Catholic Church".
