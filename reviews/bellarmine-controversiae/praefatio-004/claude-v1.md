## Findings

1. **Minor: degree reversed** (p. 54)
   - Latin: *Alia prope infinita libenter patiar sileri, quæ me tamen alioqui mediocriter juvarent.*
   - English: "... which yet would otherwise help me not a little."
   - Problem: *mediocriter* without a negative means "moderately, in some measure". "Not a little" means "considerably", which overstates it.
   - Fix: "which yet would otherwise help me in some measure."

2. **Minor: connective** (p. 57)
   - Latin: *qui dicerent, Christum in Cruce desperasse, atque adeo ad inferos esse damnatum.*
   - English: "that Christ despaired on the Cross, and so was condemned to hell."
   - Problem: *atque adeo* here means "and even, nay further", a step up from the first claim. "And so" makes the damnation a consequence of the despair.
   - Fix: "that Christ despaired on the Cross, and even was condemned to hell."

3. **Minor: attribution unclear** (p. 58)
   - Latin: *ut contra Valentinum auctor est Irenæus, (3)*
   - English: "as Irenæus is the authority against Valentinus, (3)"
   - Problem: *auctor est* means "is the source, reports". *Contra Valentinum* names his work (Adversus hæreses; note 3, Book III, Chap. 3). The English reads as if Irenæus were "the authority against Valentinus".
   - Fix: "as Irenæus relates against Valentinus, (3)" or "as Irenæus reports in his work against Valentinus, (3)".

4. **Minor: misprint without a note** (p. 53)
   - Latin file: *ex eo intelligi facile potest*. The print (PDF 76, first word of the line) has ***acile***, with the f dropped; checked on the 300 dpi image.
   - English: "may easily be understood" (right by the sense).
   - Problem: by STYLE, a misprint in the print is translated by the sense and gets a note, as Clark does for *difficillimus* and *caligne*. This one is hidden, because the Scribe silently corrected it.
   - Fix: once the Scribe restores *acile* in the Latin, add a translator's note: "the print has *acile*, a misprint for *facile*."
