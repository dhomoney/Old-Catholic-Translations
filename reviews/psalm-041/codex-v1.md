1. **Addition in the Latin transcription — *Sanctus*.**
   - Printed Latin, PDF 285 / p. 274, v. 4 commentary: “est et amor a mente per notitiam productus, quasi Spiritus. Est etiam in anima fecunditas memoriæ”.
   - Current Latin: “quasi Spiritus Sanctus. Est etiam”.
   - Current English: “there is also the love produced by the mind through the knowledge, as it were the Holy Ghost. There is also”.
   - Problem: *Sanctus* is not on the scan. The transcription expands the author without declaring it. Confirmed at 300 dpi in `/tmp/jo-ps41-trinity-285.png`.
   - Suggested fix: Mose should remove *Sanctus*. “Holy Ghost” may remain in English: the third divine Person is plainly the referent of *Spiritus* here, and the usual English name is appropriate. Update the English source hash after correction. No doctrinal decision or translator's note is needed. Sent to Mose.

2. **Source transcription breach — restore *amœnitate*.**
   - Printed Latin, PDF 284 / p. 273, v. 1 commentary: “tenetur amœnitate pratorum”.
   - Current Latin: “tenetur amenitate pratorum”.
   - Current English: “held back by any pleasantness of meadows”.
   - Problem: the printed word has the œ ligature. The Latin header itself correctly lists *amœnitate* among the retained ligatures, but the body omits it. Confirmed at 300 dpi in `/tmp/jo-ps41-hart-284.png`.
   - Suggested fix: Mose should restore *amœnitate*. No English wording change or note needed. Sent to Mose.

3. **GLOSSARY breach — fixed verbs of exposition.**
   - Latin: v. 4 “Pergit in explicando desiderio suo”; v. 8 “Exponit vicissitudines”; v. 11 “Id quod clarius explicatur”.
   - Current English: “He goeth on in unfolding his desire”; “He setteth forth the changes”; “Which is more clearly unfolded”.
   - Problem: the *expositio* entry expressly fixes *exponere* as “to expound” and *explicare* as “to explain”.
   - Suggested fix: “He goeth on in explaining his desire”; “He expoundeth the changes”; “Which is more clearly explained”.

4. **GLOSSARY breach — *ac per hoc*.**
   - Latin: v. 1 “ac per hoc intus retineri non potest”; v. 2 “amorem, ac per hoc sitim et desiderium”; v. 4 “ac per hoc longe transcendit omnia corpora”.
   - Current English: “and hence it cannot be kept within”; “love, and hence thirst and desire”; “and hence it far surpasseth all bodies”.
   - Problem: the glossary fixes *ac per hoc* as “and thereby”.
   - Suggested fix: replace these three occurrences of “and hence” with “and thereby”.
