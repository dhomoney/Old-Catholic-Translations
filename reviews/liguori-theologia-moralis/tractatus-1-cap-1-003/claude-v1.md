## Findings

1. **Nit, grammar (n. 3 and n. 4).**
   Latin: *quae cum debeat et possit vinci* (n. 3); *cum errorem debeat et vincere possit* (n. 4).
   English: "though it ought and can be overcome"; "since he ought and can overcome the error".
   Problem: "ought" needs "to": "ought and can be overcome" is not grammatical English.
   Fix: "though it ought to be and can be overcome"; "since he ought to overcome the error, and can".

2. **Nit, wording (n. 11, fourth sign).**
   Latin: *et mente haerere contra sapientum, ac etiam proprium iudicium*
   English: "and to hold fast in mind against the judgment of wise men, and even his own"
   Problem: "hold fast" suggests firm conviction. The sign is that the scrupulous man stays stuck in his fear and hesitates against the judgment of others and his own, which is why the passage goes on that he is never content with one assurance.
   Fix (optional): "and to remain stuck in mind against the judgment of wise men, and even his own".

3. **Nit, wording (n. 19, Gerson).**
   Latin: *Conscientia formata est, quando post discussionem et deliberationem ... iudicatur*
   English: "A conscience is formed, when after discussion and deliberation it is judged ..."
   Problem: Gerson defines the term *conscientia formata*, which Liguori has just used ("a formed and deliberate conscience"). As written, the English reads as a description of a process.
   Fix (optional): "A formed conscience is, when after discussion and deliberation it is judged ...", matching the next clause, "But a fear or scruple of conscience is, when ...".
