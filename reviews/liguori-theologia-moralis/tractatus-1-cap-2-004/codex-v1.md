English: “See the Homo apostolicus, tract 5, no. 51, where St. Alphonsus discusseth the same question.” The scan confirms this third-person reference to the canonised author; it is a later editor's cross-reference, not Liguori's own note. Likewise remove the (19) mark at the end of no. 35 and its visible “[Editor's long note omitted]” entry: the long editorial note is already correctly excluded, but its dangling reference need not remain in the author's text. Record the omissions in the headers or an HTML comment. No additional approval is needed under the existing rule; this is not the specifically authorised Marietti reading key.

2. **Undisclosed printed misprint — no. 36, printed p. 12 / PDF 110, right column.** Latin: *unicumque iudicium opinativum tunc ipsa haberet rem esse alienam*. English: “and he would then have the single opinative judgment that the thing is another's.” The scan really prints *ipsa*, confirmed at 600 dpi; the context concerns the masculine *possessor*, and the English correctly follows the intended *ipse*. Add a short Translator's note: “The print has ipsa; ipse (he) is understood.” Keep the printed form in the Latin and record it in its header. This answers Clark's point 3: it is not a Scribe slip.

3. **Precision of the equality condition — no. 35.** Latin: *In pari causa possessor potior haberi debet*. English: “In a like cause the possessor ought to be held the stronger.” *Pari* specifies equality, not merely similarity to another case. This is the equality of the competing claims, matching the immediately preceding *dubium ... aequale pro et contra* and the subsequent contrast of an equal and an unequal doubt. Suggested fix: “In an equal case the possessor ought to be held the stronger,” or “Where the claims are equal, the possessor ought to be held the stronger.”
