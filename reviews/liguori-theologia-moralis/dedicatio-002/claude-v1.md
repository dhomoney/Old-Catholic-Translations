## Findings

### 1. p. XIII, *Cumque plurimis perplures annos, relectis auctoribus* (minor; omission)

- English: "And when, through very many years,[^1] having read again the authors..."; note 1: "the print has *plurimis perplures annos*, which does not construe; it is rendered by the sense."
- Problem: the phrase does construe. *perplures* is a Latin word ("very many"), and *perplures annos* is the time ("for very many years"); *plurimis* goes with *relectis auctoribus* ("very many authors having been read again"), set forward for emphasis. The English drops *plurimis*, and the note calls sound Latin unconstruable.
- Fix: "And when, for very many years, having read again very many authors both of the indulgent and of the rigid opinion, I had found..."; drop note 1, or reword it: "*plurimis* is set apart from *relectis auctoribus*, with which it goes."

### 2. p. XIV, *mira suavitati* (minor; STYLE 8 note)

- Latin: mira suavitati disposuit (so the print, checked on the scan)
- English: "hath ordered with wonderful sweetness" (no note)
- Problem: the print has the dative *suavitati* where the sense needs the ablative *suavitate*; our practice is to note such a misprint.
- Fix: keep the English; add a note: "the print has *suavitati*, a misprint for *suavitate*."

### 3. p. XIV, *benignissime accommodasti* (minor)

- Latin: congregationis ss. Redemptoris, cui tu nuper apostolicam auctoritatem benignissime accommodasti
- English: "to which thou hast lately most graciously lent the apostolic authority"
- Problem: "lent" says the authority was given for a time and is to be returned. *accommodare* here is "to grant, bestow" (Benedict XIV approved the Congregation's Rule in 1749).
- Fix: "to which thou hast lately most graciously granted the apostolic authority" (or "given").

### 4. p. XIV, *divinae gloriae cooperantes* (minor)

- Latin: ut in nostro ministerio divinae gloriae cooperantes, uberrimum in vinea Domini fructum reportare valeamus
- English: "that, cooperating in our ministry with the divine glory, we may be able..."
- Problem: the dative is the end worked for, not a partner: they work together for God's glory. "cooperating with the divine glory" makes the glory a co-worker.
- Fix: "that, working together in our ministry for the divine glory, we may be able..."
