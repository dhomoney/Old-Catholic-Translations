## Findings

### 1. v.10, Ps. cxi, 1: loose quotation not noted (minor; STYLE note practice)

- Latin: « Beatus vir qui timet Dominum, in mandatis ejus cupit nimis. » ... « in mandatis ejus cupit nimis, » id est, cupit valde, et vult omnino ea servare.
- English: "...he shall delight exceedingly in his commandments." ... "shall delight exceedingly in his commandments" (*in mandatis ejus cupit nimis*), that is, he desireth greatly...
- Problem: the Vulgate reads *in mandatis ejus volet nimis*; Bellarmine has *cupit*, and his gloss builds on that word ("cupit valde"). Our practice for his loose Scripture quotations (Wis. v, 1; Ps. xxxvi, 30 in earlier works) is a translator's note naming the Vulgate reading. Without it, "shall delight ... that is, he desireth greatly" reads as a jump.
- Fix: keep the Douay-Rheims and the Latin beside it; add a note: "Bellarmine has *cupit nimis* ('desireth exceedingly'); the Vulgate reads *volet nimis*, which the Douay-Rheims renders 'shall delight exceedingly'." Update the GLOSSARY row to match.

### 2. v.6-7, *per Occidentem* (minor)

- Latin: solem exoriri ab Oriente, et per Occidentem toto cœli ambitu decurso, ad Orientem reverti.
- English: "the whole compass of the heaven being run through by the West"
- Problem: "by the West" can be read as the agent ("run through by the West"). *Per* here is the route.
- Fix: "and, having run the whole compass of the heaven by way of the West, returneth to the East".

### 3. v.4, *celerrime moti ab intelligentia* (minor)

- Latin: celerrime moti ab intelligentia per obedientiam
- English: "most swiftly moved by the intelligence through obedience"
- Problem: the comparison is to the heavens of the schools, which were moved by an Intelligence (the angelic mover of each sphere); the Apostles are moved swiftly by their superior through obedience. "by the intelligence" reads as their own mind.
- Fix: "most swiftly moved by an Intelligence, through obedience" (capital marks the technical term). A short note is optional.
