## Translator's points

1. v. 3, *quæ perfecisti* beside the Douay-Rheims "which thou hast made", with note 2: correct; the comment ("thy most perfect laws") turns on the Latin word.
2. v. 4, *Dominus in cœlo sedes ejus* kept in Latin with a literal gloss: correct; he is describing the phrase's form (STYLE 4).
3. vv. 5-6, *interrogant* / *interrogat* as "question" in the commentary, note 3: correct; the argument is about what "questioning" means when said of God.
4. v. 6, John xii, 25 with (*animam suam*, his soul): correct; his point is that loving one's soul wrongly is hating it.
5. v. 7, *gehenna* = "hell"; *pars* / *portio* kept in Latin with glosses: acceptable. *Pars hæreditatis* is glossed "the portion of the inheritance" after Ps. xv, 5, while *pars* alone is "part"; since Bellarmine's point is that *pars*, *portio* and inheritance are one thing, this costs no sense.
6. v. 8, *æquitatem* = "righteousness" (Douay-Rheims), *rectitudo* = "uprightness", *justitias* glossed as plural: correct.
7. *vocatio* = "calling" (state of life): correct; "vocation" would now suggest a religious vocation only.
8. Note 1 mapping: checked against drbo.org. Douay-Rheims 2-4 = his 1-3; its 5 = his 4 and 5; its 6-8 = his 6-8. Correct.
