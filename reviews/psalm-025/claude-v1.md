## Findings

1. **Minor: the note asserts a misprint the print does not have** (v.4-5, note 2)
   - Latin file: *quod significat occultis, vel puerliter ludentibus*
   - English: "*those that play childishly*:[^2]", where note 2 says "the print has *puerliter* ..., a misprint for *pueriliter*".
   - Problem: at 600 dpi (PDF 158, left column) the print reads *pueriliter*. The second i is damaged and thin, but it is there, and the word has the full letter width. The text layer also reads *pueriliter*. So *puerliter* is a transcription slip, not the print.
   - Fix: drop note 2 and renumber; the English text stands. Scribe: correct the Latin to *pueriliter* and remove it from the header's misprint list.

2. **Minor: margin number changed without a note** (v.12)
   - Latin: `[Margin: Vers. 11.]` against verse 12; this is the print (PDF 159, "Vers. 11.").
   - English: `[Margin: Verse 12.]`, with no note.
   - Problem: by STYLE, a misprint is translated by the sense and gets a note; verse numbering is a listed note topic. The Psalm 21 case was a blank number ("Vers. ."), which could only be filled in. Here a printed number is changed, so the reader should be told.
   - Fix: keep "Verse 12." and add a note: "the margin has *Vers. 11*, a misprint for 12."
