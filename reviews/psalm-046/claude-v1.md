## Nits (no change of meaning; take or leave)

1. v. 4 (point 5). "Hæc igitur est species Jacob, quam Deus, et propter quam elegit sibi hæreditatem".
   - Current English: "the beauty of Jacob which God chose, and for the sake of which he chose for himself an inheritance".
   - Your reading (one verb for both clauses) makes *quam* and *hæreditatem* both objects of *elegit*, and the English then says "chose" twice.
   - The more natural missing verb is *dilexit*, from the lemma he is explaining (*speciem Jacob quam dilexit*). It is also the verb of his earlier gloss (*quam pulchritudinem merito adamavit*). That gives: "the beauty of Jacob which God loved, and for the sake of which he chose for himself an inheritance". A short note could say that the verb is understood from the verse.
   - Your version is still defensible, since his gloss also says *elegit pulchritudinem*.
2. v. 7 (point 6). "nimirum psallite, quia rex noster est".
   - Translating it as printed is fine. But "to wit" makes the second "sing ye" look like an explanation of the first, when it is the second item in a series of three ("and a third time sing ye").
   - "surely, sing ye, because he is our king" keeps the series. If you suspect *iterum*, say so in a note.
3. Title. "simpliciter totum Psalmum de victoria et ascensione Christi posuerunt": "have simply set down the whole Psalm of the victory" is stiff. Suggested: "have simply taken the whole Psalm of the victory and ascension of Christ".
4. v. 5. "fortasse non sunt visi, neque auditi chori angelorum, ut in nativitate": "were not seen nor heard, as at the nativity" can be read as "as they were not at the nativity". Suggested: "were not seen nor heard, as they were at the nativity".
5. v. 7. "non leviter peccari": "no light offence committed". *peccare* is "to sin"; "no light sin committed" is closer.
