1. **Scribe omission, carried into English — title commentary, printed p. 307 / PDF 318.** The scan reads: *hic dicitur: « Ascendit Deus; » et rursum repetitur, « et Dominus in voce tubæ: »*. Current Latin goes straight from *hic dicitur* to *« et Dominus in voce tubæ »*, omitting *« Ascendit Deus; » et rursum repetitur*. English likewise says only “here it is said ‘and the Lord with the sound of trumpet’”. This removes the first half of the comparison and the express indication that the second clause repeats it. Suggested fix: restore the missing words and quotation marks in Latin; render “here it is said: ‘God is ascended;’ and again it is repeated, ‘and the Lord with the sound of trumpet.’” Retain Bellarmine’s surrounding argument. Source location: fourth line from the end of the title commentary; confirmed at 600 dpi.

2. **Scribe punctuation omission — verse 2 commentary, printed p. 308 / PDF 319, left column.** Scan: *« Quoniam, inquit, Dominus excelsus, » id est, cum plausu*. Current Latin: *« Quoniam, inquit, Dominus excelsus, id est, cum plausu*. Restore the closing guillemet after *excelsus,*; it is present in the print, confirmed at 600 dpi. Current English already closes the quotation correctly after “the Lord is high”; no English prose change or Translator’s note is needed. Name the final Latin commit in the English header.

3. **Note 5 reference label — STYLE 3.** Latin source refers here to *Psalm. LXVII*; the English note correctly identifies the parallel in Ephesians but labels it *Eph.* iv, 8. The prescribed abbreviation is *Ephes.* Suggested fix: change that label to *Ephes.* iv, 8. The note’s substantive distinction between *Ascendit / Ascendens* and the Psalm’s *Ascendisti ... cepisti* is sound.
