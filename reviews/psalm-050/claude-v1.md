## Findings

### 1. v. 17: "videtur repetenda negatio" and "littera hebraica"

- Latin: "Sed videtur repetenda negatio, cum dicitur, *et dabo,* ut sensus sit ... Vel forte Septuaginta Interpretes loco negationis legerunt particulam conditionalem; nam ea posita potest littera hebraica sic legi"
- English: "But the negation seemeth to be repeated when it is said, *et dabo* ... for, that being set, the Hebrew letter can be read thus"
- Problem: *repetenda* is a gerundive: the negation "must be supplied again" before *et dabo*. "Seemeth to be repeated" says the Hebrew does repeat it. That is false, and the reading he has just quoted shows it is false. The point is his own proposal for reading the text. "The Hebrew letter" also misleads. The question is which particle the Seventy read, so a reader takes "letter" to mean one letter of the alphabet. *Littera* here means the wording of the text.
- Fix: "But it seemeth that the negation must be understood again when it is said, *et dabo* ..." and "for, that being set, the Hebrew text can be read thus". In the same paragraph, optionally: "In the Hebrew it seemeth to be had a little otherwise" → "In the Hebrew it seemeth to read a little otherwise".

## Nits (no change of meaning; take or leave)

1. v. 5: "tu omnino vincas causam, et ego causa cadam": "thou mayst altogether win the cause, and I may lose the cause". "May" reads as permission. Suggested: "thou wouldst altogether win the cause, and I should lose it".
2. *velle* rendered "will": "they willed to explain" (v. 1), "I should will to deny" (v. 5), "the Interpreter willed to keep" (v. 15), "he willeth to offer" (v. 17). Each is a calque. Suggested: "wished" / "would".
3. v. 9: "Hoc testimonium in se aliquando Propheta expertus": "having sometimes experienced". *Aliquando* here means "at some time, once". Suggested: "having once experienced".
4. v. 11: *cupiditas* "covetousness". Here it is set against *charitas*, and it turns "chiefly to itself". That is Augustine's disordered love, not greed for goods. Suggested: "cupidity". A glossary call, so it is yours to make.
