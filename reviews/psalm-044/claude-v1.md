## Findings

### 1. Title commentary: *non semel* reversed (mistranslation)

- Latin: "Per *dilectum,* intelligunt omnes Christum, de quo non semel Pater cœlestis ait"
- English: "of whom not once the heavenly Father saith"
- Problem: *non semel* means "more than once" (he said it at the Jordan and on Thabor, the two places cited). "Not once ... saith" reads in English as "never saith".
- Fix: "of whom the heavenly Father saith more than once, *Matt.* iii and vii:[^1] ..."

### 2. v. 12, last sentence: wrong subject for *recedat* (mistranslation)

- Latin: "significat enim radicitus a mundo amorem abstrahendum, ut non solum a desiderio et cogitatione, sed etiam a memoria omnium recedat, ac si nunquam fuisset."
- English: "so that she depart not only from the desire and thought, but also from the memory of all things, as if it had never been."
- Problem: the subject that "departs ... as if it had never been" is the world (*mundus*), not the Spouse. The English has the Spouse leaving "the memory of all things", which is not what he says, and "it" then has nothing to refer to. *omnium* is "of all" (persons).
- Fix: "so that it depart not only from the desire and thought, but also from the memory of all, as if it had never been." (or "so that the world depart ...").

### 3. v. 9, St. Basil: the English supplies a subject the print does not have (mistranslation / unnoted reading)

- Latin: "dicit Christo fuisse naturalem dilectionem justitiæ ... : cæteras autem exercitio et labore virtutes acquirere."
- English: "but that the rest acquire the virtues by exercise and labour."
- Problem: the print has *cæteras* (agreeing with *virtutes*: "the other virtues"); I checked it at 300 dpi, p. 295 (PDF 306). The English translates it as if it were *cæteros* ("the rest", that is, other men) and gives no note. Either it is a misprint for *cæteros* (then STYLE asks for a note), or it is to be translated as printed.
- Fix, one of: (a) as printed: "but that the other virtues are acquired by exercise and labour"; or (b) keep "the rest acquire the virtues" and add a note: "the print has *cæteras* (the other [virtues]); the sense requires *cæteros* (the rest [of men])", and ask Mose to list it in the header. I lean to (a), since the printed words make sense.

### 4. v. 6: *docemus* is not a misprint

- Latin: "Ex quibus verbis docemus duo esse necessaria regi"
- English: "From which words we are taught[^4] that ..." with note 4 "the print has *docemus* (we teach) for *docemur* (we are taught)."
- Problem: *docemus* with the accusative and infinitive is regular Latin and makes good sense ("from which words we teach that two things are necessary to a king"). STYLE 1: translate what he wrote. There is no ground to call it a misprint.
- Fix: "From which words we teach that two things are necessary to a king"; drop note 4 and renumber. (Mose's header lists "docemus ... for docemur" as kept as printed; the "for docemur" can go too.)

### 5. v. 20: *in patria* against GLOSSARY

- Latin: "quamvis enim in patria cessabunt preces et lacrymæ"
- English: "for although in the country prayers and tears shall cease"
- Problem: GLOSSARY rows "in patria = in the heavenly country" (Suarez) and "patria = country, the heavenly country" (Ps 41). "In the country" alone reads as "in the countryside".
- Fix: "for although in the heavenly country prayers and tears shall cease".

### 6. STYLE 3: two quotations in other words without the Vulgate reading

- (a) v. 13: "Omnis pulchritudo filiæ Regis ab intus" → "All the beauty of the King's daughter is within". The Vulgate (v. 14 of this Psalm, his v. 15 lemma) has *Omnis gloria*. A changed word, not a shortening: add a note, e.g. "Bellarmine's text has *pulchritudo* (beauty); the Vulgate, v. 15 below, has *gloria* (glory)."
- (b) Note 11, Matt. v, 16: his words "ut qui ea vident, glorificent Patrem nostrum" differ from the Vulgate "ut videant opera vestra bona, et glorificent Patrem vestrum" (DR "That they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven"). The note gives only the reference; add the Vulgate reading.

## Nits (no change of meaning; take or leave)

- v. 9: "all the rest have received a measure of the spirit of thy fulness" can be read as "the spirit of thy fulness". Latin *de plenitudine tua mensuram spiritus acceperunt*: "have received of thy fulness a measure of the spirit".
- v. 5: "of God himself, which cannot be deceived": *qui falli non possunt* covers the angels and God; "who cannot be deceived".
- Note 6 (*hereticos*) is a spelling variant, not a misprint; the note does no harm but is not needed.
