# Psalm 44 — Codex review v1

**CHANGES**

English: `b6b5060`, `english/psalm-044.md`. Latin: `7255985`, `latin/psalm-044.md`.

1. **Title commentary, p. 291 — mistranslation.** Latin: *de quo non semel Pater cœlestis ait*. English: “of whom not once the heavenly Father saith”. In English “not once” denies that he said it; *non semel* means more than once. Suggested fix: “of whom the heavenly Father saith more than once”.

2. **Verse numbering — required concordance omitted (STYLE 3).** Latin has twenty numbered verses; English likewise has twenty, but none of the thirteen notes explains the difference from the eighteen verses of the Douay-Rheims. Suggested fix: add a note giving this concordance (Bellarmine → Douay-Rheims): 1–2 → 2; 3–5 → 3–5 respectively; 6 → latter half of 5; 7–9 → 6–8; 10 → 9 and the beginning of 10; 11 → remainder of 10; 12–14 → 11–13; 15 → 14 and beginning of 15; 16 → remainder of 15; 17–18 → 16–17; 19–20 → the two parts of 18. The title is Douay-Rheims verse 1. Checked against [Douay-Rheims Psalm 44](https://www.drbo.org/chapter/21044.htm). Keep the present verse divisions.

3. **Verse 12 commentary, p. 298 — wrong subject and sense.** Latin: *ut non solum a desiderio et cogitatione, sed etiam a memoria omnium recedat, ac si nunquam fuisset*. English: “so that she depart not only from the desire and thought, but also from the memory of all things, as if it had never been”. The thing forgotten is the world: it is to leave desire, thought and even everyone’s memory. The Spouse is not departing from those faculties; nor does *memoria omnium* mean the memory of all things here. Suggested fix: “so that the world pass not only from desire and thought, but even from the memory of all, as though it had never been”.

4. **Latin readings discussed as readings — repeated STYLE 4 breach.** Many are given only in English, obscuring the precise words under comparison. Examples: title *pro liliis / pro iis qui commutabuntur* becomes “for the lilies / for them that shall be changed”; v. 3 *propterea / quia / propterea quod* becomes “therefore / because / for the reason that”; v. 9 *Deus, Deus tuus* becomes “God, thy God” where the cases are discussed; v. 13 *Dominus Deus tuus / Dominus* and *adorabis eum / adorabunt eum* become “the Lord thy God / the Lord” and “thou shalt adore him / they shall adore him”; v. 14 *Et filiæ / Et filia Tyri* becomes “And the daughters / And the daughter of Tyre”; v. 18 *tuis et tibi* becomes “thy and to thee” where their gender matters; v. 19 *memor ero nominis tui / memores erunt nominis tui* becomes “I will remember thy name / they shall remember thy name”. Suggested fix: retain each Latin reading in italics, with the English gloss at first mention in its passage. Apply the same test to the other renderings explicitly attributed to an edition, language or interpreter (including Jerome’s rendering in v. 5). Explanatory repetitions may remain English; keep the present Douay-Rheims lemmas.

5. **Scripture reference forms — STYLE 3.** Latin *Sap.*, *Psalm.*, *Apocalyps.*, *Coloss.* is represented by *Wisd.*, *Psalm*, *Apocal.* and *Coloss.*; note 5 also leaves “Ps.” unitalicised. Suggested fix: use the prescribed *Wis.*, *Ps.*, *Apoc.* and *Col.* consistently (and italicise *Ps.* in the note). This concerns citation labels, not prose mentions of a Psalm or book.

6. **Verse 9 commentary, p. 295 — silent correction of a printed misprint.** Latin: *cæteras autem exercitio et labore virtutes acquirere*. English: “but that the rest acquire the virtues by exercise and labour”. That sense treats *cæteras* as *cæteros*, referring to other people rather than other virtues. The scan really prints *cæteras* (PDF 306, right column, line beginning immediately after *et a principio interno*; checked at 600 dpi). Suggested fix: keep the intended sense and add a Translator’s note that the print has *cæteras*, apparently for *cæteros*. The source header should record the kept form too.

7. **Scribe finding — source-header proof record incomplete/inaccurate.** Current Latin header lists selected retained misprints but omits the second *a gravibus eburneis* (printed p. 296 / PDF 307, left column, line immediately before *Per filias regum*), *crueret* (p. 298 / PDF 309, left column, after *ut nos de profundo lacu*), and *cæteras* above. All three are confirmed on images; the first two English notes are justified and these are not transcription slips. Also the header places *Ex quibus verbis docemus* on p. 295, whereas it is on p. 294 / PDF 305, right column near the top. Suggested fix: complete the kept-misprint list and correct that page reference; send Clark the final Latin commit for his header. No change to those Latin body readings is requested.
