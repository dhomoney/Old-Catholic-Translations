## Findings

1. **Nit, word order (v. 5 and v. 8 commentary, note 4).**
   Latin: *Adversum me cogitabant mala mihi*
   English: *They devised evils to me (mihi) against me* (v. 5); *they devised evils to me against me* (v. 8)
   Problem: putting the added "against me" at the end hides the point; in the Latin *adversum me* comes first and *mihi* is then plainly redundant beside it.
   Fix: "Against me they devised evils to me (*mihi*)" (v. 5), and "against me they devised evils to me" (v. 8). Note 4 stands as it is.

2. **Nit, wording (v. 3 commentary).**
   Latin: *ita illum consolari consuevisti, quomodo consolatur ægrotum, qui totum stratum ejus versat et reversat, ut mollius cubet et quiescat.*
   English: *thou hast been accustomed so to comfort him, as he comforteth a sick man who turneth and turneth again his whole couch, that he may lie more softly and rest.*
   Problem: "a sick man who turneth" reads as if the sick man turned the couch; the Latin means the one who comforts him turns it.
   Fix: "as he comforteth a sick man, who turneth and turneth again all his couch, that he may lie more softly and rest" or, plainer, "as one comforteth a sick man by turning and turning again all his couch, that he may lie more softly and rest."

3. **Nit, wording (v. 1 commentary, end).**
   Latin: *ac per hoc erit in illa summa penuria miserationis et gratiæ.*
   English: *and by this there will be in it the utmost dearth of mercy and of grace.*
   Fix: "and hence there will be in it the utmost dearth of mercy and of grace."
