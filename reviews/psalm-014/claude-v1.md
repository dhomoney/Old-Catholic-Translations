## Findings

1. **Verse 6, reference (minor, STYLE 5).**
   - Latin: *ut ait Apostolus ad Timoth. cap. VI*
   - English: "as the Apostle saith, I *Tim.* chap. vi,[^2]" with note 2: "Bellarmine does not say which Epistle to Timothy; the words are at I Tim. vi, 10."
   - Problem: the text supplies the "I" that note 2 says he leaves out. Under STYLE 5 an incomplete reference is kept as printed and completed in the note, so the text and the note now say different things.
   - Fix: "as the Apostle saith, *to Timothy*, chap. vi,[^2]" (or "*Tim.* chap. vi"), and keep note 2 as it is.

2. **Verse 2, commentary (minor, GLOSSARY consistency).**
   - Latin: *qui vivit sine culpa lethali. Veniale enim peccatum non proprie relinquit maculam.*
   - English: "who liveth without deadly fault. For venial sin doth not properly leave a blemish."
   - Problem: GLOSSARY.md at 6bc205c contradicts itself. The Ps 1 row fixes *veniale / lethale* = "venial / mortal", and the Ps 2 row (*lethalis laqueus* = "deadly") says '"mortal" is kept for sin'. The new Ps 14 row makes *culpa lethalis* "deadly fault", and the Liguori row makes *lethale peccatum* "deadly sin". Here *lethali* is set directly against *veniale*, which is the very pair the Ps 1 row fixes. The meaning is the same either way, but one of the rows should give way.
   - Fix: either "without mortal fault" here, dropping the Ps 14 row (and in Liguori, "mortal sin" for *lethale peccatum*); or amend the Ps 1 and Ps 2 rows to allow "deadly" for *lethalis*.
