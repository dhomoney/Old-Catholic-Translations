1. **Undisclosed printed misprint — verse 5 commentary, printed p. 346 / PDF 357, right column, line beginning “significare”.** Latin: *quibus significare videtur æterna desolatio in hoc sæculo, et æterna damnatio in futuro*. Current English: “by which words eternal desolation in this world, and eternal damnation in the world to come, seem to be signified”. The scan confirms active *significare*, not passive *significari*, at 600 dpi. The English correctly follows the intended passive, but STYLE 8 requires a note for that correction of the print. Suggested note: “The print has significare; significari (to be signified) is understood.” Retain the printed Latin, and record the form in its header. No alteration of the English sense is needed.

2. **Textual reading discussed without its Latin — STYLE 4, verse 1 commentary.** Latin: *in textu hebraico et græco constituitur punctum post vocabulum potens es: « Quid gloriaris in malitia, qui potens es. »* Current English preserves *potens es* but gives the resulting reading only as “Why dost thou glory in malice, thou that art mighty.” Since this sentence expressly shows the Hebrew and Greek punctuation, retain *Quid gloriaris in malitia, qui potens es.* beside its English gloss, as with *Quid gloriatur?* and *Quid gloriaris?* immediately above. Keep the point that ends the reading after *es*.
