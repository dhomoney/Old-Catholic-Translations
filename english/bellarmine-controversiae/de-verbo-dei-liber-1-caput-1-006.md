OF THE CONTROVERSIES

OF THE WORD OF GOD

THE FIRST BOOK

WHEREIN THE SACRED BOOKS IN GENERAL ARE TREATED OF.

CHAPTER I.

*Whether Scripture be the Word of God.*

[Left column]

As I set about to dispute of the sacred books, there first offereth itself that Question: Whether the Prophetical and Apostolical Scripture is to be received as the word of God: or whether that only is to be held for the word of God, which the Holy Ghost speaketh privately to each one in the heart. Which question, otherwise unworthy to be treated among Christian Theologians, hath been bred for us in this time partly by the dotage of Swenckfeld and the Libertines, partly by the wickedness and shamelessness of the Lutherans. Gaspar Swenckfeld (as Frederick Staphilus, in the book *Of the Concord of the disciples of Luther,* and Peter Palladius in the book *Of the heresies of this time,* testify) rejecteth the written word as the killing letter, and biddeth us be content with the inward spirit alone. Of the Libertines, who arose with Copin and Quintin for their authors, John Calvin writeth in this manner: "We have already said, saith he, that these men were wont at the beginning openly to laugh if any one alleged the Scriptures: nor did they dissemble that they held them for fables. Yet meanwhile they ceased not to use them, if there were any place which they could wrest to their own sense. Not that they gave credit to them, but only that they might trouble the unlearned, and so shake them, that at length they might the more easily draw them to themselves. If any place was objected to them, they answered: That we

(1) In the *Instruction against the Libertines,* Chap. IX.

[Right column]

are in no wise bound to the letter, but ought to follow the spirit that quickeneth. Nay more, that swine Quintin had marked every one of the Apostles with some scoff, as, in his Picard speech, calling Paul a broken vessel, John a foolish youth, Peter a denier of God, Matthew a usurer (1)." Thus he.

Moreover Martin Luther, and John Calvin, and as many as are their followers and disciples, are not afraid, by a most shameless lie, to ascribe this very error of Swenckfeld and the Libertines to the Roman Pontiff and the whole Catholic Church. The words of Luther in the book *Of Councils and the Church,* near the end, are these: "The Pope hath stuffed almost the whole world with a huge mass of commentaries and books concerning this very matter, and hath turned laws, exactions, rights divine and human, the articles of the Faith, the name of sin and of justice into mere snares of consciences: so that his Decretal hath deserved to be given again to the flames; for the Church could easily want such a book, which hath done horrible and irreparable damage in a great part of mankind: he hath buried the sacred Scripture in mire and dust, and hath blotted out almost the whole of Christian doctrine." Thus Luther; and the like of these words are read in many places in the book *against the King of England,* and in the book *of the false state of the Clergy,* and elsewhere. The words of Calvin at the beginning of the book or *Instruction against the Anabaptists* are these: "For we do not say, after the manner of the Papists, that the most holy Scriptures ought to be set aside, that we may rest in the authority of men; for this evasion we judge to be an execrable blasphemy." Wherefore, that we may briefly refute both the error of the former,
