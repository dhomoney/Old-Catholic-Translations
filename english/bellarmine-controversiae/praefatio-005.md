This the Church of Samosata once well understood, when the Arian plague was raging; for as it is in Theodoret (4), when there was in that city a Catholic people, and an heretical Bishop, the Bishop preached in the Church, but to the benches, and the walls; for all fled from him as from one sick of the plague. The Bishop went to the baths,[^1] and courteously invited others who chanced to be present, to bathe with him: but no one ever entered the place while he was present; no one even afterwards bathed, unless all the water had first been cast out, and new brought in again: boys were once playing in the market-place, and it happened that the ball they played with touched the foot of the beast on which the Bishop was riding; straightway they all cry out, and a great fire being kindled in the market-place itself, they cast the ball into it.

Such was the zeal of the ancient Christians, such their striving for piety, such their ardour for the Catholic faith; which if Germany, if Bohemia, if England, if France, if the other peoples had been willing to imitate, we should surely have another Commonwealth; if for the keeping off of heresy that diligence at least had been used in the beginning, which we see everywhere used for the shutting out of the plague: many members of the Church certainly, whose salvation is now almost despaired of, would still remain whole and uncorrupted. But since, our sins so requiring, the plague of heresy hath grown so vehemently, and groweth daily, and our affairs have been brought into so great and open a peril, that only in a long time, and by the many labours and sweats of many, can a remedy be brought to so great evils: this one thing remaineth, that the sons of the Church, and those especially,

who have been called by the Lord to help others, prepare themselves diligently to resist the heretics. Which will so be done, if[^2] they bestow sufficiently great care and pains in learning those things, which both they bring against the Church, and ours answer for the Church: to whose studies we vehemently desire that these our labours, such as they are, may also be of some profit, and we trust, the Lord helping, that they will profit. And these are the things which we thought should be said of the usefulness of our disputations.

Now I will set forth in few words, what and how many are those controversies, which we are about to explain. The enemy of mankind, although otherwise he is wont to be wholly perverse and a disturber of order, yet would not assail the truth of the Catholic Church without a certain order. And so in the first CC years from the rise of the Christian Church, he was wholly occupied in shaking the very first article of the Apostles' Creed. For what else did the Simonians, Menandrians, Basilidians, Valentinians, Marcionites, Manichees, and the whole school of the Gnostics mean, than that there is not one God, the Father almighty, creator of heaven and earth? But when that succeeded not, again in the time following, after the year of the Lord CC, the Devil arrayed a new Camp, and set about to assail the second article, in which the divinity of Christ our Lord is set forth. For thence came Praxeas, Noetus and Sabellius, and also Paul of Samosata.

But after the year CCC, there arose Photinus, Arius and Eunomius: who either did not distinguish the divine person of Christ from the person of the Father, and so, as Hilary speaketh, destroyed it; or made his divine nature diverse and alien from the nature of the Father, and in that way argued that it was not truly divine, but created. But when not even then the gates of hell had been able to prevail against the Church, a third time the Devil, a new levy being held, began to assail with greater effort and strength the third article, and together with it the fourth, fifth, sixth, and seventh, because they have a certain connexion and as it were kinship among themselves.

And so he stirred up Nestorius and Theodore of Mopsuestia, after the year CCCC. Then a little after, Eutyches, and Dioscorus: and about the five hundredth year, Peter Gnapheus, Severus of Antioch, Julius of Halicarnassus:[^3] and after the year DC and DCC, James the Syrian, Cyrus of Alexandria, Sergius, Pyrrhus, and Paul, of Constantinople: lastly all that innumerable posterity of Eutyches, variously cut and divided into the most minute sects, whose shoots are found in the East even to this day.

For all these, by arts and engines diverse indeed, and contrary one to another; yet strove to destroy and overthrow one and the same mystery of the divine Incarnation,

<!-- p. 60 (PDF 83) -->

and also of the passion, the resurrection, and the coming to judgment, that is, the five following parts of the Apostles' Creed. For the former, setting up two persons in Christ, one divine, the other human, and separating Christ God from the man Christ, would have him to be man only, and not also God, who is preached by the Catholic Church to have been conceived of the Holy Ghost, born of the Virgin Mary, to have suffered, died, and been buried, and then, alive again, to have ascended into heaven, and to be about to return again to judge the world. But the latter, mingling the natures themselves, divine and human, in Christ, and forging one out of two, taught that the mysteries of our salvation, the incarnation, passion, resurrection, ascension, coming to judgment, were feigned and counterfeit.

But after the Catholic Church had triumphed over these forces also in her wonted manner, the Devil was not therefore deterred, but again repaired his army, and set about to shake with great violence the following article, that is, the eighth, which is of the Holy Ghost.[^4] For about the year of the Lord DCCCLX, in the times of Nicholas the First, Roman Pontiff, there arose (as I indeed judge,[^5] although others think otherwise) the schism of the Greeks from the Apostolic See, and the Churches of the West: the foundation of which schism is that error of the Greeks, refuted in so many Councils, by which they most obstinately contend that the Holy Ghost proceedeth not from the Son. For although long before Macedonius denied that the Holy Ghost is God, and Theodoret that the same Holy Ghost proceedeth from the Son: yet at that time Satan was so occupied in assailing the very true Divinity and humanity of Christ, that he tried but lightly[^6] the article of faith which pertaineth to the Holy Ghost, and chiefly that part which is of the procession from the Father and the Son.

But indeed, when our most crafty enemy perceived that he profited little in pulling down those articles which pertained to the divine persons themselves, he turned himself wholly to the shaking and destroying of those which pertain to the Church, and the Sacraments. These two articles: I believe the holy Catholic Church, the communion of Saints, the Remission of sins, from the thousandth year to this day, with armies often changed, increased, renewed, by the Berengarians, Petrobrusians, Waldenses, Albigenses, Wiclefists, Hussites, Lutherans, Zwinglians, Confessionists, and Anabaptists, with every art and industry, and with all the forces of all hell, he hath endeavoured to overthrow, and endeavoureth to this day.

Now this is wont to seem to me wonderful and worthy of remark, that Berengarius, as the author and parent of the heretics of this time, by three

<!-- p. 61 (PDF 84) -->

errors of his laid the foundations of the three chief sects, to which at this time all the rest can be reduced; namely of the Sacramentarians,[^7] the Confessionists, the Anabaptists. For the first error of Berengarius was, that there is not in the Eucharist the true body of Christ, but that it is only signified by it. This error the Sacramentarians received as their own and proper. Another error of the same, which he devised after his second recantation, was, that there is indeed in the Eucharist the true body of Christ, but together with the substance of bread; and this the Augsburg Confession made its own, and in it all the Confessionists persevere. The third error of the same Berengarius was, that infants are not to be baptized, nor Marriages to be honoured, since all women are common to all men, as Guitmund relateth in Book I against Berengarius; and this is the very faith and doctrine of the Anabaptists.

Since therefore the heresies of this time properly turn almost all about these two articles of the Apostles' Creed, the ninth, and tenth, we also shall refer all the controversies to the same two articles. In the beginning therefore we shall discourse of the Holy Church: in which disputation we must treat first of Christ himself, who is the head, and Prince of the universal Church: then of the part of the Church which laboureth on earth, and also of its visible head the Supreme Pontiff, and of all its members, that is, Clerics, Monks, Laymen: then of the other part of the Church, which is cleansed in the places of Purgatory: lastly of that which now, blessed with Christ, triumpheth in Heaven, where there will be disputation of the worship, and invocation of the Saints, of relics, and images, and other like things. Which being dispatched, we shall come to the communion of Saints, that is, to the treatment of the Sacraments, where there will be as many heads of controversies, as[^8] there are Sacraments.

For almost whatever the Theologians affirm of the Sacraments, all that the heretics of this time deny. There followeth lastly, the remission of sins, to which will pertain all those questions, which are of the grace of man,[^9] of the loss of grace, of the wounds left from sin, of the repairing of grace, of free will, of justification, of the merits of good works.

Moreover, before all these questions there must be set, as a certain great proem, the controversy of the word of God. For there can be no disputing, unless we first agree with the adversaries in some common principle: now it is agreed between us, and all heretics whatsoever, that the word of God is the rule of faith; by which judgment is to be made of dogmas: that it is the common principle granted by all, whence arguments are drawn: lastly that it is the spiritual sword, which in this contest cannot be refused. Yet of

<!-- p. 62 (PDF 85) -->

this very common principle there are many questions. For some receive only the inward word of God; others the outward, but written; others partly written, partly delivered by voice. Again some hold these books, others those, for the word of God; some judge that this edition, others that that is to be used: some contend that Scripture is most clear of itself, others that it is obscure, and needeth an interpreter: lastly some teach that the sense of the Scriptures is to be sought from the inward Spirit[^10] revealing, others from the Church, and her Supreme Pastor; all which questions ought to be set forth in the beginning, that we may have something, whence we may determine of the rest; but all these things the index of the whole Work will more fully explain.
