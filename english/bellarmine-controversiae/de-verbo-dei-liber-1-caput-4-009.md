CHAPTER IV.

*Which are the sacred books.*

That the Prophetic and Apostolic Scripture is sacred and divine hath been sufficiently demonstrated, as I think, in the disputation above. It followeth that we next explain which are the truly Prophetic and Apostolic books. Now this whole disputation of the number of the sacred books is divided into three parts. For first we shall have to treat of those books of whose authority there was never any controversy among Catholic men. Then of those whose authority, although they are truly Prophetic or Apostolic, was yet not always equally certain and assured. Lastly of those which, although they were sometimes held by some most famous and most learned men in the number of the divine volumes, were yet never approved by the public judgment of the whole Church.

Of the books of the first order these are the names: the Pentateuch of Moses, the book of Josue, of Judges,

[Right column]

Ruth, four of Kings,[^1] two of Paralipomenon, two of Esdras and Nehemias, Job, the Psalter of CL Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Canticles, the four greater Prophets, the twelve lesser Prophets, the four books of the Gospels, the Acts of the Apostles, the Epistles of Paul XIII in number, to wit, the Epistle to the Hebrews alone being left out, two Canonical Epistles, one of Peter, the other of John.

The second order containeth these books: Esther, Baruch, part of Daniel, Tobias, Judith, Wisdom, Ecclesiasticus, the first and second of Machabees, certain parts of Mark, Luke, and John, the Epistle to the Hebrews, the Epistle of James, the latter of Peter, a small portion of the first of John, the second and third of John, the Epistle of Jude, the Apocalypse.

The third order comprehendeth all those which are recounted by Innocent, Epistle III to Exuperius; Gelasius, dist. XV, can. *Sancta Romana*; Athanasius in the *Synopsis*; Eusebius, *History,* Book III, chap. 25; of which these few seem to be extant: the prayer of Manasses, the third and fourth books of Esdras, likewise the third and fourth of Machabees,[^2] Psalm CLI, the Appendix of the book of Job, the book of Hermes[^3] which is entitled the *Shepherd*.

And that the books of the first order indeed have always had divine authority in the Catholic Church, all the supreme Pontiffs, all the Councils, all the Fathers are witnesses, who for whatsoever cause have endeavoured to number the books. See, of the Pontiffs, Innocent I in Epistle III to Exuperius, last chapter; Gelasius I in the decree on the sacred books, which is extant in Tome II of the Councils. Of the Councils, that of Laodicea, can. LIX; the third of Carthage, can. 47; that of Florence in the *Instruction of the Armenians,* according to the *Summa* of the Councils; that of Trent, sess. IV.

Of the Greek Fathers, Origen in Eusebius, *History,* Book VI, chap. 18. Eusebius himself, *History,* Book III, chap. 25. Athanasius in the *Synopsis*; Gregory Nazianzen in the book *Of the Genuine Scriptures,* written in verse; Epiphanius in the book *Of Measures and Weights*; likewise the same against the heresy of the Anomœans; Cyril of Jerusalem, Catechesis IV; John Damascene, *Of the Faith,* Book IV, chap. 18.

Of the Latin Fathers, Jerome, both in the *Helmeted Prologue* and in the Epistle *to Paulinus on the study of the holy Scriptures*; Rufinus

in the *Explanation of the Creed*; Augustine, *Of Christian Doctrine,* Book II, chapter 8; Isidore, *Etymologies,* Book VI, chap. 1. To these add three other writers, who recount the books of the old Testament only: Josephus, *Against Apion,* Book I; Melito of Sardis in Eusebius, *History,* Book IV, chap. 26; and Hilary in the preface on the Psalms.

Moreover the other Fathers, Justin, Irenæus, Basil, Tertullian, Cyprian, Ambrose, and all the others, both Greek and Latin, although they do not describe the series of the divine books, yet in many places so use their testimonies that they not obscurely testify that all of them were held by them among the sacred and divine books.

[^1]: Translator's note: the print has *Regnum* for *Regum.*

[^2]: Translator's note: the print points the words thus: *oratio Manassæ III et IV, lib. Esdræ III, item et IV Machabæorum.* They are translated by the sense, the numbers III and IV being taken with Esdras and with Machabees.

[^3]: Translator's note: Bellarmine has *Hermetis*; the author of the *Shepherd* is now commonly called Hermas.
