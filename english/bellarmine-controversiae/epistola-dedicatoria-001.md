TO THE MOST BLESSED AND MOST HOLY FATHER

SIXTUS THE FIFTH

SUPREME PONTIFF

ROBERT BELLARMINE

Most truly and wisely, Most Blessed Father, did St. Jerome leave it written (1), that there is no man so impious, whom the heretic doth not surpass in impiety. For since faith is the foundation and origin of the whole spiritual building, and of all heavenly benefits: they who strive to take away faith from the Church, which the heretics do, strive at the same time to corrupt all the goods of the Church, and to overthrow the Church herself from the foundation. Wherefore St. John, and many ancient Fathers following him (2), that they might express in one word the innumerable calamities, and the horrible desolation, which heretics bring both upon the Church and upon the Commonwealth, judged that they should be called by the name of Antichrist. For among all the persecutions of the Church none shall be more cruel, none more grievous than that which Antichrist shall raise at the very ending of the world. Therefore our forefathers, although in all persecutions they bore themselves bravely against the gates of hell, yet when the cause of the faith was at stake, were all so moved in spirit, that they did not satisfy themselves unless they gathered together the whole forces of the Church, and, as if called forth by a common trumpet, all ran together as one to resist. To drive other enemies from the borders of the Church, no man forsook the keeping of the flock entrusted to him: to repel heresy, leaving their flocks, altars and sees, they did not hesitate to go to regions far distant, not without great cost and danger. Nor indeed did the Bishops only, gathered in Councils, as in an army set in array, fight it out against heresies; but both before the other Bishops the supreme Pontiffs themselves, from the most lofty watch-tower and citadel of religion, were the first to give the signal, the first to hurl the thunderbolts of anathemas against the adversaries; and after the Bishops, the Emperors by edicts, by laws,

by fines, piously used severity against the heretics; while together the Doctors of all orders and nations, both by writings and by discourses, ran up as to the quenching of a common fire.

But if, in those ancient times, some one error so greatly moved the whole Christian world, that sometimes, at the summons of the supreme Pontiff, six hundred Bishops and more from the most remote provinces came together in a moment of time into one camp, so to speak: of what mind, pray, ought we to be, who are assailed and harassed not by some one error, but by the very sink of heresies; and who contend with the enemies of the Church[^1] not about one or another article of faith, but about almost the whole sum of religion? And thou indeed, Sixtus V, most blessed Pontiff, hast in this spiritual battle so proved thy labour and industry to the Catholic Church, that for this cause most of all thou seemest to have been reserved by divine providence for these most perilous times, and chosen to be the supreme leader of the spiritual warfare. In thee indeed there shone forth the splendour of many virtues, which showed thee most worthy of the supreme priesthood; integrity of manners, from thy singular disposition, and from the rule of religious life, which in the most illustrious family of Blessed Francis thou didst take up as a boy, didst piously cultivate as a youth, didst adorn as a distinguished Doctor, and as Minister General didst endeavour to recall to its former honour and brightness: singular Prudence, which bearing the torch before thee,[^2] in the office of Inquisitor, in the supreme magistracy of a religious order, in the Episcopal function, in the most august and sacred College of Cardinals, thou hast run with a foot that never stumbled: Loftiness of spirit, strength of mind, a constancy worthy of the supreme Prince, which nothing hath ever turned aside from the right, nothing from the severity of the laws; which, although now, joined with the supreme power, it is seen most of all in repressing the boldness of the wicked, yet the Pontificate did not beget it, but showed it.

These and many other things of this kind were making for thee, in the opinion of all, a step to the supreme dignity: but among so many most illustrious virtues that one, beyond the rest, God providing for his Church, raised thee to that summit, which at this time is chiefly necessary to the supreme Priest, namely an ardour and zeal for turning away the heretical plague from the Church, which indeed long since the spirit of the Lord, and the knowledge of the sacred letters, stirred up in thee; which afterwards the office of Inquisitor bravely discharged sharpened; which at last the examples of Pius V, a Pontiff truly pious, enkindled, who, because thou wast most hostile to the sacrilegious sects of impious men, at once enrolled thee among the Most Illustrious Fathers and Princes of the Roman Church, and prepared for the universal Church a most excellent Leader for overthrowing the heresies of these times; and the judgment of that most wise and most holy man the beginnings of thy Pontificate forthwith confirmed. For at the very outset of thy Apostolate thou didst set such a course of thy actions,[^3] that all may easily understand what, for all the time that remaineth, is to be hoped by Catholics from that greatness of thy spirit, and what is to be dreaded by heretics.

But I, to come now to myself, a son, such as I am, of the Catholic Church, have brought to my pious mother, so grievously labouring, what help I could, especially as the command of my Superiors was added. And when I had now spent fifteen years in expounding the controversies of the faith, partly at Louvain, in the most flourishing University of all Germany, partly at Rome, for instructing those young men whom the Apostolic kindness had called forth from the regions beyond the Alps: that I should at length commit to the press, and bring forth into the light, those things which I had discoursed in teaching, the authority of the same

Superiors of mine compelled me. Further, in whose name above all these disputations ought to appear, I had no great need to consider; for whether I turned my mind and thought to the books which are contained in this volume, or to the reasons for dedicating a labour of this kind, thy Holiness alone presented itself, as one who seemed with the best right to be set before all others; so that there was not one to be sought who might be chosen, but one to be chosen who stood pre-eminent. The books which were to be offered, since they explain the Controversies of the faith from the principles of true Theology, were surely due to none more justly than to thee, who art both the supreme Theologian, and the one judge of all Controversies, appointed by God himself with singular providence.

But if, in the dedication of works, regard is wont to be had chiefly to him who is able, and willing, either to defend by his patronage the gift offered to him, or to adorn it by the splendour of his name, regard was surely to be had by me to none rather than to thee, who art both of that power by which thou comest nearest to God, and of that kindness in which (as the common father of all) thou yieldest to no man; and from whom this our work seemed by its own right to demand patronage and protection, because in this age, among so many heresies by which the Catholic faith is assailed, none is more far and wide diffused and propagated, none more grievous, none more hostile than that which striveth to shake that supreme Pontificate; so that we Catholics are now called by the heretics by no other name than Papists; as if the only contention between us and them were about the Papacy: this was the cause why in our work no disputation was to be treated more diligently and more copiously, none was to be greater, none better furnished than that in which the institution and authority of the supreme Pontificate is asserted and defended. Wherefore not undeservedly do those books in their turn seem to demand the patronage of the Apostolic See, which are chiefly spent in maintaining the authority of the same See, and in vindicating it from the bites and calumnies of the heretics. Lastly, if the dedications of books are wont to be testimonies of a mind grateful, and mindful of benefits received from some great Prince, a pupil of the Society of Jesus could by no means prefer any man to thy Beatitude in this kind of duty; for whatsoever cometh forth from us, that the most ample beneficence of the Apostolic See toward the whole Society of my order claimeth for itself by its own right, which thou, best of Fathers, Sixtus, makest daily more abundant. For when thy Pontificate was scarcely begun, our Society was not only received by thee with most gracious words, and bidden to be of good hope, but a little after, when first the occasion offered itself, obtained such benefits, that it both rejoiceth in the thing received for the present, and can also conceive the hope of greater things for the future.

Receive therefore, Most Blessed Father, a little gift due to thee on many titles, and continue to deserve well, as thou hast done hitherto, of our Society, small indeed, but most devoted to thee, and of the whole Christian Commonwealth; ours, namely, shall be the part to pray God without ceasing, that he may preserve thy Holiness safe for the whole Christian world, and may command that thy Pontificate be very long, and that the course of thy counsels and affairs, answering to thy name, be happy.
