BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM LII.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*Unto the end, for Macheleth, understanding to David.*[^1]

This title hath one word not interpreted, namely אל מחלת, *pro macheleth* (for Macheleth). And St. Jerome rendereth *per chorum* (by the choir); but by the choir he seemeth to have understood a musical instrument, as he himself testifieth in the epistle *to Dardanus* (if indeed that epistle is his) *on musical instruments,* as some take the word choir in the last Psalm in those words: "With timbrel and choir, with strings and organs." Nor is it improbable that by the choir may be understood a company of Singers singing, that the sense may be, that the Psalm was composed for a choir, namely that it may be sung by many together. Yet I confess that the former exposition seemeth the truer, because the Seventy Elders would not have left that word not interpreted, unless they had judged that by that word was signified the proper name of some musical instrument. That which is added, *intelligentia David* (understanding to David), signifieth that in this Psalm *the understanding of David is opened,* or *the instruction* (*eruditionem*) *of David,* as St. Jerome rendereth: from which title is gathered the argument of the Psalm, which is a lamentation of the folly of mankind; for mention is made of the understanding of David for this reason, that through him the blindness and folly of mankind was to be reproved.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. THE FOOL SAID IN HIS HEART: THERE IS NO GOD.**[^2]

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. THEY ARE CORRUPTED, AND BECOME ABOMINABLE IN THEIR INIQUITIES: THERE IS NONE THAT DOTH GOOD.**[^3]

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. GOD LOOKED DOWN FROM HEAVEN, TO SEE IF THERE WERE ANY THAT DID UNDERSTAND, OR DID SEEK GOD.**[^4]

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. ALL HAVE GONE ASIDE, THEY ARE BECOME UNPROFITABLE TOGETHER, THERE IS NONE THAT DOTH GOOD, NO NOT ONE.**

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. SHALL NOT ALL THE WORKERS OF INIQUITY KNOW, WHO EAT UP MY PEOPLE AS THEY EAT BREAD?**

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. THEY HAVE NOT CALLED UPON GOD: THERE HAVE THEY TREMBLED FOR FEAR, WHERE THERE WAS NO FEAR.**

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. FOR GOD HATH SCATTERED THE BONES OF THEM THAT PLEASE MEN: THEY HAVE BEEN CONFOUNDED, BECAUSE GOD HATH DESPISED THEM.**

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. WHO WILL GIVE OUT OF SION THE SALVATION OF ISRAEL? WHEN GOD SHALL BRING BACK THE CAPTIVITY OF HIS PEOPLE, JACOB SHALL REJOICE, AND ISRAEL SHALL BE GLAD.**

This Psalm is almost the same with the thirteenth Psalm; thence therefore let the explanation be sought: for it will be enough here to note the difference of the two Psalms.

The first verse is altogether the same.

The second verse also is the same, except that in this there is *in iniquitatibus* (in iniquities); in that, *in studiis* (in pursuits): but the sense is the same; for those pursuits are evil affections and desires, which are all iniquitous, and sins, and thereby iniquities.

The third verse is the same, except that here it is said *Deus* (God), there it is said *Dominus* (the Lord).

The fourth verse is altogether the same.

The fifth verse, the sixth and the seventh, which are had in the thirteenth Psalm, are not had in this; but, as we admonished there, those three verses were added from divers places of Scripture, and pertain not properly to the Psalm.

The fifth verse, which answereth to the eighth in the thirteenth Psalm, is the same with it, except that here it is said: "*Nonne scient?*" there it is said: "*Nonne cognoscent?*"[^5]

The sixth verse, which answereth to the ninth, is the same with it, except that here it is said: *Deum* (God), there *Dominum* (the Lord).

The seventh verse, which answereth to the tenth, is plainly different. For there it is said: "For the Lord is in the just generation: you have confounded the counsel of the poor man, but the Lord is his hope;" but here it is said: "For God hath scattered the bones of them that please men: they have been confounded, because God hath despised them." This verse therefore, which alone is to be explained, giveth the reason why the impious tremble for fear, where there is no fear; and saith that the cause is, "because God scattereth all their bones," that is, weakeneth all their strength, so that they dread even the very lightest things: and this he doth by his wonderful providence, making foolish their counsels, hindering their endeavours, and bringing to nothing all their devices: for bones in the Scriptures are taken in many places for strength. The Prophet addeth, "of them that please men," because men of that kind are full of human, or worldly, fear, and their whole study is to please men. But the Apostle on the contrary saith, *Gal.* chap. i: "If I pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ." In the Hebrew there is not: *Qui hominibus placent* (Of them that please men), but: *Circumdantium te* (Of them that encompass thee); but the Seventy Interpreters did not read חנך *chonach,* which is, *circumdantium te,* but חנף *chonaph,* which signifieth *hypocritam* (a hypocrite), *simulatorem* (a dissembler), *adulatorem* (a flatterer), which word they most rightly interpreted ἀνθρωπαρέσκων, that is, of them that

<!-- p. 350 (PDF 361) -->

please men; for such are flatterers and hypocrites. The Prophet adjoineth: "They have been confounded, because God hath despised them," by which words he seemeth to allude to the tenth verse of the thirteenth Psalm: "You have confounded the counsel of the poor man, but the Lord is his hope." As if he said: Ye impious have confounded the counsel of the poor man trusting in God, and God will confound you, so that ye are often compelled to blush, seeing your counsels made void, because ye have not hoped in God, and God hath despised you, and thereby hath not helped you. These things might also be referred to the last judgment, in which all the impious shall be confounded, because God the judge of all will despise them, saying: "I know you not, depart into everlasting fire,"[^6] *Matt.* xxv.

[Margin: How doth God turn away the captivity?]

The eighth verse, which is the last, is the same with the last verse of Psalm XIII, except that there it is said: "When God shall have turned away the captivity."[^7] But although *avertere* (to turn away) and *convertere* (to bring back) seem to be contraries, yet in this place they make the same sense: for God is said to turn away the captivity, when he taketh away out of the midst and bringeth to nothing the captivity itself; which is done when, the captives being freed, the captivity ceaseth to be. And the same God is said to bring back the captivity, when he recalleth the captives and leadeth them back into their country. Lastly, when the captivity is said to be turned away, captivity is taken for the form, that is, for the very true captivity itself: when the captivity is said to be brought back, captivity is taken for the persons who were captive.

[^1]: Translator's note: Bellarmine's title has *pro Macheleth, intelligentia David*; the Vulgate has *pro Maeleth, intelligentiæ David,* and the Douay-Rheims Bible "for Maeleth, understandings to David."

[^2]: Translator's note: Bellarmine does not number the title, and divides the Psalm into eight verses; the Douay-Rheims Bible has seven, and its verse 1 holds the title and his verse 1. His 2 to 5 are its 2 to 5; his 6 and 7 are its 6; his 8 is its 7.

[^3]: Translator's note: Bellarmine's lemma has *in iniquitatibus suis* (in their iniquities); the Vulgate has *in iniquitatibus* (in iniquities), as he himself quotes it below.

[^4]: Translator's note: Bellarmine's lemma lacks the words *super filios hominum* (on the children of men), which the Vulgate has after *prospexit.*

[^5]: Translator's note: the Douay-Rheims Bible has "Shall not ... know" in both Psalms; the Latin verbs differ, *scient* here and *cognoscent* in *Ps.* xiii.

[^6]: Translator's note: the words join two places of *Matt.* xxv: verse 12, *nescio vos* (I know you not), and verse 41, *Discedite a me, maledicti, in ignem æternum* (Depart from me, you cursed, into everlasting fire).

[^7]: Translator's note: Bellarmine's text has *Cum averterit Deus captivitatem*; *Ps.* xiii, 7 has *cum averterit Dominus captivitatem* (when the Lord shall have turned away the captivity).
