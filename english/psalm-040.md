BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM XL.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*Unto the end, a psalm for David himself.*

This Psalm also, like the foregoing, seemeth to belong wholly to the passion of Christ. For so the Fathers take it, Ambrose, Jerome, Augustine and Chrysostom, who addeth that it is rash to expound it otherwise, since one verse of the Psalm is cited by Christ himself, *John* xiii, to show that the betrayal of Judas had been foretold long before. Of the title we say nothing, because it containeth nothing.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. BLESSED IS HE THAT UNDERSTANDETH CONCERNING THE NEEDY AND THE POOR: THE LORD WILL DELIVER HIM IN THE EVIL DAY.**[^1]

[Margin: Christ poor not of necessity, but of will.]

"Blessed is he that understandeth concerning the needy and the poor," that is, blessed is he who attentively considereth Christ poor: for he will find him to be poor, not of necessity, but of will; and poor of will, that he might enrich us by his want. Likewise he will find him poor without, but rich within: for in him "are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge of God,"[^2] as Paul writeth, *Col.* ii. Lastly he will find him poor in the flesh, as St. Ambrose saith, but rich in the kingdom: for the same was poor, when he had not where to lay his head; and at the same time was heir of all things, King of kings and Lord of lords. Moreover, "blessed is he that understandeth" concerning Christ poor, namely hanging naked on the cross, that is, who attentively thinketh upon his passion; for Jeremias also had said before: "Attend, and see if there be any sorrow like to my sorrow," *Lam.* i; and the Apostle afterwards repeated the same: "Think diligently upon him that endured such opposition from sinners against himself," *Heb.* xii. For an infinite treasure is prepared for them that understand the passion of Christ, as is meet, and diligently meditate upon it. Lastly he also is blessed, who understandeth concerning the needy and the poor Christ in his members, of whom he is to say, *Matt.* xxv: "What you did to one of my least brethren, you did to me."[^3] But the Psalmist saith not: *Blessed is he that giveth alms to the poor man,* but he that understandeth concerning the needy and the poor, that is, who prudently considereth the necessities of the poor, and giveth to whom, when, and as much as is fitting, out of pure charity and liberality, not out of vainglory, or hope of recompense. For the Hebrew word משכיל *maschil* hath this force: for it signifieth to think attentively, or to consider prudently. There followeth: "The Lord will deliver him in the evil day." This is the reason why he is to be called blessed who understandeth concerning the needy, namely because he himself shall be delivered from need: for the evil day is the day of dearth and barrenness. But in this place the evil day is chiefly said of the day of the last judgment, which will be the day of justice alone, and thereby there will be in it the utmost dearth of mercy and of grace.

[Margin: Which is the evil day?]

In that day therefore they will be secure, who shall have loved the cross of Christ, and who for Christ's sake shall have been kind to the poor; for it shall be said to them: "Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess you the kingdom prepared for you. For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat," etc. But even in this world God will deliver merciful men in the evil day, as the thirty-sixth Psalm taught us before, saying: "They shall not be confounded in the evil time; and in the days of famine they shall be filled." For God is wonderfully delighted, when he seeth his children, in imitation of their Father, liberally sharing with others also the things which they liberally receive from him: and therefore he repayeth with usury that which is given to the poor, according to that of *Prov.* xix: "He that hath mercy on the poor, lendeth to the Lord."

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. THE LORD PRESERVE HIM AND GIVE HIM LIFE, AND MAKE HIM BLESSED UPON THE EARTH: AND DELIVER HIM NOT UP TO THE WILL OF HIS ENEMIES.**

He declareth what he said: "The Lord will deliver him in the evil day;" and by way of a good imprecation he foretelleth the good things which shall be given to him that understandeth concerning the needy and the poor. "The Lord, saith he, preserve him," that is, keep him while he liveth; "and give him life," that is, after death make him to live again, by raising him up with the just. "And make him blessed upon the earth," that is, and then render him truly and perfectly blessed in the land of the living. "And deliver him not up to the will (*in animam*) of his enemies," that is, and neither in this world nor in that to come suffer him to be subjected to the will of his enemies, whether they be men or devils. For he calleth the will, or desire, the soul (*animam*). The Greek copies vary in this place; for some have, *in manus inimicorum* (into the hands of the enemies); others, *in animam inimicorum* (unto the soul of the enemies), as St. Ambrose hath noted; but the sense is almost the same, save that it seemeth to be something more to be delivered up *in animam*, that is, to the will, or the pleasure, or the lust, than to be delivered up *in manus*, that is, into the power; for they who are to be punished are delivered into the hands of the execu-

[Margin: To be delivered up *in animam* (to the will) is more than to be delivered up *in manus* (into the hands).]

tioners, but not unto their will: because the executioners can put them to death, yet not in the manner they would, but only in that manner which the laws prescribe.

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. THE LORD HELP HIM ON HIS BED OF SORROW: THOU HAST TURNED ALL HIS COUCH IN HIS SICKNESS.**

Because he had promised so many good things to the merciful, who understand concerning the needy and the poor, some one might suspect that godly men of this kind would suffer no tribulation on earth: therefore he now addeth that godly men will indeed suffer in this exile divers tribulations, or temptations, but not without divine consolation. "The Lord, saith he, help him on his bed of sorrow," that is, if at any time that godly man shall fall upon a bed of sorrow, whether the sorrow be bodily or spiritual, whether he be properly sick in bed, or metaphorically lie as it were in bed, afflicted with any tribulation whatsoever, "the Lord will help him," and will so comfort him, that he may bear it patiently, and may understand that he is proved by that very patience, and from the proving may conceive great hope, and from that hope may find such joy, that he may glory in tribulations, and say with the Apostle: "I am filled with comfort: I exceedingly abound with joy in all our tribulation," II *Cor.* vii. Then, to prove that this will be so, he addeth an example from the past, saying: "Thou hast turned all his couch in his sickness," that is, thou, O God, sweet and mild, art wont so to deal with thy faithful; for if at any time thou hast seen some godly man burdened with temptations, or afflictions, thou hast been accustomed so to comfort him, as one comforteth a sick man by turning and turning again his whole couch, that he may lie more softly and rest. St. Ambrose and St. John Chrysostom so expound it, that they will have the couch to be turned in sickness to be the state of the sick man changed into the state of health. St. Jerome and St. Augustine expound it in the contrary manner, but altogether excellently and most profitably; for they will have this to be the sense. Because the Prophet had said: "The Lord help him on his bed of sorrow," a godly man might answer: If God giveth help, whence are so many scandals and tribulations to godly men? The Prophet answereth, that the same Lord turneth and removeth "all the couch," that is, those earthly things in which even the godly rest and take delight, that the godly pilgrim may not love the inn instead of his home; but from the loss of temporal consolations, though lawful, may learn to delight in God alone, to seek him only, to pant and sigh after the vision of him. For this is the cause why holy men have not only loved the tribulations which came from elsewhere, but have even brought them upon themselves, wearing down their own flesh with fastings, watchings, lyings on the ground, nakedness and scourges: because they understood that the mind cannot be wholly lifted up to seek God, while it hath anything on earth which it may love, or in which it may delight and rest. Both expositions are to be embraced, although the former seemeth to us more literal.

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. I SAID: O LORD, BE THOU MERCIFUL TO ME: HEAL MY SOUL, FOR I HAVE SINNED AGAINST THEE.**

Here now Christ beginneth to declare that he is that needy and poor one, of whom he had said in the beginning: "Blessed is he that understandeth concerning the needy and the poor." "I said, saith he: O Lord, be thou merciful to me," that is, be merciful to my mystical body, to my weak members. "Heal my soul, for I have sinned against thee," that is, for this I implore thy mercy, that thou mayest heal the infirmities of my faithful, whose sins I impute to myself, as if I had done them. It can also be so expounded, that Christ himself speaketh of his own passion, saying: "Be thou merciful to me," who am set in tribulation, that by raising me up speedily thou mayest deliver me from all suffering. "Heal my soul," which is sorrowful even unto death, and is therefore sorrowful and languisheth, fearing, grieving and being heavy; "for I have sinned against thee," that is, I have taken upon me the sins of the whole world. For that he speaketh not of sins committed by himself is manifest from the thirteenth verse, where he saith: "But thou hast upheld me by reason of my innocence." Therefore he who speaketh in the Psalm is not David, or any other, but he alone who both was innocent as to his own actions, and bore the sins of others.

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. MY ENEMIES HAVE SPOKEN EVILS AGAINST ME: WHEN SHALL HE DIE AND HIS NAME PERISH?**

These things manifestly belong to the Pharisees and priests of the Jews, who most greedily thirsted for the death of Christ, and often talked among themselves of putting him to death. Now that, *mihi* (to me), is put for *de me* (of me), as in the Psalm above: "That say to me," that is, of me, "'Tis well, 'tis well;" or it is superfluous, as in this very Psalm: "Against me they devised evils to me (*mihi*),"[^4] and often elsewhere.

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. AND IF HE CAME IN TO SEE ME, HE SPOKE VAIN THINGS: HIS HEART GATHERED TOGETHER INIQUITY TO ITSELF.**

He passeth from the Jews to Judas, as if he should say: "And if any one of them," as Judas the traitor, "came in to see," that is, to spy out and seek an opportunity of betraying me, "he spoke vain things," that is, he feigned something false, lest his mind should be discovered. This might also be referred to others, who came to Christ "tempting him, that they might insnare him in his speech," as in *Matt.* xxii. But this man "spoke indeed vain things" to Christ; yet in the mean time "his heart was gathering together iniquity to itself," that is, in his heart he was meditating deceits, and so he was multiplying and gathering together iniquity to himself, namely to his own eternal perdition. This truly is the just reward of liars and of the deceitful, that while they attempt to deceive others, they themselves are deceived by

<!-- p. 268 (PDF 279) -->

Satan, and while they contrive destruction for others, they prepare destruction for themselves.

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. HE WENT OUT AND SPOKE TO THE SAME PURPOSE.**

The Fathers take these words also of the same Judas, who, when he had spied out an opportunity of betraying the Lord, "went out, and spoke together" with those to whom he was about to betray the Lord. That, *in idipsum* (to the same purpose), the Hebrew copies join with the following verse; but we follow the ancient division of the Seventy Interpreters, who are worthy of greater credit than the later Rabbins, who added to the Bible the points and stops which we now have.

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. ALL MY ENEMIES WHISPERED TOGETHER AGAINST ME: THEY DEVISED EVILS TO ME.**

When they had heard Judas the traitor, they began to whisper among themselves, lest haply the matter should become known if they spoke with a loud voice, concerning the money to be given, and the way to be found of taking and destroying Christ. "All my enemies, saith he, whispered against me," that is, spoke with a low voice; "against me they devised evils to me," by whispering they deliberated of bringing death upon me. I have already said above that that *mihi* (to me) is superfluous, from the Hebrew phrase.

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. THEY DETERMINED AGAINST ME AN UNJUST WORD: SHALL HE THAT SLEEPETH RISE AGAIN NO MORE?**

From that whispering of the enemies this followed, that the Jews firmly determined to kill Christ; and this is what he addeth, saying: "They determined against me an unjust word," that is, they established an unjust decree against me, namely that they should deliver me, though innocent, to death. But he addeth forthwith: "Shall he that sleepeth rise again no more?" That is, although they have made so unjust a decree, shall they take from me the power of rising again? Nay rather, he therefore called his death a sleep, because as easy as it is for a man to rouse his sleeping companion from sleep, so easy is it for the Word of God to recall his dead flesh to life. Which thing he foretelleth long before, when he saith: "No man taketh my life away from me, but I lay it down: I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it up again," *John* x. In the Hebrew copy it is far otherwise; for it can be turned thus to the word: *Verbum belial infusum sibi, et qui dormivit non adjiciet ut resurgat* (A word of belial is poured into him, and he that hath slept shall rise again no more). But the Seventy Interpreters, who both were most learned and had most correct copies, for *verbum belial* (a word of belial) rightly turned, *verbum iniquum* (an unjust word); for *belial* signifieth *without yoke*, and is taken also for *the devil*; but in many places *men of belial* are called *men of iniquity*, as those who have shaken off the yoke of the law and follow the devil as their leader; and as *men of belial* are men of iniquity, so *a word of belial* is an unjust word. That which followeth, *infusum* (poured in), in Hebrew יצום, the Seventy read יציקו, which is *constituerunt* (they determined); and for בו *bo*, which is *ei* (to him), or *sibi* (to himself), they read בי *bi*, that is *mihi* (to me), or *in me* (upon me), or *contra me* (against me): for in Hebrew *sibi* and *mihi* are alike. Lastly for ואשר, which signifieth *et qui* (and he that), the Seventy read האשר, which signifieth *numquid* (shall ...?). But let the Hebrews look to it, whether by negligence or by malice they have corrupted this place; certainly our Vulgate edition agreeth with the Greek edition, and the Greek altogether consenteth with the true Hebrew; for it must needs be, either that the Hebrew reading, such as it now is, hath been corrupted by reason of the likeness of the letters, or that the Seventy Interpreters were most unskilful. There are not wanting some who refer this verse to David lying sick, and to the talk of men disputing of his health. But these are at variance with all the Fathers, while they expound the oracles of the Holy Ghost too meanly.

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. FOR EVEN THE MAN OF PEACE, IN WHOM I TRUSTED, WHO ATE MY BREAD, HATH GREATLY SUPPLANTED ME.**

He giveth the reason why the enemies determined an unjust word against him, and saith that the cause of that unjust decree was the betrayal of Judas. "For even, saith he, the man of peace (*homo pacis meæ*, the man of my peace)," that is, my enemies determined an unjust word against me, because "the man of my peace," that is, with whom I had peace, as a master with a disciple and a lord with a servant; and "the man in whom I trusted," that is, in whom I could confide as in a friend and familiar. "Who ate my bread," that is, who at my table, as one of the household, did eat with me. "Hath greatly supplanted me (*magnificavit super me supplantationem*)," that is, hath wonderfully supplanted me, when by treachery he delivered me to my enemies. This place the Lord cited thus, *John* xiii: "I speak not of you all: I know whom I have chosen. But that the scripture may be fulfilled: He that eateth bread with me, shall lift up his heel against me." In the Hebrew it is to the letter, *elevavit contra me calcaneum* (hath lifted up his heel against me); but the Seventy have explained the sense: for to *lift up the heel* against any one, and to *supplant* him, is the same thing. In which place it is to be noted that, when it is said *that the scripture may be fulfilled*, it is signified that the betrayal of Judas was foretold, and fulfilled as it had been foretold; and therefore that this place of the Psalm was not cited by Christ as something like, or common, but as properly belonging to this matter, as St. John Chrysostom hath noted; and therefore that they do not rightly, who endeavour to wrest these words of the Psalm to other senses. *Secondly* it is to be noted, with St. Augustine, that Judas was called *the man of peace*, because the Prophet foresaw that Christ was to be betrayed by Judas through the sign of peace, that is, through a kiss: which the Lord also signified, when he said to him: "Friend, whereto art thou come?" and: "Judas, dost thou betray the Son of man with a kiss?" Likewise that it was said by the Prophet, "who ate my bread," because he had foreseen that the betrayal of Judas was to be made

manifest by the morsel. It may be added that it was said by the same Prophet, "in whom I trusted," because he had foreseen that Christ would entrust the bag to Judas, which was a sign of confidence; and so the prediction agreeth excellently with the event. *Thirdly* it is to be noted that the indignity of this betrayal is expressed by the Prophet in a wonderful manner: for first he calleth Judas the man of Christ's peace, to show that no enmity, no injury, no cause of hatred had come between Christ and Judas; then he addeth that not only had no cause of hatred come between them, but that a cause of benevolence had, when he addeth: "In whom I trusted," because the Lord, entrusting the purse to Judas, showed him, as we have said, signs of confidence. Lastly he added that he was not only no enemy, nor only a friend, but even an intimate familiar, and one loaded with the greatest benefits; for on that very night in which he betrayed Christ, he had not only eaten common bread at the table of Christ, but also the heavenly and most divine bread; and before the supper the same Christ had humbly washed his feet, and thereby had shown examples of the highest charity and humility.

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. BUT THOU, O LORD, HAVE MERCY ON ME, AND RAISE ME UP AGAIN: AND I WILL REQUITE THEM.**

He now prayeth to the Father, and by way of prayer foretelleth what was to be: for truly after his resurrection he requited the Jews with the destruction which they deserved. "But thou, O Lord, saith he, have mercy on me, and raise me up again," that is, they indeed have established an unjust word against me, and, my disciple supplanting and betraying me, they will kill me; but "thou, O Lord, have mercy on me," dying on the cross, and a little after "raise me up again;" and then I will requite them with what they deserve. Now Christ the Son prayeth to the Father, not because he himself, as the Word of the Father, could not raise up his own flesh; but because he speaketh in the form of a servant, he deferreth to the Father, to whom in the form of God he was equal; otherwise he himself said, *John* ii: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up: but he spoke of the temple of his body;" and *John* x: "I have power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it up again." But what Christ rendered to the Jews after his resurrection is known to all, who from that time see the Jews rooted out of their own land, scattered among the nations, wandering without king, without priest, without God. Which the Lord had foretold to them, when he said: "The kingdom of God shall be taken from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof;" and elsewhere: "Your house shall be left to you, desolate;" and elsewhere: "They shall not leave in thee a stone upon a stone," *Matt.* xxi and xxiii, and *Luke* xix.

[Margin: Christ as man deferreth to the Father.]

[Margin: Verse 12.]

**12. BY THIS I KNOW, THAT THOU HAST HAD A GOOD WILL FOR ME: BECAUSE MY ENEMY SHALL NOT REJOICE OVER ME.**

He saith that his prayer was heard, and that this could be known from hence, that "the enemy should not rejoice over his death." Which indeed was fulfilled in Judas, who killed himself by hanging before Christ died, and before he himself could enjoy the price of his betrayal. Yet this can also be referred to the rest of his enemies, who rejoiced for so short a time, that they may be said not to have rejoiced.

[Margin: Verse 13.]

**13. BUT THOU HAST UPHELD ME BY REASON OF MY INNOCENCE: AND HAST ESTABLISHED ME IN THY SIGHT FOR EVER.**

He teacheth that the enemies will not long rejoice over his death, because he hath been upheld and exalted by God for his innocence. "But thou, saith he, hast upheld me," out of their hands, "by reason of my innocence," best known to thee, "and hast established me in thy sight for ever," that is, thou hast humbled me for an hour in the sight of men, but "thou hast upheld and established me in thy sight," that is, thou hast made me to stand before thy sight in heaven even for ever: for the word ותציבני signifieth *statuisti ante conspectum tuum* (thou hast set before thy sight); which was done by the exaltation and assumption into heaven.

[Margin: Verse 14.]

**14. BLESSED BE THE LORD THE GOD OF ISRAEL FROM ETERNITY TO ETERNITY. SO BE IT. SO BE IT.**

This conclusion, whether it be Christ's or the Prophet's, signifieth nothing else but that, for the exaltation of Christ and the confusion of his enemies, there is due honour and glory, "and blessing to God from eternity and unto eternity," that is, without end. Now that, *fiat, fiat* (so be it, so be it), in Hebrew אמן אמן *amen, amen*, is a confirmation of the things said, so that the sense is: truly, or faithfully, so be it, or so let it be done, that praise and glory be always rendered to God. And this is the end of the first book according to the Hebrews, who have divided the Psalter into five books.
