# TO THE MOST BLESSED AND MOST HOLY FATHER BENEDICT XIV, SUPREME PONTIFF, ALPHONSUS DE LIGUORI

The majesty of thy dignity, most blessed Father, and the height of thy doctrine and learning, ought either to have wholly persuaded me, or, while I doubted, greatly to have deterred me, from offering to thee this moral work of mine, written in a simpler and altogether lowly style: but the exceeding kindness of thy Holiness, and the most fervent zeal which hath ever shone forth in thee, first that thou mightest fulfil the part of an excellent prelate of particular churches, and afterwards that thou mightest most watchfully satisfy the office of supreme pastor of the universal Church, have moved me most willingly to dedicate to thee these slender labours of mine, which I resolved to undertake and to bring forth into the public light only for the salvation of souls. For when I had been called by the favour of God to the ministry of the missions, for the help of the people scattered through the country places, and of those chiefly who are more destitute of spiritual helps, there seemed to me needful for this a more than middling knowledge of moral matters, such as should be fitted both for instructing and for governing souls. Wherefore, as well for my own understanding as for that of the young men of our society, I judged it seasonable to choose out the opinions that are more probable and more profitable for the salvation of souls. And when, for very many years, having read again very many authors both of the indulgent and of the rigid opinion, I had found some yielding too much to indulgence, others too much given to austerity, I believed that I should do a thing worth the pains, if I should put forth a book which, holding the middle way, should set out the opinions more agreeable to truth, and more necessary to be known for the directing of consciences, and, this opportunity being taken, should in it commit to my brethren many things pertaining to practice, which I had learned in the exercise of the sacred missions. The work, God helping, I began and finished, and, being finished, I committed it to the press: and since it was generally well received, and had to be made public again, I brought it into better order, certain doctrines being more diligently unfolded, very many others being added, and besides some dissertations being joined to it on the infallibility of the pontifical definitions, and on their superiority over councils.

But because all books which from the principles of theology treat of controversies of faith and morals are owed to thee alone, who art both the supreme prince of theology and the one ruler of the Church, the preserver and defender of divine truth, and the one judge of controversies, appointed to this by God himself with a singular providence, as Maximian, bishop of Constantinople, said (*Epistle to the Orientals*): "That all the ends of the earth, and all who profess the true faith, look upon the power of the Roman pontiffs as upon the sun, whom out of the rest of mortals from the whole world the Maker of the world chose, to whom he granted, by the right of a perpetual privilege, to hold the chair of teaching as his own in chief, that whosoever desireth to know anything divine or deep, may have recourse to the oracle and doctrine of this instruction."

Hence I have thought that this work was justly to be dedicated to thee, and the more because one of the chief causes which led me to put it forth was, that all might have knowledge

of thy most wholesome bulls and encyclical letters, which have so greatly profited, and will profit even to the consummation of the ages, the universal reformation of the manners of all the faithful; seeing that in them thy Holiness, with the highest prudence, declining neither to too great rigour nor to too great indulgence, but condescending to the defect of the times, hath ordered with wonderful sweetness[^1] that which with mighty fortitude of mind thou hadst set before thee.

It was moreover my purpose to bring forward very many doctrines which I have learned from thy golden works, and in which thy Holiness hath delivered to the whole commonwealth of letters not a few things worthy of immortal remembrance and to be learned by heart. For this great eminence of thy doctrine, no less than the other virtues which shine forth in thee, namely integrity of manners, a wonderful detachment from kindred, singular prudence, an incomparable zeal for the salvation of souls, and one above all necessary to the supreme priesthood, have rendered thee most worthy of the supreme pontificate.

Lastly, if the dedications of books are wont to be witnesses of a mind grateful, and not unmindful of benefits received from some great prince, since I am myself one of the brethren of the congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer, to which thou hast lately most graciously granted the apostolic authority, it was by right and desert fitting that this work should be presented to thee alone, and to none besides.

Do thou therefore, most blessed Father, by that charity wherewith thou embracest mankind, by that care which thou both owest and earnestly renderest to all Christians, by him whose place thou holdest on earth, Jesus Christ himself, receive with thy wonted kindness this little gift, such as it is, which I consecrate and present to thee, that thou mayest moderate, correct, blot out whatsoever in it shall seem out of harmony with truth, and, if there is anything good, mayest favour and defend it, and mayest not disdain to follow with thy fatherly and apostolic blessing both the work itself, that it may profit the salvation of souls, and me and my companions, that, working together in our ministry for the divine glory, we may be able to bring back most abundant fruit in the vineyard of the Lord.

But it shall be ours continually to pour forth prayers to God, that he may long keep thy Holiness safe for the whole Christian world, and long leave thee on earth, to the end that at length, not without the protection of almighty God, the enemies of the Catholic religion, and all who stray from the true faith, may most happily be led to the fold and assembly of the faithful, to the one haven of salvation.

## TO OUR BELOVED SON ALPHONSUS DE LIGUORI, PRIEST OF THE CONGREGATION OF THE MOST HOLY REDEEMER

### BENEDICT XIV.

BELOVED SON, HEALTH AND APOSTOLIC BENEDICTION.
