BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM V.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*Unto the end, for her that obtaineth the inheritance. A psalm of David.*[^1]

[Margin: The Church is like a dove.]

This title is variously expounded, because in the Hebrew there is a certain word, נחלות *nechilot*, which the Rabbins, and they who follow the Rabbins, will have to signify a musical instrument. But they are altogether to be rejected by Catholics, since St. Jerome, most skilled in the Hebrew tongue, translated *pro ea quæ hæreditatem consequitur* (for her that obtaineth the inheritance). And the Hebrew words themselves altogether admit these interpretations, since נחל *nachal* signifieth *to inherit*; נחלות *nachalot*, *inheritances*; נחלת *nochelet*, *her that inheriteth*. It is credible therefore that the Hebrew text, changed by length of time in one letter or another, hath brought forth these varieties: but in a doubtful matter more credit is to be given to the Seventy Interpreters than to the recent Rabbins, especially since the version of St. Jerome favoureth the Seventy more than the Rabbins. This Psalm therefore is composed in the person of the Church, which daily obtaineth the inheritance of eternal salvation in her members, and at the end of the world the whole Church

<!-- p. 29 (PDF 40) -->

[Margin: is like a dove. (continues the gloss begun on p. 28)]

shall obtain it. But because the Church, in her better part, is like a dove, which perpetually mourneth, and keepeth innocence, therefore in this Psalm, exercising the office of a dove, she partly prayeth, partly detesteth sin. Which same thing all ought to do who pertain to the dove; for so are we united to God, in whom the inheritance consisteth, if by prayer we draw God to us, and by purity of life draw near to God.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. GIVE EAR, O LORD, TO MY WORDS, UNDERSTAND MY CRY.**

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. HEARKEN TO THE VOICE OF MY PRAYER, O MY KING AND MY GOD.**

In three ways it can come to pass that one is not heard by another: either because he perceiveth not the sound of the words, or because he perceiveth not the signification of the same words, or because he attendeth not, being intent on other things. God indeed perceiveth all things, and understandeth, and always attendeth: but he is said sometimes not to perceive, or not to understand, or not to attend, because he so despiseth him that prayeth as if he perceived not, or understood not, or attended not. Therefore the holy Prophet, being about to pray to God, asketh in the beginning that God would perceive, understand and attend. Now God despiseth him that prayeth, as if he perceived not the sound of the words, when he who prayeth is so distracted that he perceiveth not his own words himself; or prayeth so coldly that the prayer cannot ascend on high. And then doth God bear himself as if he understood not what is asked, when he who prayeth knoweth not what he asketh, asking those things which are not profitable for him, even though he pray attentively and fervently, and even cry out. Then, lastly, is God like one who attendeth not, when he that prayeth is not worthy to be heard, either because he prayeth without due humility, trust, and the other due circumstances, or because he is a sinner, and hath not yet begun to think of penance. The Prophet therefore, taught by the Holy Ghost with wonderful art, prayeth God for the gift of praying well, namely, that it may be given him so to pray as is needful, to the end that God may not reject his prayer, but may perceive the sound, understand the cry, and hearken to the prayer. And he addeth, *my King*, that he may the more easily obtain: for a good king is wont to hear his people; and he adjoineth, *and my God*, to show that he is not only the people of God as of a king, but also his creature, and dependeth on him in all things, and without him is nothing.

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. FOR TO THEE WILL I PRAY: O LORD, IN THE MORNING THOU SHALT HEAR MY VOICE.**

He giveth the reason why he asked to be heard by the Lord: for, saith he, to thee will I pray, that is, because my chief study is to be prayer, therefore am I so solicitous about being heard. And because the study of praying is not only my chief, but also my first study, therefore very early in the morning, before I set upon any other work, thou, O Lord, shalt hear my voice: for I will speak to none before thee. "Thou wilt open my lips, and my mouth shall declare thy praise," as is said in *Ps.* l.

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. IN THE MORNING I WILL STAND BEFORE THEE, AND WILL SEE: BECAUSE THOU ART NOT A GOD THAT WILLEST INIQUITY.**

Not only, saith he, will I pray in the morning, but I will also stand before thee, that is, I will endeavour to show myself present to thee; and I will contemplate (for so the Hebrew word soundeth) in that morning silence, before the throng of the businesses of this life rusheth in. And the chief matter of my contemplation shall be to consider that thou hatest nothing more than sin; that innocence and justice are exceedingly pleasing to thee, since thou art light and justice, and that therefore, if I will please thee, innocence and justice must be followed by me, and iniquity held in hatred.

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. NEITHER SHALL THE WICKED DWELL NEAR THEE: NOR SHALL THE UNJUST ABIDE BEFORE THY EYES.**

Not only doth God not will iniquity, but neither doth he will men of iniquity: therefore "the wicked," saith he, "shall not dwell near thee," that is, shall not draw near to thee by right of friendship; "nor shall the unjust abide before thy eyes," that is, thou wilt not long look upon the unjust with the eye of clemency. For now God looketh even upon the unjust with the eye of clemency, that is, he granteth them many temporal goods, but this shall not long endure. For shortly he will cast them from his face into eternal destruction.

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. THOU HATEST ALL THE WORKERS OF INIQUITY: THOU WILT DESTROY ALL THAT SPEAK A LIE.**

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. THE BLOODY AND THE DECEITFUL MAN THE LORD WILL ABHOR.**

Not only doth God not will iniquity; nor only doth he not will friendship with men of iniquity, but he hateth them, and willeth to destroy them, and in very deed will destroy them; and since sins are committed by work, by word, and by thought, or desire, therefore the Psalm numbereth them one by one: first, the workers of iniquity; secondly, them that speak a lie, which is understood of a hurtful lie; thirdly, the bloody man, that is, the cruel, thirsting after blood; and the deceitful, that is, he who meditateth and contriveth deceits.

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. BUT AS FOR ME IN THE MULTITUDE OF THY MERCY, I WILL COME INTO THY HOUSE; I WILL WORSHIP TOWARDS THY HOLY TEMPLE, IN THY FEAR.**

The Prophet had said that in the morning time he would contemplate how greatly God turneth away from sin and sinners. He now setteth forth the fruit of the contemplation, and saith: "But as for me in the multitude of thy mercy," that is, trusting in thy great mercy, not in my own strength, that I may avoid all sins, "I will come into thy house,"

<!-- p. 30 (PDF 41) -->

which is the house of prayer; "and I will worship towards thy holy temple," that is, I will prostrate myself before thy tabernacle; "in thy fear," fearing and trembling I will implore thy help.

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. CONDUCT ME, O LORD, IN THY JUSTICE: BECAUSE OF MY ENEMIES, DIRECT MY WAY IN THY SIGHT.**

This is the prayer which he prayeth in the house of God, namely, that God would conduct him in his justice, that is, would lead him through the paths of justice, making him to fulfil all the divine commandments, and so to avoid all sins; and, which is the same thing, would direct his way in his sight, that is, would make him to walk by the right way, having God always before his eyes. And this he prayeth chiefly because of his enemies, or (as the Hebrew hath it) because of them that lie in wait for him; for the divine help, directing and protecting, going before and following after, is necessary on account of the multitude of the enemies who lie in wait for us, and strive to draw us into sins, whether they be devils, or men that affright or allure. For he calleth enemies all who hinder the way of salvation, even though some seem most friendly; for "a man's enemies shall be they of his own household," as it is in *Matt.* chap. x. Now it mattereth little that in the Hebrew it is: *Dirige in conspectu meo viam tuam* (Direct thy way in my sight), as St. Jerome also, in his epistle *to Sunia and Fretella*, witnesseth that it is in the edition of the Seventy Interpreters, and in our Greek and Latin: *Dirige in conspectu tuo viam meam* (Direct my way in thy sight). For the sense cometh to the same, whether thou say according to the Hebrew copies: *Dirige in conspectu meo viam tuam*, that is, make me to find and see thy right way; or according to the Greek and the Latin: *Dirige in conspectu tuo viam meam*, that is, make me to walk before thee by the right way. For always the grace is asked of walking in the way of the commandments of God.

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. FOR THERE IS NO TRUTH IN THEIR MOUTH: THEIR HEART IS VAIN.**

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. THEIR THROAT IS AN OPEN SEPULCHRE: THEY DEALT DECEITFULLY WITH THEIR TONGUES.**

He giveth the reason why he desireth help against the enemies that lie in wait, namely, because they intend nothing but to deceive, and it is hard to avoid their snares. "There is no truth," saith he, "in their mouth," because, if they affright, they deceive, persuading to the flight of a small evil, that they may lead into the greatest evil; if they allure, they deceive, persuading to the pursuit of a small good, that they may bring to the loss of the highest good. "Their heart is vain": within and without they are perverse: they savour not, nor desire, any but vain things, therefore they cannot speak any but vain things. Now he repeateth the same in the following verse, but with the order changed; for what he had said: "Their heart is vain," he repeateth, saying: "Their throat is an open sepulchre"; and what he had said: "There is no truth in their mouth," he repeateth, saying: "They dealt deceitfully with their tongues." And so, when the throat is called *an open sepulchre*, it is signified that the throat, or mouth, and the tongue, which are the instruments of speaking, are as the mouth of a sepulchre; but that the inward parts of the sepulchre are the soul, or heart, where there are desires and thoughts horrid and stinking, as rotten carcasses, whence breatheth forth the stench of deadly words. And therefore he addeth: "They dealt deceitfully with their tongues," that is, my enemies speak not only false things, because there is no truth in their mouth, but also deceitful things, because under the appearance of good they persuade to evil. In the Hebrew it is *linguis suis blandiuntur* (they flatter with their tongues), which cometh to the same.

[Margin: Verse 12.]

**12. JUDGE THEM, O GOD. LET THEM FALL FROM THEIR DEVICES: ACCORDING TO THE MULTITUDE OF THEIR WICKEDNESS CAST THEM OUT: FOR THEY HAVE PROVOKED THEE, O LORD.**

It is a prophecy rather than an imprecation; for the Psalm signifieth that it shall come to pass that the enemies of the just shall not only not be admitted to the inheritance, of which the title of the Psalm speaketh, but shall be condemned to eternal punishments, and shall obtain nothing of the things which they contrive. "Judge them," that is, thou shalt judge, or, as the Hebrew hath it more significantly, *condemnabis illos* (thou shalt condemn them). "Let them fall from their devices," that is, they shall be disappointed of their hope, whereby they hoped that they could overthrow the elect. "According to the multitude of their wickedness cast them out," that is, according to their deserts they shall be cast out from the inheritance into eternal darkness. "For they have provoked thee, O Lord," that is, because, while they thought that they were hurting men, they offended not men, but God, according to that: "They have not rejected thee, but me," I *Kings* viii; and: "Thou hast not lied to men, but to God," *Acts* v.

[Margin: Verse 13.]

**13. AND ALL THEY SHALL BE GLAD THAT HOPE IN THEE: THEY SHALL REJOICE FOR EVER, AND THOU SHALT DWELL IN THEM.**[^2]

[Margin: Verse 14.]

**14. AND ALL THEY THAT LOVE THY NAME SHALL GLORY IN THEE: FOR THOU WILT BLESS THE JUST.**

[Margin: Verse 15.]

**15. O LORD, THOU HAST CROWNED US, AS WITH A SHIELD OF THY GOOD WILL.**

Here he foretelleth the most happy inheritance of the elect, to whom the title of the Psalm had promised it. And first he inviteth the just to gladness, for the good hope of things to come. "Let all them be glad that hope in thee," that is, although the just are now found in the labour of the combat, yet let them be glad in hope, who hope not in vanities, but in the true God, and in their own time they shall rejoice for ever, praising God, who shall dwell in them, that is, *super eos ad modum tentorii* (over them after the manner of a tent), as we read in the Hebrew; and so they shall be in God, and God is in them, and God shall be all in all. Now this rejoicing and praise, being as it were outward, shall spring from an inward gladness and glory beyond measure. For "all they that love thy name," that is, all the truly just, "shall glory in thee," O God.

[Margin: Charity maketh men just and friends of God.]

For charity it is which maketh men just, and friends, and sons of God. And they shall glory, "for thou wilt bless the just," that is, thou wilt bless every just man, and

<!-- p. 31 (PDF 42) -->

in blessing wilt do them good, rendering to them the crown of glory which they merit. And since the root of all goods is the benevolence of God, who chose us before the foundation of the world, seeing that from it proceed vocation, justification, good merits, and glorification itself; therefore he concludeth: "O Lord, thou hast crowned us, as with a shield of thy good will," that is, I confess to thee, O Lord, that all our goods flow from this, that thy good pleasure, or thy grace, hath encompassed us, and hath protected us on every side, as a shield is wont to protect soldiers. Like to which is that of *Ps.* cii: "Who crowneth thee with mercy and compassion." The Hebrew word is ambiguous as to the pronoun. For it can be translated *coronasti eum* (thou hast crowned him), that is, the just man, whom thou hast blessed, and so St. Jerome translateth. It can also rightly be translated, the points being changed, *coronasti nos* (thou hast crowned us), as the Seventy translated: but the meaning is altogether the same.

[^1]: Translator's note: Bellarmine does not number the title, and divides the Psalm into fifteen verses. The Douay-Rheims Bible counts the title as verse 1 and has thirteen verses: its verses 2 to 6 are his 1 to 5; its 7 is his 6 and 7; its 8, 9 and 10 are his 8, 9 and 10; its 11 is his 11 and 12; and its 12 and 13 are divided among his 13, 14 and 15.

[^2]: Translator's note: so the heading is printed (*Et lætabuntur*, shall be glad). The Vulgate reads *Et lætentur*, the Douay-Rheims "But let all them be glad", and Bellarmine himself quotes it so (*Lætentur*) in the commentary below.
