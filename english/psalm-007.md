BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM VII.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*The psalm of David which he sung to the Lord for the words of Chusi the son of Jemini.*[^1]

In the Hebrew there is not *Psalmus* (psalm), but שגיון *sigaton*, which properly signifieth *ignorance*; yet because the Seventy and St. Jerome rendered it *Psalmus*, it will have to be said that that word is ambiguous, and signifieth not only ignorance, but also some kind of canticle. Likewise who Chusi was is not so certain. St. Augustine, and others who know not the Hebrew tongue, think that he is Chusi the Arachite, the friend of David, who (as we read in II *Kings* xvi) sent a messenger to David, and made known to him the counsel of Achitophel. But that Chusi is written with the letter *Chet*, this one, of whom this Psalm speaketh, with the letter *Caph*; nor is it certain whether that Chusi was a son of Jemini. Lastly, David in this Psalm refuteth the calumny of a certain son of Jemini, as is plain from those words: "If I have done this thing"; but Chusi the Arachite spoke no calumny against David. St. Jerome thinketh that the title is to be understood of Saul, who is called Chus, that is, the Ethiopian, for the blackness of his manners, and was of the tribe of Benjamin, or (which is the same) a son of Jemini, as is plain from book I of *Kings*, chap. ix. For Saul, book I of *Kings*, chap. xxii, said before all his servants: "There is no one that pitieth my case, because my son hath raised up my servant against me, plotting against me to this day." This calumny therefore David refuteth in this Psalm, according to the opinion of St. Jerome. But it can also be said with equal probability that Chus in this place is Semei, who was also a son of Jemini, and cursed David when he fled from the persecution of Absalom, and said: "Come out, thou man of blood, and thou man of Belial. The Lord hath repaid thee for all the blood of the house of Saul: because thou hast usurped the kingdom in his stead," II *Kings* xvi. On account of this most wicked and most false calumny he seemeth to have composed this Psalm, praying God to defend his innocence and justice, and therefore the Psalm is called *ignorantia David* (the ignorance of David), because he was truly ignorant that he had done those evils of which he was accused by Saul, or by Semei. Therefore also he saith in the beginning of the Psalm: "O Lord my God, if I have done this thing, if there be iniquity in my hands," etc. The Psalm therefore seemeth to have been written in the time of the persecution of Absalom, on account of the words of the cursing of Semei, or in the time of the persecution of Saul, on account of the words aforesaid. But prophetically David beareth the person of Christ, and of every just man suffering calumny.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. O LORD MY GOD, IN THEE HAVE I PUT MY TRUST: SAVE ME FROM ALL THEM THAT PERSECUTE ME, AND DELIVER ME.**

"In thee," saith he, "have I put my trust," because almost all have forsaken me, so that even my son Absalom, or my father-in-law Saul, seek to kill me: I have none in whom to trust, but thee my God. "Save me from all them that persecute me." For many persecuted him, some by counsel, some by cursing, some by war and arms.

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. LEST AT ANY TIME HE SEIZE UPON MY SOUL LIKE A LION, WHILE THERE IS NO ONE TO REDEEM ME, NOR TO SAVE.**

He looketh to the chief of the persecution: lest perhaps, saith he, Saul, or Absalom, seize upon my soul, that is, my life, as a lion is wont to kill other living creatures without any mercy. "While there is no one to redeem me, nor to save," that is, while thou redeemest not, nor savest. For David knew that all human industry without God is vain. Scripture useth the word *redemption* for any deliverance whatsoever, even though it be not properly done by the paying of a price. For as God is said to sell those whom he in a manner estrangeth from his mercy, and delivereth up to the ministers of his justice to be punished: so God is said to redeem those whom, plucked again out of the hands of the ministers of justice, he mercifully delivereth.

[Margin: How is God said to sell and to redeem?]

In the Hebrew it is somewhat otherwise, namely: Lest perhaps he seize upon my soul like a lion, tearing it, and there be none that saveth; but in the meaning there is no variety.

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. O LORD MY GOD, IF I HAVE DONE THIS THING, IF THERE BE INIQUITY IN MY HANDS:**

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. IF I HAVE RENDERED TO THEM THAT REPAID ME EVILS, LET ME DESERVEDLY FALL EMPTY BEFORE MY ENEMIES.**

He giveth the cause why he asked to be delivered by God, namely, because God knoweth his innocence, and so he refuteth the calumny of Saul and of Semei concerning the plotting against Saul, and concerning the usurping of the kingdom; nor doth he only say that he hath not rendered evil for good, nor evil for evil, but that he hath rendered good for evil. First therefore he saith that he hath not rendered evil for good: "If I have done," saith he, "this thing," that is, if I have plotted against the king, or if I have usurped the kingdom by any fraud, or force; "if there be iniquity in my hands," that is, if I have wrought iniquity, rendering evil for good, that is, whereas I received from Saul great honour, that I should be his son-in-law, and captain over a thousand men, if I have rendered for these benefits the evil of plotting, as he himself saith. He addeth that he hath not rendered evil for evil: "If I have rendered to them that repaid me evils," that is, whereas Saul, or Semei, have repaid me many evils for the benefits received from me, so that they would have killed me, if I have rendered them the like, by procuring their death,

<!-- p. 39 (PDF 50) -->

and have not rather always abstained from slaying them, when I could easily and without punishment have done it. "Let me deservedly fall empty before my enemies," that is, if their calumnies are not false, I refuse not to fall in battle wholly empty, that is, without any glory of war, no loss being inflicted on the enemy, and the greatest received. In the Hebrew it is read in this manner: *Si reddidi retribuentibus mihi malum, et dimisi hostes meos vacuos* (If I have rendered evil to them that repaid me, and have let my enemies go empty); but the word *dimisi* (I have let go) can signify also *recedam* (I shall go back), or *discedam* (I shall depart), and then there is made the meaning which the Seventy rendered. For it is the same thing: I shall go back from my enemies empty. But if we read with St. Jerome: *Et dimisi hostes meos vacuos*, then the sense will be that he hath rendered good things for evil, as if he said: If I have let my enemies go without their having received from me some notable benefit, let this or that evil befall me.

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. LET THE ENEMY PURSUE MY SOUL, AND TAKE IT, AND TREAD DOWN MY LIFE ON THE EARTH, AND BRING DOWN MY GLORY TO THE DUST.**

These are the evils which he imprecateth upon himself, if the calumnies of Saul, or of Semei, are true. And the speech ever riseth. *First*, saith he, "let the enemy pursue my soul," that is, let him endeavour to kill me. *Secondly*, "and take it," that is, let me not be able to escape his hands, but that he take me to be slain. *Thirdly*, "and tread down my life on the earth," let him destroy my life with ignominy, as those things are destroyed which are ground small under the feet. *Fourthly*, "and bring down my glory to the dust," that is, after death let my glory be drawn down from the high place in which it now is, even to the dust, so that my memory may live among men with infamy and reproach. *Fifthly*, there is added in the Hebrew *Sela*, that is, may I remain infamous *always*.

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. RISE UP, O LORD, IN THY ANGER: AND BE THOU EXALTED IN THE BORDERS OF THY ENEMIES.**[^2]

His innocence being shown, he asketh of God a just defence. And because God is said by a metaphor to sleep when he helpeth not, and to rise from sleep when he beginneth to help, according to that of *Ps.* xliii: "Arise, why sleepest thou, O Lord?" therefore he now saith: "Rise in thy anger," that is, be angry with my enemies, and drive them back, and affright them, that they hurt me not. And that: "And be thou exalted in the borders of thy enemies," signifieth almost the same; for the sense is: Appear on high in the borders of the enemies, that all may see thee, that is, that all may feel thy presence. Now it mattereth little that in the Hebrew it is *inimicorum meorum* (of my enemies); in the Greek and the Latin, *inimicorum tuorum* (of thy enemies). For the same were the enemies of David and of God.

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. AND ARISE, O LORD MY GOD, IN THE PRECEPT WHICH THOU HAST COMMANDED: AND A CONGREGATION OF PEOPLE SHALL SURROUND THEE.**

He had asked simply the help of God against his enemies; now he addeth the reason why it becometh God to do this. And the reason is, that God commandeth the judges of the earth to deliver the innocent out of the hands of them that oppress them. Whence it followeth that it becometh him also to do it, who is the supreme judge over all judges. "Arise," saith he, "in the precept which thou hast commanded," that is, according to the precept which thou hast commanded. In the Hebrew it is: *Judicio quod mandasti* (In the judgment which thou hast commanded), and the sense is the same, namely, according to the judgment which thou hast commanded to be done. Moreover in the Hebrew it is: *Exurge ad me* (Arise to me), for that which we have: *Exurge, Domine, Deus meus* (Arise, O Lord my God). But it easily appeareth whence the diversity of interpretation hath arisen: for the Hebrew word can be read אלי *eli*, and so the Seventy read, and rightly translated *Deus meus* (my God); it can also be read אלי *elai*, and so they read who added the points, and St. Jerome; and therefore St. Jerome rendered *ad me* (to me), and so all render who receive the points. But, as I have often said, in varieties of this kind the authority of the Seventy ought to be great. "And a congregation of people shall surround thee," that is, from this showing of thy presence in restraining my enemies, and in my defence, it shall come to pass that many others shall know thee, and confess to thee, and praise thee, and so they shall surround thee, because where there are some gathered together in thy name, there art thou in the midst of them.

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. AND FOR THEIR SAKES RETURN THOU ON HIGH. THE LORD JUDGETH THE PEOPLE.**

Because he had said: "A congregation of people shall surround thee," therefore he now adjoineth: "And for their sakes return thou on high," that is, as for my sake thou wast exalted in the borders of my enemies, affrighting them from the throne of thy justice: so for the sake of the congregation of people praising thee, as often as there shall be need, ascend again to the high throne of justice, and appear in open sight in the borders of its enemies. That which followeth: "The Lord judgeth the people," is in the Hebrew joined with the following verse, and truly it agreeth very well with it; but it agreeth very well also with the present verse, as the Seventy Interpreters divided it. For it is the giving of a reason, as if the Prophet said: O Lord, for the congregation of people cleaving to thee, ascend the throne of judgment against their enemies, because the Lord judgeth the people, that is, because it is proper to the supreme Lord, such as thou art, to judge the people. Nor doth it matter whether we read *judicat* (judgeth) with the Greeks, or *judicabit* (shall judge) with the Hebrews, because the sense is the same, namely, it pertaineth to the Lord to judge the people.

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. JUDGE ME, O LORD, ACCORDING TO MY JUSTICE, AND ACCORDING TO MY INNOCENCE IN ME.**

This is the conclusion of the whole foregoing prayer. For because he was conscious to himself that the calumny of Saul, or of Semei, was false, and had God as the witness of his conscience; he asketh God himself, the supreme judge, to judge his cause according to

<!-- p. 40 (PDF 51) -->

his justice, and the innocence which is in him: and to give to every one as he deserveth.

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. THE WICKEDNESS OF SINNERS SHALL BE BROUGHT TO NOUGHT: AND THOU SHALT DIRECT THE JUST: THE SEARCHER OF HEARTS AND REINS IS GOD.**

This is as it were the second part of the Psalm, in which the Prophet teacheth that they who do evil do it against themselves, and thereby he exhorteth all to be converted from iniquity to justice. He saith therefore: "The wickedness of sinners shall be brought to nought" (*consumetur*),[^3] that is, let the wickedness of sinners be made complete, and come to the height, yet it shall not hurt the just man, for it shall not bend him to the right hand, or to the left; because "thou shalt direct the just," that is, by thy providence thou wilt be present to the just man, that he decline not to the right hand, or to the left. Thou, I say, wilt do this, who alone knowest who are truly just, since thou searchest hearts, that is, thoughts, and reins, that is, desires. In the Hebrew there is a verb of the future tense, יגמר *igmar*, that is, *consumetur* (shall be consumed), or *consummabitur* (shall be consummated); but the Seventy Interpreters read it without the letter י *iod*, which is very small, and can have been added afterwards: yet the sense cometh to the same. For if thou read: *Consumetur nequitia* (The wickedness shall be consumed), the sense will be: The wickedness of sinners shall grow even to the height, and shall be wholly consumed in the vexing of the just, and yet it shall not cast them down, nor bend them, because God will direct and establish them: for the Hebrew word תכונן *theconen* signifieth both.

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. JUST IS MY HELP FROM THE LORD: WHO SAVETH THE UPRIGHT OF HEART.**

From the universal sentence he gathereth in particular that he deservedly hopeth for the help of the Lord. "Just," saith he, "is my help," which shall come "from God," because it is just that God should help the just, since it is proper to him to save the upright of heart, that is, those who are truly just before God, who searcheth hearts. In the Hebrew the first word of this verse is joined with the verse above, *scrutans corda et renes Deus justus* (the searcher of hearts and reins is the just God); and then a new verse beginneth: *Adjutorium meum a Domino* (My help is from the Lord). The matter is not of great moment, and in doubtful things the authority of the Seventy, and of our Vulgate edition, seemeth to be preferred.

[Margin: Verse 12.]

**12. GOD IS A JUST JUDGE, STRONG AND PATIENT: IS HE ANGRY EVERY DAY?**

In this verse the Hebrew, Greek and Latin copies seem to disagree. For from the Hebrew St. Jerome translateth, *Deus judex justus, et fortis, comminans tota die* (God is a just judge, and strong, threatening all the day). From the Greek St. Augustine rendereth, *Deus judex justus, et fortis, et patiens, non iram adducens tota die* (God is a just judge, and strong, and patient, not bringing on anger all the day). Now these seem plainly contrary, *comminans* (threatening), or *irascens* (being angry) (for the Hebrew word signifieth both), *tota die* (all the day); and *non iram adducens per singulos dies* (not bringing on anger every day), that is, not being angry every day. But the Latin copy hath it by way of question, *numquid irascitur per singulos dies?* (is he angry every day?) But the agreement is easy. For the Hebrew word, if it be read אל *el*, as St. Jerome read, and as it is now read according to the points of the Rabbins, signifieth *strong*; but if it be read אל *al*, as it can be read with other points, and as the Seventy Interpreters read, it signifieth *not*. And this reading we judge to be the truer; and so the Greek copy agreeth with the Hebrew: for each hath *non irascitur* (he is not angry), or, he threateneth not. Now that *fortis* (strong), which is in the Greek, is not an interpretation of the Hebrew word אל *el*, but is a word added over and above by the Seventy for the sake of explaining, as also *patiens* (patient): for in the Hebrew it is to the word: *Deus judex justus non comminans* (God is a just judge, not threatening), or *non irascens omni die* (not being angry every day), or *tota die* (all the day). But the Latin Interpreter, following the Greek reading, rendered the sense, not the words, when he set it by way of question, *numquid irascitur per singulos dies?* for it is the same thing, he is not angry, and, is he angry? The meaning therefore of this verse is, that God the just judge, who is at once strong and patient, doth not always threaten, or be angry, but sometimes in very deed poureth out his anger by punishing most grievously; which is said against those who, seeing that God often threateneth many evils to sinners, and yet putteth off the execution, despise the threats of God. Now that this is the sense of this verse is gathered from the following.

[Margin: Verse 13.]

**13. EXCEPT YOU WILL BE CONVERTED, HE WILL BRANDISH HIS SWORD: HE HATH BENT HIS BOW AND MADE IT READY.**

The Prophet proveth that God is not always angry, or threatening, but sometimes poureth out his anger, and bringeth on the evils which he threatened: "Except you will be converted," saith he, "he will brandish his sword" (*vibrabit*; for so it is to be read, not *vibravit*, he hath brandished), that is, he will shake his sword in striking, and so will make it to glitter. Nor will he smite with the sword only, but also with the bow: for he hath already bent his bow and made it ready. Now he useth the similitude of the sword and of arrows, to signify that God striketh as well near as far off. When the cause of the evil brought upon us is near and manifest, then God striketh hand to hand. When the cause of the evil is remote and hidden, then God seemeth to shoot his arrows from afar.

[Margin: Verse 14.]

**14. AND IN IT HE HATH PREPARED THE INSTRUMENTS OF DEATH, HE HATH MADE READY HIS ARROWS FOR THEM THAT BURN.**[^4]

Lest we should think that the arrows of God can easily be driven back, or avoided, the Prophet addeth that those arrows are "the instruments of death" (*vasa mortis*, vessels of death), that is, deadly instruments, and arrows made, or fashioned, *ardentibus*, that is, of burning matter, so that they are fiery darts, which most swiftly and with the greatest force pierce all things. Moreover the word *vas* (vessel) is taken in many places in the Scriptures for an instrument, as *Ps.* lxx: *In vasis Psalmi* (With the instruments of psaltery); *Isa.* xxii: *Vasa musicorum* (Vessels of music); *Jer.* l: *Vasa iræ* (The weapons of wrath), and chap. li: *Vasa belli* (The weapons of war).

[Margin: Verse 15.]

**15. BEHOLD HE HATH BEEN IN LABOUR WITH INJUSTICE; HE HATH CONCEIVED SORROW, AND BROUGHT FORTH INIQUITY.**

In the three following verses the Prophet showeth that the arrows of God, as being truly fiery, are borne most swiftly and with the greatest force, so that they cannot be avoided. For by that very

<!-- p. 41 (PDF 52) -->

action, the providence of God so disposing, by which sinners make ready to hurt the just, they kill themselves. For this is the wonderful punishment of God upon sinners, when he bringeth it to pass that all their contrivances are turned against themselves. "Behold," saith he, the sinner "hath conceived sorrow, and brought forth iniquity," and hath opened a hole, and dug deep, that he might overwhelm the just man, either openly or secretly; but, God so working, the sinner himself is fallen into his own hole, and the sorrow which he had conceived, and the iniquity which he had brought forth, have fallen back upon his head. But let us explain each thing: "Behold he hath been in labour with injustice," the sinner, namely, hath wrought some unjust work against the just man. Now in this place *parturiit* (he hath been in labour) is not something different from the word *peperit* (he hath brought forth), but altogether the same; for he straightway explaineth more clearly from what seed he hath been in labour, or hath brought forth. "He hath conceived," saith he, "sorrow, and brought forth iniquity." For the seed is conceived, and the offspring is brought forth.[^5] The conceiving of sorrow is the conceiving of envy, or of hatred toward a neighbour, which are the seeds of evils; and rightly is the conceiving of envy, or of hatred, called the conceiving of sorrow, because envy and hatred torment and rend the mind of him that envieth, and of him that hateth. From this evil seed conceived there is brought forth an iniquitous work, as murder, robbery, detraction, false witness, and the like. Now although to some there seem to be three distinct things: *Parturiit injustitiam, concepit dolorem, peperit iniquitatem* (He hath been in labour with injustice, he hath conceived sorrow, he hath brought forth iniquity), and labour is truly something in the midst between conception and birth, yet I have said that in this place there are but two, both because two only answer to them a little below, when it is said: "His sorrow shall be turned, and his iniquity shall come down upon his crown"; and because if *parturiit* were set in this place for a labour distinct from birth, it would be set after the word *concepit* (he hath conceived), not before it. Therefore *parturiit injustitiam* was said in sum and briefly; then it was explained by those words, *concepit dolorem, et peperit iniquitatem*.

[Margin: Verse 16.]

**16. HE HATH OPENED A PIT AND DUG IT; AND HE IS FALLEN INTO THE HOLE HE MADE.**

He had said that the sinner hath brought forth iniquity against the just man; he addeth that he hath also opened a pit, or cistern, as it is in the Hebrew, בור *bor*. Now he signifieth by these similitudes that the sinner contriveth evil for the just sometimes openly, sometimes secretly; and because both bringing forth and digging are very laborious works, it is signified that evil works are not done without sorrow and labour. Hence that voice of the lost, *Wis.* v: "We have walked through hard ways." "And he is fallen into the hole he made." Now the Prophet beginneth to show that those evils of sinful men hurt the sinners themselves only, and that this is the sword, or the arrows, of God; and he beginneth from the last evil, because he had ended in it. He hath opened, saith he, a pit, or cistern, that the just man, not knowing that there was a hole there, might fall into it; but he himself, who had dug it, first fell into it.

[Margin: Verse 17.]

**17. HIS SORROW SHALL BE TURNED ON HIS OWN HEAD: AND HIS INIQUITY SHALL COME DOWN UPON HIS CROWN.**

Not only hidden evils, as the opening of a hole, but manifest ones also, as hatred, or envy conceived, which by many signs showeth itself outwardly; and the very evils which proceed from hatred and envy, as murders and robberies, and the like, shall, God so disposing, fall back upon their author. Saul and David, Christ and the Jews, the martyrs and the persecutors, are an example.

[Margin: Verse 18.]

**18. I WILL GIVE GLORY TO THE LORD ACCORDING TO HIS JUSTICE: AND WILL SING TO THE NAME OF THE LORD THE MOST HIGH.**

He concludeth the Psalm with the praise of God: "I will give glory to the Lord" (*Confitebor Domino*, I will confess to the Lord), that is, I will praise the Lord. For so confession is taken in many places in the Scriptures for praise, because, to wit, he who praiseth confesseth that he whom he praiseth is worthy of praise; "according to his justice," that is, I will praise him as his justice deserveth, which in so wonderful ways delivereth the just, and punisheth the unjust. "And will sing to the name of the Lord the most high"; the same is signified in other words: I will say a hymn to the Lord the most high, that is, to the supreme judge, who sitteth on the highest throne above all other judges.
