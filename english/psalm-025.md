BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM XXV.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*Unto the end, a Psalm for David.*

In the Hebrew there is nothing for a title but the simple name *David*, or rather *to David*. But the argument is the same as that of the seventh Psalm. For when David was accused by most grievous calumnies, as if he sought to seize the kingdom, and was therefore forced to live in exile, and to seek hiding places, he wrote this Psalm, in which he bringeth God as the witness of his innocence, and prayeth the same God for his deliverance.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. JUDGE ME, O LORD, FOR I HAVE WALKED IN MY INNOCENCE: AND I HAVE PUT MY TRUST IN THE LORD, AND SHALL NOT BE WEAKENED.**

David, having a cause with the king, in a certain manner appealeth to God, who is the King of kings: for there was then none other on earth, to whom he could appeal. "Judge me, O Lord," that is, be thou, O Lord, my judge; suffer me not to be judged by Saul, but take thou this judgment upon thee. "For I have walked in my innocence," that is, I boldly appeal to the judgment of God, because my conscience, which God alone seeth, reproveth me not; for I have walked in innocence, that is, I have lived innocently. "And I have put my trust in the Lord, and shall not be weakened," that is, trusting[^1] in the justice of God, I shall not fail in this judgment, but shall prevail. That phrase, *non infirmabor* (I shall not be weakened), is in the Hebrew, *non deficiam* (I shall not fail), or, *non vacillabo* (I shall not waver); therefore *non infirmabor* signifieth: I shall not be weak and feeble, so as to lose my cause.

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. PROVE ME, O LORD, AND TRY ME; BURN MY REINS AND MY HEART.**

[Margin: God doth not prove nor try.]

Because he had said that he had lived innocently, he proveth this by the testimony of God, who can neither be deceived nor deceive: for it is not said to God, *prove and try*, as if he were ignorant of anything, but that he may make known to others the things which he alone seeth. And so holy David, relying on a good conscience and the sincerity of his heart, saith to the Lord: "Prove me, and try me," that is, search diligently whether it be so, look into the inmost and deepest recesses of my heart; nay more, "burn my reins and my heart," that is, examine my thoughts and my desires, with that diligence with which gold is proved, when it is examined by fire. For in this place, as I at least judge, David asketh not to be proved and tried by adversities, or that his reins and his heart be burned with the fire of tribulations, since on the contrary he rather asketh in this Psalm deliverance from adversities; but he asketh, as I said, to be proved and tried by an exact searching and inspection, and because God seeth and looketh through all things most exactly, the meaning of this place is, that God would make known to men the innocence of his servant, and refute the calumnies of the enemies in very deed and by facts.

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. FOR THY MERCY IS BEFORE MY EYES; AND I AM WELL PLEASED WITH THY TRUTH.**

He giveth the cause, why he would be proved and tried, because his conscience bore him good witness, as if he said: I ask that thou prove me, because I have walked by thy ways; for "all thy ways are mercy and truth," *Ps.* xxiv. And "thy mercy is before my eyes," that is, I always behold and consider thy mercy, that I may imitate it, and live with my neighbours according to it; and likewise: "I am well pleased with thy truth," or thy truth hath pleased me, and therefore I have walked according to it. In the Hebrew there is התהלכתי, that is, *ambulavi in veritate tua* (I have walked in thy truth); therefore the Seventy looked not so much to the word as to the sense, which they did also in *Ps.* xxxiv, verse 17.

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. I HAVE NOT SAT WITH THE COUNCIL OF VANITY: NEITHER WILL I GO IN WITH THE DOERS OF UNJUST THINGS.**

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. I HAVE HATED THE ASSEMBLY OF THE MALIGNANT; AND WITH THE WICKED I WILL NOT SIT.**

Rightly, in my judgment, Theodoret expoundeth these words of the meetings of the Gentile idolaters in their profane temples, which David always abhorred, while he lived in exile with the king of the Philistines. For these things are set against those which are afterwards in the same Psalm: "I have loved, O Lord, the beauty of thy house"; and a little before: "I will compass thy altar, O Lord"; and below: "In the churches I will bless thee, O Lord." He calleth the company of idolaters the council of vanity, because nothing is more empty than idols, which are false images. Which the Apostle signifieth, when he saith: "We know that an idol is nothing in the world," I *Cor.* viii; and everywhere in the Scriptures, idols are called vain things, or

<!-- p. 147 (PDF 158) -->

vanities, as *Deut.* xxxii: "They have provoked me with that which was no god, and have angered me with their vanities"; and I *Kings* xii: "Turn not aside after vain things which shall never profit you, nor deliver you, because they are vain"; see also III *Kings* xvi, IV *Kings* xvii; *Jer.* ii, and elsewhere. The same idolaters are rightly called doers of unjust things, because it is the highest injustice to give to created things the worship due to God alone. And what he had called *the council of vanity*, in the following verse he calleth *the assembly of the malignant*, and those whom he had called *doers of unjust things*, he calleth the wicked, which word properly agreeth to the worshippers of idols. In the Hebrew for *iniqua gerentibus* (the doers of unjust things), there is נעלמים *nahalamim*, which signifieth *the hidden*, or *those that play childishly*: for *hhalam* is to be hidden, and *hhelem* is *a child*. Now hypocrites, dissemblers, scoffers, and in general all doers of unjust things, as the Seventy rightly render, are called *the hidden*, because "every one that doth evil hateth the light," as is said, *John* iii. But idolaters may also most rightly be called those that play childishly, because as children fashion out of clay little houses and various living creatures, so idolaters fashion gods out of various matter. Therefore Moses called idolatry play: "The people sat down to eat, and drink, and they rose up to play," *Exod.* xxxii; which play the Apostle teacheth to have been idolatry, I *Cor.* x, verse 7.

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. I WILL WASH MY HANDS AMONG THE INNOCENT; AND WILL COMPASS THY ALTAR, O LORD.**

After he had said that he hated the conventicles of the unbelieving idolaters, among whom he then dwelt, he addeth that on the contrary he followeth with the highest love the tabernacle of the Lord and the assembly of the Saints, and briefly describeth what he will do, when by the help of God he shall have been called back from exile into his country. "I will wash," saith he, "my hands among the innocent; and will compass thy altar, O Lord," that is, I will wash my hands before entering the tabernacle, as pious men are wont to do, and I will go round thy altar with them that sing psalms. In the Hebrew there is, *lavabo in innocentia manus meas* (I will wash my hands in innocence); but the sense is the same: for to wash the hands in innocence is in this place *to wash the hands after the manner of the innocent*, or as the innocent are wont to do, which signifieth the same as to wash the hands among the innocent, or with the innocent. Now as to the meaning, some will have it that David alluded to the washing of hands which is sometimes done in sign of innocence, as Pilate washed his hands before the Jews, saying: "I am innocent of the blood of this just man," *Matt.* xxvii, as if he said: Behold I have washed my hands, I will not defile them with the blood of this just man, therefore I dare not condemn him, which we also commonly say, when we will not be mixed in a business, in which there is danger of sinning: *Io me ne lavo le mani* (I wash my hands of it). But I judge it more probable, and more agreeable to what followeth, if we say that David speaketh of the rite of the Hebrews, who before entering the tabernacle purified both themselves and the victims, which washings the Apostle, *Heb.* ix, calleth *divers washings*, and *justices of the flesh*; and because that outward washing ought to be a sign of inward purity, therefore David saith: "I will wash my hands among the innocent," that is, I will wash my hands in sign of true inward purity, as the innocent do, and I will not wash my hands with hypocrites, who bear clean hands and a filthy heart. And that which followeth, *and will compass thy altar*, some refer to the multitude of victims, with which the altar was to be filled on every side. But it seemeth to me truer, that it be referred to the choirs of them that sing round about the altar; for in the following Psalm there is: "I have gone round, and have offered up a sacrifice of jubilation," etc.; and in this place it is straightway added: "That I may hear the voice of thy praise: and tell of all thy wondrous works."

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. THAT I MAY HEAR THE VOICE OF THY PRAISE: AND TELL OF ALL THY WONDROUS WORKS.**

[Margin: Who are they that hear the voice of the praise of God?]

He explaineth to what end he would compass the altar of the Lord, namely, saith he, that mingled with the choirs of them that sing, I may hear the praises of the Lord, and may myself also sing them. In the Hebrew there is, if we receive the points, *ut audire faciam vocem laudis* (that I may make the voice of praise to be heard); but if the points be changed it may be read, as the Seventy and St. Jerome read, *ut audiam vocem laudis* (that I may hear the voice of praise). St. Augustine acutely and piously teacheth in this place against the Pelagians, that those only hear the voice of the praise of God, who perceive with inmost sense that whatsoever they have is the gift of God; for in the hearts of those who have ears to hear, all their good merits, and all their virtues, sound the praise of grace: but on the contrary, they who presume upon their own justice, and are puffed up with their own merits, as if they had received them from themselves, not from God, hear not the voice of the praise of God, but the voice of their own praise.

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. I HAVE LOVED, O LORD, THE BEAUTY OF THY HOUSE; AND THE PLACE WHERE THY GLORY DWELLETH.**

The Prophet showeth that for no other cause was exile more grievous to him, than because he could not see the tabernacle of the Lord; for a mind vehemently kindled toward the love of God judgeth nothing on earth more beautiful than that place, in which the presence of the Lord showeth itself more clearly. Now the tabernacle, in which was the ark of the covenant, is called *the house of God, the place where his glory dwelleth*,[^2] because there often came down upon that place a bright cloud, in sign of the presence of God, "who inhabiteth light inaccessible," as the Apostle saith, I *Tim.* vi; and because in the same place was the oracle, from which God was wont to give answers. In the Hebrew there is: *Dilexi habitaculum domus tuæ* (I have loved the habitation of thy house), for that which we have: *Dilexi decorem domus tuæ* (I have loved the beauty of thy house); perhaps the Seventy did not read מעון, *habitation*; but נעם, the letters being inverted, which signifieth *beauty*.

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. TAKE NOT AWAY MY SOUL, O GOD, WITH THE WICKED: NOR MY LIFE WITH BLOODY MEN.**

Because at the beginning he appealed to God as judge, and set forth his innocence, of which the same God was witness,

<!-- p. 148 (PDF 159) -->

he concludeth, praying that sentence be given for him: "Take not away," saith he, "my soul with the wicked," that is, condemn me not, as thou art wont to condemn the wicked. He hath put the soul for the life, or for his whole person, as often elsewhere. By *bloody men* (*viros sanguinum*, men of bloods) he understandeth murderers, such as they were, who persecuted him. Now that word, *Deus* (O God), is not in the Hebrew, nor in the Greek, nor is it read by Jerome and Augustine; yet it is read by Theodoret and Euthymius, from which we understand that it was not wanting in their Greek copies.

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. IN WHOSE HANDS ARE INIQUITIES: THEIR RIGHT HAND IS FILLED WITH GIFTS.**

He declareth who are the wicked and the bloody men, of whom he had spoken in the verse above, and saith that they are those, who judge perversely for gifts received; for he marketh the sins of princes, who are the judges of peoples. And he beautifully saith: "In whose hands are iniquities"; for so he placeth iniquity in that part of the body, by which gifts are received, to show that the receiving of gifts was the cause of the iniquity.

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. BUT AS FOR ME, I HAVE WALKED IN MY INNOCENCE: REDEEM ME, AND HAVE MERCY ON ME.**

He repeateth the cause, why he ought not to be condemned with the wicked, namely because "he hath walked in innocence," that is, hath lived innocently. "Redeem me, and have mercy on me," that is, deliver me from the present calamity, and henceforth have mercy, lest I fall again into the same; for everywhere in the Scriptures *to redeem* is put for *to deliver*, that is, the species for the genus, unless perhaps the Holy Ghost would intimate, that every deliverance of the elect from any tribulation whatever may be called a redemption, because it is purchased by the price of the blood of Christ our redeemer.

[Margin: Verse 12.]

**12. MY FOOT HATH STOOD IN THE DIRECT WAY: IN THE CHURCHES I WILL BLESS THEE, O LORD.**[^3]

[Margin: A prophecy of the Psalms of David to be sung in the churches.]

These words look back to the last clause of the verse above, namely *and have mercy on me*, as if he said: I have asked deliverance from the present calamity, because I have lived innocently; I ask also that thou have mercy for the time to come, because *my foot hath stood*, that is, is established and fixed *in uprightness*, and thereby I shall not easily depart from the right road of thy law: and for thanksgiving *I will bless* and praise thee with hymns and psalms *in the churches*, that is, in the assemblies of the pious.[^4] Now this is, as Euthymius noteth, a prophecy, that in very deed in the time of the new Testament the Psalms of David were to be sung in all the churches.

[^1]: Translator's note: the print has *infidens*, taken as *confidens* (trusting).

[^2]: Translator's note: the print has *locus habitationis gloria ejus*, taken as *gloriæ ejus*, after the verse.

[^3]: Translator's note: the margin has *Vers. 11* against this verse, a misprint for 12.

[^4]: Translator's note: the print has *impiorum conventibus* (the assemblies of the wicked), which the sense forbids; it is taken as *piorum conventibus*.
