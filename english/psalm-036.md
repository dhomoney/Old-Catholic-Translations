BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM XXXVI.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*A psalm for David himself.*

There is nothing new in the title. Now this Psalm containeth a most wholesome medicine for healing the minds of the little ones, who are easily scandalized when they see the wicked prosper in this world, and thence begin to doubt of the providence of God, and to be emboldened to imitate the works of the wicked. There is not in this Psalm one continued discourse, but a collection

<!-- p. 230 (PDF 241) -->

of sentences, as it were of aphorisms, and therefore, that it might the more easily be kept in memory, it is disposed by the alphabet, so that the first aphorism, as it were, containing two verses, beginneth with the first letter, the second with the second, and so of the rest. Which letters we shall mark, that the division of the sentences may appear.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. *Aleph.* BE NOT EMULOUS OF EVILDOERS; NOR ENVY THEM THAT WORK INIQUITY.**[^1]

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. FOR THEY SHALL SHORTLY WITHER AWAY AS GRASS, AND AS THE GREEN HERBS SHALL QUICKLY FALL.**

[Margin: The prosperity of the wicked shall not last long.]

The Prophet, as a spiritual physician, admonisheth the faithful that, if they see the wicked prosper, they be not provoked to imitate them, nor be angry, or have indignation against God, as though he were unjust: for the prosperity of the wicked shall not last long, nay, it shall pass most swiftly; and then shall appear the justice and providence of God, which suffereth not the wicked long to exult and rejoice. Yet the words are somewhat obscure, by reason of the construction of the verbs with prepositions, which savoureth of a Hebraism, or a Grecism: but we shall endeavour to make the meaning plain. "Be not emulous of evildoers," that is, be not provoked to emulation, or, become not an emulator and rival of evildoers. In the Hebrew it is, אל תתחר *al thithchar,* that is *ne irascaris* (be not angry); or, as St. Jerome rendereth, *ne contendas cum malignantibus* (contend not with evildoers); but the sense is the same. For he speaketh not of the contention and fight which is made with sword, or spear; but of the anger and contention of emulation, as some strive in the theatre in the singing of songs, others in the lists in the racing of horses, or of chariots, and are angry unless they overcome. Therefore the sense is: contend not with evildoers in getting temporal goods by right and wrong, nor be angry, if that emulation succeed but ill. That which followeth: "Nor envy (*neque zelaveris*) them that work iniquity," is the same as if it had been said: Nor have indignation, kindled with zeal, over them that work iniquity, judging it, namely, unjust that God should bestow so great goods upon men of iniquity. This is had more clearly in *Ps.* lxxii: "How good is God to Israel, to them that are of a right heart! But my feet were almost moved; my steps had well nigh slipped. Because I had a zeal (*zelavi*) on occasion of the wicked, seeing the prosperity (*pacem*, peace) of sinners," that is, God indeed seemeth good to them that are conformable to his will: but I, who am not strong in so great uprightness, almost fell into scandal and doubt, while I am kindled with zeal as it were for justice, and am angry over the prosperity of the wicked, while I think on the peace and the other goods which they possess, whereas they are most worthy of torments and punishments. It followeth: "For they shall shortly wither away as grass, and as the green herbs shall quickly fall." A most fit similitude to shew the shortness of temporal prosperity. For grass and green herbs have not deep roots, as have cedars and palm trees, to which the just are wont to be compared. "The just, saith the same David, *Ps.* cxi,[^2] shall flourish like the palm tree: he shall grow up like the cedar of Libanus." Hence it cometh that grass and green herbs straightway wither and rot, while palm trees and cedars endure a very long time. Nor did the Prophet say that the prosperity of the wicked shall quickly perish, but that the wicked themselves shall most quickly perish: and when they perish, there is no doubt but all their glory and prosperity perisheth. And although sometimes the wicked live many years, and all things succeed prosperously with them, yet that multitude of years is nothing at all, if it be compared with the long continuance of the just and of their felicity. For "the just shall live for evermore," *Wis.* v; and: "The just shall be in everlasting remembrance," *Ps.* cxi; and nothing is lasting which hath an end. See *Wis.* v, if thou wilt see painted to the life the swiftness and shortness of all temporal things. "All those things, it saith, are passed away like a shadow, and like a post that runneth on, and as a ship that passeth through the waves: whereof when it is gone by, the trace cannot be found; or as when a bird flieth through the air, of the passage of which no mark can be found, but only the sound of the wings beating the light air; or as when an arrow is shot at a mark, the divided air presently cometh together again, so that the passage thereof is not known: so we also being born, forthwith ceased to be," etc.

[Margin: Nothing is lasting which hath an end.]

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. *Beth.* TRUST IN THE LORD, AND DO GOOD, AND DWELL IN THE LAND, AND THOU SHALT BE FED WITH ITS RICHES.**

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. DELIGHT IN THE LORD, AND HE WILL GIVE THEE THE REQUESTS OF THY HEART.**

David hath deterred us from evil in the first aphorism; now in the second he stirreth us to good, as if he said: If thou wilt be blessed and happy, understand who is the author of all felicity, and from him expect it, from him hope for it, not from others. "Trust, saith he, in the Lord." For he, who is Lord of all things, he alone can truly give what is his own; but that thy hope may be firm, and thou be not confounded, "do good," by working well, according to the commandments of God: for who would readily dare to hope in him whom he knoweth to be his enemy? And then at length "dwell in the land" secure: for who shall cast thee out, if thou be the friend of him whose is the earth, and the fulness thereof; nay more, "thou shalt be fed with its riches:" for the earth shall bring forth its fruits abundantly to nourish thee. St. Jerome readeth from the Hebrew *fide* (with faith), for *divitiis* (with riches). And truly in the Hebrew text there is אמונה *emunah,* which signifieth *fidem* (faith), or *veritatem* (truth). But the Seventy Interpreters seem not to have had in their text *emunah,* but המונה *hamonah,* which word signifieth *divitias* (riches), as is plain also from this same Psalm, verse 16, where it is read: "Better is a little to the just, than the great riches of the wicked."

<!-- p. 231 (PDF 242) -->

Although even if we read, *pasceris fide* (thou shalt be fed with faith), or *veritate* (with truth), the sense will be: Thou shalt be fed in the land with faith, that is, faithfully and truly, which is almost the same as *pasceris in divitiis ejus* (thou shalt be fed with its riches). For he is faithfully and truly fed in the land, to whom there is not wanting abundance of the fruits of the earth, which are its riches. But that a man may work well, and be the friend of God, so as to trust securely in him, charity is necessary; therefore he saith: "Delight in the Lord," that is, love God from the heart, and let him be thy delight; and then thou shalt be secure, because "he will give thee the requests of thy heart," that is, whatsoever thy heart shall desire. But, thou wilt say, we have known many who hoped in God, and did good, and delighted in the Lord, and yet were not suffered to dwell in the land, nor were fed with its riches, nor did God give them the requests of their heart. For, to pass over the well-nigh infinite multitude of the faithful, whom we see daily passing their life in extreme need, surely blessed Paul hoped in the Lord, and did good, and yet, as he himself testifieth, I *Cor.* iv, he was cast out "as the refuse of this world, and the offscouring," and hungered, and thirsted, and was naked. And though he delighted in the Lord, the Lord gave him not the requests of his heart, since thrice he besought "that the sting of the flesh might be taken from him," and obtained it not, II *Cor.* xii. I answer that the greatest part of those who are in extreme need do not hope in the Lord as they ought, nor keep his commandments, as he himself biddeth; much less also do they delight in the Lord: for besides the promises of this Psalm, Christ himself, *Matt.* vi, saith most plainly: "Behold the birds of the air, for they neither sow, nor do they reap, nor gather into barns: and God feedeth them.[^3] Are not you of much more value than they?" and below: "Seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his justice, and all these things shall be added unto you." Therefore it cannot be doubted that God will provide all things necessary for his own, if they earnestly trust in God, and keep his commandments. But if at any time this be not done, as was said of the Apostle, the reason is, because God giveth them something better, and wherewith they are more content, that is, the exceeding great merit of patience. For the same blessed Paul, who wrote of his need and other tribulations, wrote also: "I am filled with comfort: I exceedingly abound with joy in all our tribulation," II *Cor.* vii. And if the Lord gave him not the request of his heart, that he should take away the sting of the flesh; he gave him abundant grace, by which that sting might be turned into an instrument of triumph. Therefore he gave not a small thing, that he might give a great thing, which he knew to be the true request of his heart.

[Margin: Why are the just sometimes pressed with grievous want?]

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. *Ghimel.* COMMIT THY WAY TO THE LORD, AND TRUST IN HIM, AND HE WILL DO IT.**

**6. AND HE WILL BRING FORTH THY JUSTICE AS THE LIGHT, AND THY JUDGMENT AS THE NOONDAY.**

The Prophet, as a good physician, hath given to drink a medicine against the inward disease of hunger, thirst and the like evils: now he setteth forth another against the outward disease of persecutions and calumnies. For when these befall us, we are not forbidden to defend ourselves, and to repel calumnies, but the chief remedy ought to be prayer to God, and confidence in God: which Susanna observed, who, condemned to death by calumny, weeping looked up to heaven, saith the Scripture, *Dan.* xiii: "For her heart had confidence in the Lord." "Commit (*revela*, reveal), saith David, thy way to the Lord, and trust in him," that is, open in prayer before God thy works, and trust in the Lord, committing thy cause to him; "and he will do it," namely judgment and justice. He will find the way whereby the falsehood of the witnesses, who have borne witness against thee, may be laid bare, and whereby thy innocence may be made manifest. Which is had more clearly in the following verse: "And he will bring forth thy justice as the light," that is, God by his wonderful providence will bring it to pass that thy justice, that is, thy right, which was as it were buried in darkness by reason of the calumnies of persecutors, shall come forth and be most clearly seen, as light is seen when a candle is lighted, or when it is brought out of a closed and dark lantern. And repeating the same in other words, he addeth: "And thy judgment as the noonday," that is, he will bring forth into the open and the clear that same right of thine, that it may shine, and be seen of all, as the noonday sun is seen. Which indeed appeared manifestly in the judgment of Susanna. For as at the first the justice and innocence of Susanna was in darkness, when she was convicted by two witnesses, agreeing in their witness, and above all exception: so a little after, the providence of God stirring up the spirit of Daniel, and the falsehood of the witnesses being shewn by him out of their own mouth, the innocence of Susanna and the iniquity of the witnesses appeared so clearly, that straightway both she was delivered, and they, condemned by the people, perished by an evil death. As to the words, that word, *revela* (reveal), St. Jerome and the masters of the Jews translate from the Hebrew *volve* (roll), or *revolve* (roll back), so that the sense is: Cast thy cause upon God, according to that of *Ps.* liv: "Cast thy care upon the Lord." But *revela,* which we have from the version of the Seventy Interpreters, is not contrary to the Hebrew word גול *gol,* which signifieth *revolve;* for that also is rolled back which is cast upon another, and that also is rolled back which is opened and unfolded, that it may be seen: which cometh to the same as *revela,* or *aperi* (open). Either exposition is true, but we ought to follow the Latin edition and the commentaries of the Greek and Latin Fathers, who have expounded *revela,* not *rejice* (cast). Moreover when we are bidden to reveal anything to God, we are not therefore bidden because God is ignorant of anything; but because he willeth to be prayed to by us, and that what we desire be unfolded by prayer, that we ourselves may understand for what cause we need the divine help. Of the words *justitia* (justice) and *judicium* (judgment) it is known that they are taken variously in various places. But in this place it is certain that justice is taken for right; and judgment is either to be taken also

[Margin: Susanna a singular example of the providence of God towards his own.]

<!-- p. 232 (PDF 243) -->

for right, since the same thing is treated of; or for the just sentence which is due to right: for judgment is an act of justice.

*Daleth.* BE SUBJECT TO THE LORD, AND PRAY TO HIM.

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. ENVY NOT THE MAN WHO PROSPERETH IN HIS WAY; THE MAN WHO DOTH UNJUST THINGS.**

Those words, *subditus esto Domino, et ora eum* (be subject to the Lord, and pray to him), in the Greek and Latin edition belong to the verse above; but in the Hebrew copy they are the beginning of a verse, as manifestly appeareth from the fourth letter of the alphabet, which is found here. We, that we might shew the Psalm to be alphabetical, have set those words under the letter *daleth,* and yet have not joined to them the number of the seventh verse, lest we should change the division of verses which we have in the Latin edition, and according to which division the Psalms are sung in the Church. The sense of this sentence, which may be counted the fourth spiritual aphorism, is this: See that thou be always obedient to God at his beck, and pray to him continually, lest perchance, if thou see a man happy in this world, and at the same time unjust, his felicity tempt thee, and lead thee to injustice. For truly the felicity of men of iniquity is a great temptation; but it is easily overcome, when a man hath God always before the eyes of his heart, and cleaveth to him by obedience and prayer. For he who so cleaveth to God looketh as it were from on high upon the felicity of men of iniquity, and seeing it to be small and fleeting, easily contemneth and despiseth it. He saith therefore: "Be subject to the Lord, and pray to him," that is, yield to God a pure and simple obedience, and by prayer converse and speak often with him. St. Jerome translateth from the Hebrew, *tace Domino, et exspecta eum* (be silent to the Lord, and wait for him); but the sense is the same, which the Seventy Interpreters would unfold in more words. That therefore, *tace Domino,* which is in the Hebrew, signifieth: murmur not against God, though thou know not why he commandeth, or doth anything, but, as becometh a subject, obey in silence in all things. And that, *exspecta eum,* signifieth: ask in prayer, and wait patiently. For the Lord will give what thou askest, but when he shall judge it seasonable. "Envy not the man who prospereth in his way; the man who doth unjust things," that is, become not an emulator, and as it were a rival, of the man who prospereth in his way, that is, in his actions; of a man, I say, unjust and iniquitous, who prospereth by doing evil. St. Jerome translateth from the Hebrew, *qui facit quæ cogitat* (who doth what he thinketh), for that which we have from the Greek, *faciente injustitias* (who doth unjust things). But the word מזמות *mezimmoth,* although it cometh from the verb זמה *zamah,* which is *cogitare* (to think), yet signifieth also *scelera* (crimes), as the same St. Jerome interpreteth the same word in *Ps.* xxv, verse 12,[^4] and *Jer.* xi, verse 15.

[Margin: The felicity of the wicked is a great temptation.]

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. *He.* CEASE FROM ANGER, AND LEAVE RAGE; HAVE NO EMULATION TO DO EVIL.**

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. FOR THE EVILDOERS SHALL BE CUT OFF: BUT THEY THAT WAIT UPON THE LORD SHALL INHERIT THE LAND.**

This sentence is as it were a repetition and declaration of the first sentence; for in the whole Psalm the same thing is often repeated and inculcated, that it may become clearer, and cleave more to the memory. In the first verse he had said: "Envy not them that work iniquity;" he now saith the very same more clearly, when he saith: "Cease from anger, and leave rage," that is, when thou seest the wicked man flourish on the earth, be not angry, nor begin to rage, and to say: Why doth this man of iniquity prosper, where is the justice of God? where his providence? Likewise in the first verse he had said: "Have no emulation to do evil,"[^5] that is, by emulating evildoers become not a companion of evildoers; imitate not the unjust works of those whose felicity thou enviest, and he addeth: "For the evildoers shall be cut off," to declare what he had said before, "for they shall shortly wither away as grass." Afterwards he subjoineth: "But they that wait upon the Lord shall inherit the land," to repeat and declare what he had there said: "Trust in the Lord, and dwell in the land." For they wait upon the Lord (*sustinent Dominum*), who patiently expect his promises, and expect them secure; for the Lord is faithful and mighty, who hath promised. And therefore without any doubt, the evildoers, though they seem to flourish for a time, shall not flourish long, but shall be cut off from the earth, and thrust down into hell fire to eternal infelicity: but they who abstain from iniquity, and hope for their reward from the Lord, "shall inherit the land," that is, they shall hold the land with a stable possession, as their own inheritance which shall never be taken from them. For truly godly men, when they pass to God, lose not the possession of the earth, but gain heaven and earth, since it is said of them: "He shall place them over all his goods," *Matt.* xxiv.

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. *Vau.* FOR YET A LITTLE WHILE, AND THE WICKED SHALL NOT BE: AND THOU SHALT SEEK HIS PLACE, AND SHALT NOT FIND IT.**

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. BUT THE MEEK SHALL INHERIT THE LAND, AND SHALL DELIGHT IN ABUNDANCE OF PEACE.**

Because he had said: "The evildoers shall be cut off," he addeth that this shall be shortly. "For yet, saith he, a little while" of time shall pass, and "the wicked," who seemed happy, shall not appear on the earth; "and thou shalt seek his place among men, and shalt not find it," namely because he hath utterly perished, and as an unfruitful tree hath been plucked up by the roots. "But the meek," that is, they who have no indignation, nor are angry with God, when they see the wicked prosper, but patiently bear, and endure that which pleaseth God, "shall inherit the land," not only this wherein we are pilgrims, but also that of the living, which alone is properly land, that is, firm and stable; whence also the Lord saith, *Matt.* v: "Blessed are the meek:

<!-- p. 233 (PDF 244) -->

for they shall possess the land;" and because that land is called Jerusalem, that is, vision of peace, since all enemies are very far away from it, therefore "they shall delight in abundance of peace," that is, they shall enjoy much peace, because that peace shall be with a great multitude of them that inhabit that land, and shall last a long time, or rather for eternal time, and so they shall have the delight which peace is wont to bring with it.

[Margin: Verse 12.]

**12. *Zain.* THE SINNER SHALL WATCH THE JUST MAN: AND SHALL GNASH UPON HIM WITH HIS TEETH.**

[Margin: Verse 13.]

**13. BUT THE LORD SHALL LAUGH AT HIM: FOR HE FORESEETH THAT HIS DAY SHALL COME.**

The holy Prophet warneth the just man not greatly to fear the snares of men of iniquity, because God watcheth to keep him. "The sinner, saith he, shall watch the just man," that is, the sinner shall look heedfully upon all the actions of the just man, that he may find an entrance to destroy him. "And shall gnash upon him with his teeth," that is, like a hungry dog he shall desire his destruction, and for anger and indignation shall gnash with his teeth. "But the Lord shall laugh at him," that is, the Lord, who foreseeth all things, and in whose hand are all things, so that not a leaf falleth to the ground, but at his bidding, or permission, "shall laugh at him. For he foreseeth that his day shall come," that is, he shall laugh at him, because he seeth the day of his destruction to be at hand, and that he shall be snatched away before he can hurt the just man. For even if perchance God permit the just man to be slain by the sinner, yet the sinner slayeth his own soul before he slayeth the body of the just man; and because the death of the just is precious in the sight of the Lord, and is gain, not loss, but the death of sinners is very evil, and thrusteth the sinner down to everlasting death, therefore the sinner is always snatched away before he can hurt the just man. Wherefore he is deservedly to be laughed at, because while he procureth another's destruction, he seeth not his own destruction hanging over him.

[Margin: Verse 14.]

**14. *Chet.* THE WICKED HAVE DRAWN OUT THE SWORD: THEY HAVE BENT THEIR BOW.**

[Margin: Verse 15.]

**15. TO CAST DOWN THE POOR AND NEEDY, TO KILL THE UPRIGHT OF HEART.**

[Margin: Verse 16.]

**16. LET THEIR SWORD ENTER INTO THEIR OWN HEARTS, AND LET THEIR BOW BE BROKEN.**

Here the Prophet declareth what he had said more obscurely in the foregoing verses of the letter *zain.* For what he said there, *the sinner watcheth the just man,* he declareth here, saying: "The wicked have drawn out the sword: they have bent their bow," that is, sinners stand armed with drawn swords, and bent bows, watching when it may be time to slay the just man, namely that then they may cast down with the bow, and kill with the sword, the just man, who is poor and needy, and upright of heart. But God, who foreseeth all things from on high, bringeth it to pass that the sword is turned into their own hearts, and their bow is broken, and hurteth them alone, and so he shall laugh at them. It is to be observed in this place, that what is here said of sword and bow may be understood both of a bow and sword of iron, and of the sword and bow of the tongue. For sinners often slay the just, or at least endeavour to slay them, by calumnies and false witness, often also with a true sword and bow. Likewise it is to be noted that that word, *ut dejiciant* (to cast down), is rightly so set in the edition of Pope Sixtus V, whereas before there was had in the common copies, *ut decipiant* (to deceive): for, since in the Hebrew and Greek copy there is להפיל *lehappil, dejiciant,* and the ancient Fathers so read, and the joining with bow and sword requireth this, and lastly there were not wanting ancient Latin manuscript copies which had, *ut dejiciant,* it could easily be judged that by the error of copyists, through the likeness of the letters, *decipiant* had crept in for *dejiciant.* Again it is to be considered that the just man is in this place called *rectum corde* (upright of heart), or, as it is in the Hebrew, ישרי דרך, *rectum via* (upright of way), because the just man hath a heart conformable to the law of God, which is most right; and because the same law is the right way, by which we ought to walk, while we work; therefore the upright of heart is called also upright of way, because he never strayeth from the right way, which is the law of the Lord. Moreover it is to be noted in this place, that the just man is called poor and needy, either because all the just are poor in spirit; and though they sometimes abound in riches, they understand that those riches are not properly their own, since they are to render an account of them to God; or at least David speaketh not of all the just, but only of the poor and abject, who are oppressed by the mighty. Furthermore between *pauperem* (poor) and *inopem* (needy) there is this difference as to the Hebrew words, that עני, for which we have *pauperem,* signifieth *humilem, afflictum, mitem* (humble, afflicted, meek); אביון, for which word we have, *inopem,* signifieth properly *egenum* (in want), because he desireth all things, who lacketh all things. Lastly that imprecation, *let their sword enter into their own hearts,* is a prediction rather than an imprecation. Now the sword of the sinner, stretched out against the just man, entereth into the heart of the sinner, when the sinner, while he intendeth to hurt the just man, hurteth himself more: for while perchance he strippeth the just man of his cloak, he strippeth himself of faith and charity; and while he depriveth the just man of the life of the flesh, he depriveth himself of the grace of God, which is the life of the soul; and while by his calumnies he thrusteth the just man into prison, he casteth himself headlong into hell fire.

[Margin: The sinner, while he would hurt the just man, hurteth himself more.]

[Margin: Verse 17.]

**17. *Teth.* BETTER IS A LITTLE TO THE JUST, THAN THE GREAT RICHES OF THE WICKED.**

[Margin: Verse 18.]

**18. FOR THE ARMS OF THE WICKED SHALL BE BROKEN IN PIECES; BUT THE LORD STRENGTHENETH THE JUST.**

Lest the just who are poor should envy rich men of iniquity, and forsaking justice go over to the working of iniquity, David fortifieth them with these two verses: "Better, saith he, is a little to the just, than the great riches of the wicked," that is, a small substance profiteth the just man more than vast riches the sinner: wherefore a just man of slender means is happier than a wealthy sinner; and therefore justice

<!-- p. 234 (PDF 245) -->

with small wealth is more to be desired than great plenty with injustice.[^6] The reason is, because the just man is directed by God, that he may know how to use riches; he is not covetous, he is not prodigal, and therefore he is not in need, nor is he burdened with debt, nor doth he keep idle wealth, whereby he might either be lifted up to pride, or provoked to luxury. But the sinner is proud and prodigal, and knoweth not how to use riches; hence he is always in need, and always in debt, nor can he long preserve his estate. And this is what followeth: "For the arms of the wicked shall be broken in pieces; but the Lord strengtheneth the just;" that is, the power and strength of the sinner shall easily fail, because he leaneth on an arm of flesh, nor shall riches be able to help him. But the strength and power of the just man cannot fail, because he leaneth on the arm of God; God also, who is the friend of the just, strengtheneth him, or, as the Hebrew word סומך expresseth the thing more clearly, *sustentat* (upholdeth). Lastly the sinner, notwithstanding his very great riches, shall not escape eternal destruction, because when he shall die he shall carry nothing away with him, nor shall his glory descend with him. But the just man, who trusteth not in earthly riches, but in God, shall live for ever.

[Margin: Verse 19.]

**19. *Iod.* THE LORD KNOWETH THE DAYS OF THE UNDEFILED; AND THEIR INHERITANCE SHALL BE FOR EVER.**

[Margin: Verse 20.]

**20. THEY SHALL NOT BE CONFOUNDED IN THE EVIL TIME; AND IN THE DAYS OF FAMINE THEY SHALL BE FILLED.**

The Prophet confirmeth what he said a little before of the felicity of the just, even though they have small wealth: "The Lord, saith he, knoweth the days of the undefiled," that is, the Lord approveth the life of the just, and favoureth and blesseth them, and therefore their days shall be long, and their inheritance shall be preserved with them a very long time. "They shall not be confounded in the evil time," that is, in the time of barrenness and dearth they shall not be confounded, because they shall not be forced to blush in begging bread; "and in the days of famine they shall be filled," that is, not only shall they not blush in begging in the time of common famine, but they shall also have of their own store whereof to eat to the full. Now these things are sometimes fulfilled in this world, but shall always be fulfilled in the world to come. For there is to come after this life a certain incredible barrenness, when no man shall be able to sow, or to reap, and the rich man in hell shall desire a drop of water, and shall not obtain it. Then therefore the undefiled, who have left nothing on earth, but have carried all things over into heaven, shall find an eternal inheritance, nor shall they be confounded in begging with the foolish virgins: "Give us of your oil," *Matt.* xxv; they shall be satisfied, when the glory of the Lord shall appear.

*Caph.* BECAUSE THE WICKED SHALL PERISH.

[Margin: Verse 21.]

**21. AND THE ENEMIES OF THE LORD, PRESENTLY AFTER THEY SHALL BE HONOURED AND EXALTED, SHALL COME TO NOTHING AND VANISH LIKE SMOKE.**

The three first words belong to the verse above in the Greek and Latin edition; but we have chosen to set them in this place, that the art of the Prophet may appear, who disposed the Psalm by the letters of the alphabet. For in that particle, *quia* (because), he set the letter *caph,* which followeth in the alphabet; and thence beginneth in the Hebrew a new verse. Therefore those words: "Because the wicked shall perish," give the reason why the undefiled shall peaceably possess their inheritance in the land, and in the time of famine shall be filled. For that shall be, because "the wicked," who were wont to trouble them, and to plunder their goods, "shall perish." And that which followeth: "And the enemies of the Lord, presently after they shall be honoured and exalted, shall come to nothing and vanish like smoke," answereth to the two verses above, so that this is the sense: Undefiled men, as friends of God, shall have an eternal inheritance, and in the evil time shall not be confounded; "and the enemies of the Lord," such as are all sinners, on the contrary shall have a most short felicity; for as soon as they begin to be exalted, they shall vanish like smoke, which while it is lifted up is consumed, nor leaveth any trace behind it. That word, *exaltati* (exalted), is read by St. Jerome, *sicut monocerotes* (as unicorns); by others, *agni* (lambs), which are things most diverse. In the Hebrew there is now כרים *carim,* which properly signifieth *agnos* (lambs), and maketh nothing to the meaning of this place. St. Jerome seemeth to have read *carem,* and therefore he translated, *sicut monocerotes;* for the unicorn in the book of *Num.* chap. xxiii and xxiv, is called ראם *reem,* and in the book of *Judges,* chap. xxxviii,[^7] is called רים *rem,* and then the sense is, that *the enemies of the Lord, when they shall be honoured,* and glorying *as unicorns, shall be consumed as smoke.* But the Seventy Interpreters read not כרים *carim,* nor *carem,* but *carum,* and therefore rightly translated, *cum exaltati fuerint* (when they shall be exalted), which reading is the best, and our Interpreter also hath followed it, and the whole Church useth it.

[Margin: Verse 22.]

**22. *Lamed.* THE SINNER SHALL BORROW, AND NOT PAY AGAIN; BUT THE JUST SHEWETH MERCY AND SHALL GIVE.**

[Margin: Verse 23.]

**23. FOR SUCH AS BLESS HIM SHALL INHERIT THE LAND: BUT SUCH AS CURSE HIM SHALL PERISH.**

He confirmeth what he had said above: "Better is a little to the just, than the great riches of the wicked." For it very often happeneth that the sinner, though most rich, borroweth money, and yet restoreth it not; because sinners will live sumptuously above their means, or be proudly clothed, or housed: hence they are always in distress, burdened with debt; but the just man, who, as we said above, knoweth how to use riches, though he have but a little, yet out of that little hath wherewith to shew mercy to the poor, and to give without hope of recovering. "For such as bless him," namely God, as Augustine expoundeth, "shall inherit the land," and so shall always have whereof to give; "but such as curse him," as blasphemers and ungrateful, "shall perish," so that though they would give, they cannot. St. Jerome readeth passively, *quia benedicti ab eo,* namely by God,

[Margin: Why have the poor less often than the rich?]

<!-- p. 235 (PDF 246) -->

*hæreditabunt terram, maledicti ab eo disperibunt* (for the blessed of him shall inherit the land, the cursed of him shall perish). And indeed the Hebrew words, a small change of the points being made, can be expounded as well actively as passively, and the one meaning inferreth the other. For they who bless God in all things shall also be blessed by him; and they who curse God shall obtain his curse.

[Margin: Verse 24.]

**24. *Mem.* WITH THE LORD SHALL THE STEPS OF A MAN BE DIRECTED, AND HE SHALL LIKE WELL HIS WAY.**

[Margin: Verse 25.]

**25. WHEN HE SHALL FALL HE SHALL NOT BE BRUISED, FOR THE LORD PUTTETH HIS HAND UNDER HIM.**

In this place the Prophet beginneth to set forth the singular providence of God towards the just, that he may so much the more strengthen them, lest perchance the prosperity of the wicked allure them to sin. He saith therefore that the life of the just is directed and kept by the Lord. "With the Lord, saith he, the steps of a" just "man," that is, he shall direct the actions and works of the just man, who made him just. "And he shall like well (*volet*, shall will) his way," that is, hence it shall come that the just man willeth, loveth, and gladly followeth the way of the Lord: or, *God shall like well the way of the just man,* that is, he shall approve his actions and works. The former explanation is St. Augustine's, the latter Euthymius's. That, *apud Dominum* (with the Lord), is in the original text מיהוה, *a Domino* (from the Lord), and so read St. Augustine and the Greek Interpreters; but the words of our edition, that they may not disagree with the original text, are thus to be explained: "With the Lord," that is, in the secret counsel of the Lord "shall be directed." It followeth: "When he shall fall he shall not be bruised, for the Lord putteth his hand under him." This may be referred to bodily miseries, and also to the miseries of the soul. For if the just man fall into any affliction and tribulation, which are as it were holes, or stones, or mire in the way of this life, "he shall not be bruised," that is, he shall not wholly sink under it, or be overwhelmed, "for the Lord putteth his hand under him," that he be not hurt, or, as it is in the Hebrew, *because God upholdeth him with his hand,* that is, with his help and providence. And if perchance he fall into a temptation of sin, he shall not be bruised, that is, he shall not come to the consent of mortal fault, nor lose patience, or faith, or other virtue, because the Lord shall uphold him with the help of his grace.

[Margin: Verse 26.]

**26. *Nun.* I HAVE BEEN YOUNG, AND NOW AM OLD; AND I HAVE NOT SEEN THE JUST FORSAKEN, NOR HIS SEED SEEKING BREAD.**

[Margin: Verse 27.]

**27. HE SHEWETH MERCY, AND LENDETH ALL THE DAY LONG; AND HIS SEED SHALL BE IN BLESSING.**

He proveth from his own experience what he had said above, that the just shall not be confounded in the evil time, and in the days of famine shall be filled. "I have been young," saith he, or, as it is had more expressly in the Hebrew נערי היתי, *puer fui* (I have been a boy), and afterwards I grew old, and in so long a time "I have never seen the just forsaken by God," so as to be pressed with want; nor have I seen his seed, that is his children, seeking and begging bread. But rather on the contrary I have seen that the just man "sheweth mercy, and lendeth all the day long," that is, at all times he so aboundeth, that either he giveth out of mercy, or at least giveth to his neighbours of his means as a loan, whether of a thing to be repaid or of a thing to be used and returned. Wherefore "his seed," that is, his children, not only shall not beg bread, but "shall be in blessing," that is, blessed by God they shall abound in plenty, or shall be blessed by all, as children of the best of parents. Observe concerning the words, that that *etenim* (for indeed) is in the Hebrew גם *gam,* which signifieth *etiam* (also), so that the sense is, *puer fui, et etiam senui* (I have been a boy, and also am grown old). In the Greek indeed there is ναὶ γάρ, *etenim;* but it may be taken for *etiam.* Observe moreover that this sentence is not contrary to the truth, even though we see in the Church many of the faithful begging bread. For mendicant religious beg of their own will for the love of poverty, not from the necessity of want, nor can they be called forsaken, since God by his wonderful providence feedeth and sustaineth them. Other beggars for the most part are not children of those who have been wont to shew mercy to others, and to give: for to these this promise properly belongeth. Often also neither are they just themselves, nor children of the just. Lastly, as we said above, if any who are truly just, and hoping in the Lord, seem forsaken, and seeking crumbs of bread, as Lazarus of the Gospel, *Luke* xvi, these have received from the Lord something better than is plenty of temporal things: nor would they change the merit of patience for all temporal felicity.

[Margin: Mendicant religious are not contrary to this sentence.]

[Margin: Verse 28.]

**28. *Samech.* DECLINE FROM EVIL AND DO GOOD, AND DWELL FOR EVER AND EVER.**

[Margin: Verse 29.]

**29. FOR THE LORD LOVETH JUDGMENT, AND WILL NOT FORSAKE HIS SAINTS: THEY SHALL BE PRESERVED FOR EVER.**

From those things which he said a little before, that he had found by experience from boyhood even to old age, he concludeth by way of exhortation, and saith: "Decline from evil and do good," which are the two first precepts of justice; "and dwell for ever," that is, see that thou be just, and thou shalt dwell secure in the land even for evermore. And he giveth the reason why the just shall dwell in the land perpetually. "For, saith he, the Lord loveth judgment," that is, his just servants;[^8] therefore I affirm that "they shall be preserved for ever." This promise pertaineth indeed in some manner to this life, in which through divers successions the just are wont to dwell in the land a long time: but absolutely and properly it pertaineth to the life to come, which in the land of the living shall be everlasting.

[Margin: Verse 30.]

**30. *Ain.* THE UNJUST SHALL BE PUNISHED, AND THE SEED OF THE WICKED SHALL PERISH.**

[Margin: Verse 31.]

**31. BUT THE JUST SHALL INHERIT THE LAND, AND SHALL DWELL THEREIN FOR EVERMORE.**

In this place the two first words are wanting in the Hebrew, namely, *injusti punientur* (the unjust shall be punished), which are most plainly read in the Greek text: and hence it hath come that

<!-- p. 236 (PDF 247) -->

the letter *ain* doth not appear at the beginning of these two verses, as the number and order of the letters of the alphabet required. But the word *injusti,* which is with the Greeks and Latins, would be with the Hebrews יעוילים,[^9] which beginneth with *ain.* Therefore if "The unjust shall be punished" be added to the Hebrew text, the order of the alphabet will stand in the Hebrew words. And the meaning of these verses is most clear, so that they need no explanation.

[Margin: Verse 32.]

**32. *Phe.* THE MOUTH OF THE JUST SHALL MEDITATE WISDOM: AND HIS TONGUE SHALL SPEAK JUDGMENT.**

[Margin: Verse 33.]

**33. THE LAW OF HIS GOD IS IN HIS HEART, AND HIS STEPS SHALL NOT BE SUPPLANTED.**

He said a little before that the divine providence will watch, lest the just be oppressed by the wicked: now he addeth that the just also by their own wisdom, which is likewise the gift of God, will bring it to pass that they be not supplanted by the malice of men of iniquity. "The mouth of the just shall meditate wisdom," that is, the just man shall speak wisely, so that he cannot be caught in his speech: for to meditate wisdom with the mouth is nothing else but to exercise wisdom in speaking, as we have said elsewhere; and David repeateth the same when he addeth: "And his tongue shall speak judgment," that is, the tongue of the just man shall not pour forth words rashly, but shall speak what is just, and what ought to be said according to the time, which is to speak wisely. He addeth the reason, saying: "The law of his God is in his heart," that is, the speech of the just man shall be seasoned with the salt of wisdom, because the just man hath the law of God in his heart, and therefore while he speaketh, he thinketh on the commandments of God, that he sin not with his tongue; and therefore "the law is a light," as Solomon saith, *Prov.* vi; and, as the same David saith, *Ps.* xviii: "The law of the Lord enlighteneth hearts, and giveth wisdom to little ones;"[^10] and therefore it is no wonder, if the just man, who hath the law in his heart, and thinketh on it and loveth it, speak wisely. From these the Prophet gathereth: "And his steps shall not be supplanted," that is, because the just man always speaketh,[^11] no man by supplanting shall drive him to a fall. He is said to supplant, who by holding or hindering the sole (*planta*) of another's foot, forceth him to fall, especially when that is done by craft rather than by violence. Therefore his feet shall not be supplanted, and by this the just man shall not be driven to a fall, circumvented by the guile of any man of iniquity.

[Margin: What it is to meditate wisdom with the mouth.]

[Margin: Verse 34.]

**34. *Zade.* THE WICKED WATCHETH THE JUST MAN, AND SEEKETH TO PUT HIM TO DEATH.**

[Margin: Verse 35.]

**35. BUT THE LORD WILL NOT LEAVE HIM IN HIS HANDS; NOR CONDEMN HIM WHEN HE SHALL BE JUDGED.**

These two verses declare the two verses above. "The wicked, saith he, watcheth the just man," namely observing diligently what he speaketh, and what he doth, that he may supplant him; "and seeketh to put him to death," that is, seeketh by supplanting to slay him. Which indeed is chiefly wont to be done in judgments, when an iniquitous judge, or accuser, desireth to circumvent and destroy some innocent man. "But the Lord will not leave him in his hands," that is, the Lord will not permit the sinner to have the just man in his power, and therefore he will inspire him with wisdom, that he may know the frauds of his enemies, and by speaking wisely avoid their supplantings. "Nor condemn him when he shall be judged," that is, the judge shall not condemn the just man, when the time of his judgment shall come, because God will not permit justice to be obscured. Those words, *cum judicabitur illi* (when he shall be judged), are clearer in the original text, בהשפטו *behiscapto,* and signify nothing else but *cum ille,* namely the just man, *judicandus erit* (when he is to be judged); or, *cum fiet ei judicium* (when judgment shall be done upon him); or, *cum judicabitur* (when he shall be judged). Almost the same words are had in verse 12: "The sinner shall watch the just man: and shall gnash with his teeth upon him," etc.; but there violence is treated of, here craft.

[Margin: Verse 36.]

**36. *Coph.* EXPECT THE LORD AND KEEP HIS WAY: AND HE WILL EXALT THEE TO INHERIT THE LAND: WHEN THE SINNERS SHALL PERISH THOU SHALT SEE.**

The Prophet exhorteth the just man to hope in God, and to persevere in justice. "Expect the Lord," that is, hope in God, if perchance his promise seem to thee to be deferred; "and keep his way," that is, observe his law, and decline not from the way of holiness and justice already begun, "to inherit the land," that is, that when his promise shall be fulfilled, thou mayest possess the land of the living as thy own inheritance. "When the sinners shall perish thou shalt see," that is, when all sinners, condemned by the divine judgment, shall have perished, then thou shalt see what now thou hopest.

[Margin: Verse 37.]

**37. *Resch.* I HAVE SEEN THE WICKED HIGHLY EXALTED, AND LIFTED UP LIKE THE CEDARS OF LIBANUS.**

[Margin: Verse 38.]

**38. AND I PASSED BY, AND LO, HE WAS NOT: AND I SOUGHT HIM AND HIS PLACE WAS NOT FOUND.**

Because he had said: "When the sinners shall perish thou shalt see," the just man might ask when this shall be; therefore he now addeth that this shall be shortly. For "I have seen, saith he, the wicked highly exalted, and lifted up like the cedars of Libanus," that is, set in the highest degree of dignity and power, and rooted and founded in wealth, dependants, and the like things, so that his felicity seemed like to be eternal: and yet scarce "had I passed by, and lo, he was not," that is, passing I saw that man lifted up and rooted, as the cedars of Libanus, and scarce had I passed him by, and lo, I look behind me, and he had vanished; "I sought" where he had been, and whether traces remained of so great greatness, "and his place was not found," as though he had never been there. That these things are most true daily experience teacheth. For where are now those most mighty monarchs, to say nothing of lesser ones, of the Assyrians, Persians, Greeks and Romans? Surely unless we read something in the books of historians, we should not know that they had ever been. So no traces remain of so great

<!-- p. 237 (PDF 248) -->

power: and yet human pride so dazzleth the eyes of mortals, that they cannot see what they touch with their hands, and will not believe what even against their will they are forced to feel and experience. As to the words, in the Hebrew there is, *vidi impium robustum, fortissimum, sicut indigenam virentem* (I have seen the wicked strong, most mighty, as a native that is green). But the Seventy Interpreters, looking to the similitude of the cedar, that they might explain the thing more, said, *superexaltatum, et elevatum* (highly exalted, and lifted up); from which nevertheless it followeth that he was strong and most mighty: for cedars are most high, and strong, and most mighty. And for that, *sicut indigenam virentem,* the Seventy translated, *sicut cedros Libani* (like the cedars of Libanus), because they read not, as we now have, כאזרח, *sicut indigenam* (as a native), but with one letter transposed, כארזח, *sicut cedros* (like cedars); and because green cedars are found chiefly on mount Libanus, therefore for *cedros virentes* (green cedars), they put *cedros Libani.* Moreover where we read, *et transivi* (and I passed by), in the Hebrew is ויעבר, *et transivit* (and he passed by); but it is credible that the Hebrew word hath been corrupted by the carelessness of copyists by the change of one letter. For not only the Seventy Interpreters translated in the first person, but also St. Jerome.

[Margin: Human pride seeth not the things which can be touched.]

[Margin: Verse 39.]

**39. *Schin.* KEEP INNOCENCE, AND BEHOLD JUSTICE: FOR THERE ARE REMNANTS FOR THE PEACEABLE MAN.**

[Margin: Verse 40.]

**40. BUT THE UNJUST SHALL BE DESTROYED TOGETHER: THE REMNANTS OF THE WICKED SHALL PERISH.**

The Prophet goeth on in his exhortation, and saith: "Keep innocence, and behold justice (*æquitatem*, equity)," that is, preserve innocence towards thyself, "and behold justice," that is, judge what is right towards thy neighbours. "For there are remnants for the peaceable man," that is, because God will give for a reward to the peaceable man, that he leave children after him. Or in a higher sense, because many goods are left for the peaceable man after his death. "For good works follow them who die in the Lord," as is said *Apoc.* xiv.[^12] "But the unjust" on the contrary "shall perish," themselves all together, none excepted, "and the remnants shall perish with them," because they shall neither leave children in this world, nor shall any other good be left to them, since they have consumed all things in their concupiscences and crimes.

[Margin: Verse 41.]

**41. *Thau.* BUT THE SALVATION OF THE JUST IS FROM THE LORD, AND HE IS THEIR PROTECTOR IN THE TIME OF TROUBLE.**

[Margin: Verse 42.]

**42. AND THE LORD WILL HELP THEM AND DELIVER THEM: AND HE WILL RESCUE THEM FROM THE WICKED, AND SAVE THEM, BECAUSE THEY HAVE HOPED IN HIM.**

This is as it were a recapitulation of the whole Psalm, and is sufficiently open and plain.
