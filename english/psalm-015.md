BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM XV.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*The inscription of a title to David himself.*[^1]

This Psalm is most obscure, and its obscurity beginneth from the title itself. The Hebrew word מכתם *micltham* is unknown. St. Jerome rendered it, *of the humble and simple* David, perhaps because מך *mac* signifieth *poor*, and thereby *humble*, and תם *tham* signifieth *simple and perfect*; others will have מכתם *micltham* to be something *precious*, because חתם *chetem* signifieth *gold*. To me it is probable that the Seventy Interpreters did not read מכתם *micltham* but מכתב *miclhab*, the last letter being a little changed; for from the verb *cathab*, *to write*, is made the noun *micthab*, *a writing*, or *an inscription*. But because the Greek word στηλογραφία doth not signify a simple inscription, but an inscription cut upon a pillar for an everlasting memorial, therefore we ought to understand that the inscription in this place signifieth a memorable title, worthy never to be blotted out. The argument of the Psalm is a prayer of Christ to the Father: and the same can be applied to the members of Christ, both all together and each one. For that the last four verses, and especially those words: "Thou wilt not give thy holy one to see corruption," are to be understood of Christ, and not of David, the Apostles teach, Peter, *Acts* ii, and Paul, *Acts* xii;[^2] and because the whole Psalm is one continued prayer, the last four verses cannot be understood of Christ, without the whole Psalm being understood of Christ.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. PRESERVE ME, O LORD, FOR I HAVE PUT MY TRUST IN THEE. I HAVE SAID TO THE LORD, THOU ART MY GOD, FOR THOU HAST NO NEED OF MY GOODS.**

In the Hebrew, as it is now read, there is: *Dixisti* (Thou hast said); but there is no doubt that it ought to be read אמרתי *amarthi*, not *amarth*: for not only did the Seventy render *dixi* (I have said), but St. Jerome also; yet if both readings ought to be defended, that word, *dixisti*, will have to be referred to the soul, because אמרת *amarth* is the second person of the feminine gender, and the sense will be: *Thou hast said, O my soul!* and it is the same thing, *I have said*, or *thou hast said, my soul*. Christ therefore saith, and any Christian whatsoever can say: "Preserve me, O Lord," that is, keep me, protect me from the tribulation that hangeth over me, for I have put my trust in thee alone, not in any created thing. And that I have put, and do put, my trust in thee alone, can be known from this: "For

<!-- p. 77 (PDF 88) -->

I have said to the Lord," that is, I have confessed to thee the Lord, and have said from the heart: "Thou art my God," that is, thou art my supreme Lord, as the Hebrew and Greek text hath it more clearly; for the Latin put *Deus meus* (my God) for *Dominus meus* (my Lord), to vary the speech: "for thou hast no need of my goods," but I have need of thy goods; thou in no way dependest on me, but I depend on thee in all things: therefore thou alone art my true and supreme Lord; and because I know and confess this, therefore in thee alone I hope and trust. These words the Prophet wrote in the person of Christ for that time in which Christ was not only man, but also subject to suffering and death. For although in Christ, as man and passible, there was not hope, which is a theological virtue, and regardeth the beatitude of the soul, as St. Thomas teacheth, Part III, *Quest.* vii, art. 4, yet there was hope as it signifieth confidence, and the expectation of the glory of the body, and of other things which he had not yet: and of this hope David speaketh in the Psalms.

[Margin: In Christ there was not hope, but confidence, and the expectation of the glory of his body.]

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. TO THE SAINTS, WHO ARE IN HIS LAND, HE HATH MADE WONDERFUL ALL MY DESIRES IN THEM.**

In the Greek it is read thus: *Sanctis, qui sunt in terra ejus, mirificavit Dominus omnes voluntates suas in eis* (To the saints, who are in his land, the Lord hath made wonderful all his desires in them). Thus the Greek and the Latin disagree in that word, *meas* (my) and *suas* (his). For that addition of the word *Dominus* (the Lord) doth not change the sense. In the Hebrew it is read word for word thus: *Sanctis qui in terra ipsi, et magnificis omnis voluntas mea in eis* (To the saints who are in the land, they, and to the magnificent, all my desire in them). As to the Greek, St. Augustine in Epistle 59 *to Paulinus*, Quest. 29, saith that the choicer copies have *meas*, although others have *suas*. Therefore it is probable that in the Greek a fault hath crept in by the error of the copyists, especially since in the Hebrew also there is *meas*. The same Augustine also saith that it is not a disagreement of any moment, since the works of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost are undivided. Now the Hebrew copy, when it saith: *Qui in terra ipsi* (Who are in the land, they), doth not differ from our copy, save that it omitteth the pronoun *ejus* (his). But when it saith: *Et magnificis* (And to the magnificent), it must be known that the word ואדירי *veaddire* seemeth altogether corrupt. For it is a noun of the plural number, in the construct state, that is, governing another noun of the Hebrew genitive case: for they form in one way a noun put absolutely, and in another a noun governing after it another noun of the genitive case: but here there followeth no other noun to be governed by this one, *magnificis*; besides, if the Seventy had read ואדירי *veaddire*, they could not have rendered *mirificavit*,[^3] unless they had been most unskilful. Therefore I think that they read האדיר *heedir*, which signifieth *he hath made wonderful*. But those words, *omnis voluntas mea in eis* (all my desire in them), have no difficulty, because, since the Hebrews do not decline nouns by cases, the words ought to be rendered according to the requirement of the verbs. This place therefore can rightly be rendered: *Sanctis qui sunt in terra, mirificavit omnem voluntatem meam in eis* (To the saints who are in the land, he hath made wonderful all my desire in them), and so all the readings are brought into agreement. Now the meaning seemeth to be this: Because God hath no need of my goods, therefore I study to do good to his elect: of which good will of mine God himself is my witness, who to the saints who are in his land, that is, to his saints and elect who are in his land, hath made wonderful, that is, hath rendered marvellous, all my desires in them, that is, all my good will and all my zeal toward them. Now God is said to have rendered marvellous the good will of Christ toward the elect, by declaring it both by the Prophets, and by the various figures of the Old Testament, and also by the miracles of Christ himself and of the Apostles; and marvellous indeed was the good will of Christ toward the elect, since he willed to lay down his life for them.

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. THEIR INFIRMITIES WERE MULTIPLIED: AFTERWARDS THEY MADE HASTE.**

In the Hebrew there is ירבו *irbu* in the future, *multiplicabuntur* (shall be multiplied), but the future is taken for the past. That word also, *postea* (afterwards), is commonly rendered from the Hebrew, *alienum* (another); but the Seventy did not read אחר *acher*, *another*, but אחר *achar*, which signifieth *afterwards*. In this place is explained the effect of the good will of Christ upon the elect, who before were weak by reason of the most grievous wounds of sins, so that they could not walk, and afterwards, being healed by grace, began to run the way of the commandments of God: "Their infirmities," saith he, "were multiplied," that is, their sicknesses, and spiritual diseases; whence the Apostle, *Rom.* v: "When as yet we were weak (*infirmi*), Christ died for us";[^4] and a little after, explaining the infirmities: "When," saith he, "we were sinners." From the Hebrew עצבותם *hatsbotham* many render *idols*; but the Hebrew word properly signifieth *infirmity* and *sorrow*; and by a figure it is taken for *idols*, either because idols are weak, and of no power; or because they make men sinners, and thereby weak. "Afterwards they made haste," that is, before they were most weak, their sins being multiplied to the utmost, but afterwards, being healed by grace, they were made strong, and therefore they rejoiced to run the way. This appeared in the beginning of the Church, when men converted from idolatry hastened to martyrdoms.

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. I WILL NOT GATHER TOGETHER THEIR MEETINGS FOR BLOOD OFFERINGS: NOR WILL I BE MINDFUL OF THEIR NAMES BY MY LIPS.**

From the Hebrew they commonly render: *Non libabo libamina eorum de sanguine* (I will not pour out their libations of blood),[^5] and truly the verb נסך *nasac* signifieth *to make a libation* and *to pour out*; but it signifieth also *to cover* and *to hide*; and because things which are covered are gathered together, that they may be able to be covered, it is credible that the same verb signifieth also *to gather together*; but the meaning is the same, for because assemblies are wont to be gathered for the celebrating of sacrifices, therefore it is all one, whether it be said: *Non libabo libamina eorum de sanguine* (I will not pour out their libations of blood), that is, their sacrifices, which consist in the shedding of blood, shall not please me; or: *Non congregabo conventicula eorum de sanguinibus* (I will not gather together their meetings for blood offerings), that is, their assemblies

<!-- p. 78 (PDF 89) -->

for celebrating bloody sacrifices shall not please me, therefore I will not gather them together, I will not be the author that such assemblies be made. "Nor will I be mindful of their names by my lips," that is, not only will I not be the author of such assemblies and sacrifices, but I will not so much as name them. Now this verse cohereth with the one above, because it giveth the reason why the elect, after their sins were multiplied, and chiefly those of idolatry, made haste to run the way of the Lord; for the reason is, that the Lord vehemently detesteth idols, and the veneration of them, so that he would not have them so much as named by his own: therefore those whom he chose in Christ he cleansed from the sin of idolatry; and so he made the saints to run the way of his commandments.

[Margin: God greatly detesteth idols and the veneration of them.]

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. THE LORD IS THE PORTION OF MY INHERITANCE AND OF MY CUP: IT IS THOU THAT WILT RESTORE MY INHERITANCE TO ME.**

Because he had said that he detesteth idols and sins, he addeth for a reason, that he hath all his goods in the one God. It is the proper voice of Christ, who, being altogether separated from sinners, and in the highest degree the friend of God the Father, hath all his goods in God. The same we also ought to feel according to our small measure. It is said therefore: "The Lord is the portion of my inheritance," that is, the portion which ought to fall to me from the division of the inheritance, that is, whatsoever at all I look for, the Lord is that to me, he is all things to me; "and of my cup," is a repetition of the same sentence: for the cup, which was wont to be divided among the guests, is taken for the inheritance. Unless it please better to understand by the portion of the inheritance riches, by the portion of the cup delights, so that the sense is, that I have placed all my riches and all my delights in God alone. "It is thou that wilt restore my inheritance to me": this is added, because when Christ, being yet mortal, said these things, he did not yet fully possess the inheritance; and we, when we say these things, have indeed all goods in God by desire, but do not yet possess them in very deed. Now this possession is in the meantime laid up with God, and he will restore it to us at the last day, as he restored it to Christ on the day of his resurrection. To this Blessed Paul alludeth when he saith: "I know whom I have believed, and I am certain that he is able to keep that which I have committed unto him, against that day," II *Tim.* i. In the Hebrew there is אתה תומיך גורלי *attha thomic gorali*, *tu sustentas hæreditatem meam* (thou upholdest my inheritance), and the sense is the same; for the Lord will restore the inheritance for this reason, that he now upholdeth it, and keepeth it for us, according to that word of St. Peter: "Unto an inheritance incorruptible, reserved in heaven," I *Pet.* i.

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. THE LINES ARE FALLEN UNTO ME IN GOODLY PLACES: FOR MY INHERITANCE IS GOODLY TO ME.**

By a similitude drawn from an earthly inheritance, he declareth the excellence of the eternal inheritance, which they shall have who choose God alone. For the fields which came to a man from the division of an inheritance were wont to be measured with lines, and because the division was made by lot, therefore the lines were said to fall in a goodly part, when the best part of the inheritance fell to a man. "The lines are fallen unto me in goodly places," that is, I have obtained the best inheritance by a most happy lot; for "my inheritance is goodly to me," he saith the same in other words. Now he maketh mention of the lot, because the beginning of this inheritance is drawn from predestination; and the grace of predestination, in respect of us, is a kind of lot, whence St. Paul: "We are called by lot"; and: "He hath chosen us to be partakers of the lot of the saints,"[^6] Epistle *to the Ephesians* ii,[^7] and *to the Colossians* i.

[Margin: Our predestination, in respect of us, is a lot.]

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. I WILL BLESS THE LORD, WHO HATH GIVEN ME UNDERSTANDING: MOREOVER MY REINS ALSO HAVE CORRECTED ME EVEN TILL NIGHT.**

He giveth thanks to God, who inspired him with the counsel, and kindled in him the desire, to choose so goodly an inheritance: "I will bless the Lord," saith he, that is, I will praise God, the author to me of so great a good, "who hath given me understanding," that is, who hath made me to know, and prudently to choose; for the Hebrew word יעץ *iahats* properly signifieth *to give counsel*. "Moreover my reins also have corrected me even till night." By the *reins* in Scripture is understood affection and desire, whence are those words: "The searcher of hearts and reins is God"; and: "Prove my heart and my reins,"[^8] where by the heart is understood thought, and by the reins affection. By the *night* is understood the time of tribulation; as by the *day*, the time of prosperity. By the word *increpuerunt* (they have rebuked)[^9] is signified rather *instruction* than *reproof*. For both in the Hebrew, יסרוני *isseruni*, and in the Greek there is a word signifying *instruction*; therefore the Latin word is to be interpreted of *a rebuke that exhorteth*, such as is: "Why art thou sad, O my soul? and why dost thou disquiet me? Hope in God, for I will give praise to him," *Ps.* xlii. Lastly, that phrase, *usque ad noctem* (even till night), is in the Hebrew לילות *leloth*, they instructed me *in the nights*, from which we understand that there is signified not only the whole day, but also even to the whole night, which is commonly said *inclusively*. And so the sense will be this: "Moreover also even till night," that is, not only in the time of prosperity, but besides even to the time of adversity, and in adversity itself, my affection, kindled toward God, instructed me, stirring up and exhorting me to undergo the passion with an even mind, and always to hope well of God.

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. I SET THE LORD ALWAYS IN MY SIGHT: FOR HE IS AT MY RIGHT HAND, THAT I BE NOT MOVED.**

From the understanding, and the good affection, for which in the verse above he praised God, it came that he had God always before his eyes; for the soul is more where it loveth, than where it giveth life. In the Hebrew there is: *Posui Dominum in conspectu meo semper* (I have set the Lord always in my sight),[^10] from which it is more clearly declared that Christ took pains to have the Lord always present.

[Margin: The soul is more where it loveth, than where it giveth life.]

<!-- p. 79 (PDF 90) -->

"For he is at my right hand, that I be not moved," that is, nor was that thought of mine false, by which I always set the Lord before the eyes of my heart; for truly he is always at my right hand, as covering my side, and going before me, as a strong helper, that I be not moved from my place, but may stand fast, and persevere unto the end.

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. THEREFORE MY HEART HATH BEEN GLAD, AND MY TONGUE HATH REJOICED: MOREOVER MY FLESH ALSO SHALL REST IN HOPE.**

He explaineth now at length the goodly inheritance which God will restore to him, and to others who have chosen the Lord. "Therefore," that is, because the Lord is at my right hand, a most faithful helper and protector, "my heart hath been glad," with a true and solid gladness, of which the Lord in the Gospel, *John* xvi, 22: "Your heart shall rejoice; and your joy no man shall take from you." "And my tongue hath rejoiced," because eternal joy is wont to overflow outwardly also. In the Hebrew there is: *Exultavit gloria mea* (My glory hath rejoiced), which cometh to the same thing; for the inward glory rejoiceth outwardly by the tongue. "Moreover my flesh also shall rest in hope," that is, the mind shall be glad, and the flesh shall fall asleep in a death peaceful and secure, on account of the certain expectation of a most speedy resurrection.

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. BECAUSE THOU WILT NOT LEAVE MY SOUL IN HELL; NOR WILT THOU GIVE THY HOLY ONE TO SEE CORRUPTION.**

These things have been explained by the Apostles Peter and Paul, *Acts* ii and xiii, and although these things are properly said of Christ alone, whose soul was not left in hell, namely in the limbo of the holy Fathers, nor did his flesh in the sepulchre experience any rottenness; yet we all can say these things, both because we are members of Christ, and in him, as the Apostle saith, *Ephes.* ii, God "hath raised us up together"; and because our souls shall not be left in hell, that is, in Purgatory, nor shall our flesh see eternal corruption.

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. THOU HAST MADE KNOWN TO ME THE WAYS OF LIFE, THOU SHALT FILL ME WITH JOY WITH THY COUNTENANCE: AT THY RIGHT HAND ARE DELIGHTS EVEN TO THE END.**

Here he setteth forth the perfect promise of the inheritance: "Thou hast made known to me," saith he, "the ways of life," that is, thou hast taught me the manner of returning to life after death; it is a most beautiful metaphor, by which the manner of rising again by one's own power is called a way unknown until that day, because no one ever rose again by his own power, Christ excepted. Nor only, saith he, hast thou taught me to rise again from the dead, but also "thou shalt fill me with joy with thy countenance," making me glorious, immortal and blessed, by showing me thy countenance; for from the vision of God, in which essential beatitude consisteth, glory overfloweth also into the body, which alone Christ lacked: for the glory of the soul he had had from his conception. "At thy right hand are delights even to the end." Nor only wilt thou make me glorious, but thou wilt place me at thy right hand in heaven, where there are delights without any end. All which things belong also in their own manner to the elect, to whom God showeth the ways of life, when he teacheth that the observance of the law is the way to the kingdom of heaven. The same he filleth with joy with his countenance, when he giveth himself to be seen by them face to face, and lastly his right hand affordeth them delights even to the end, when he placeth them on the right side, and when with his right hand, as from an unexhausted fountain of goods, he filleth them unceasingly with delights. At the end of this Psalm it pleaseth me to note the incredible rashness of Theodore Beza, who dared thus to interpret those words: "Thou wilt not leave my soul in hell," from the Greek text, *Acts* ii: *Non derelinques cadaver meum in sepulcro* (Thou wilt not leave my carcass in the grave): for what is a corruption of the sacred letters, if this is not? See, if it please thee, the refutation of this paradox in our *Controversies*, in the book *On the Soul of Christ*, chap. xii, where we demonstrate that the words, both Hebrew and Greek, which are found in this Psalm and in chap. ii of the *Acts*, do not properly signify *carcass* and *grave*, but *soul* and *hell*.

[Margin: The incredible rashness of Beza is noted.]
