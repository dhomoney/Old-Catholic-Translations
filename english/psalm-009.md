BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM IX.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*Unto the end, for the hidden things of the Son. A psalm for David.*[^1]

This title the Hebrews read: *Victori super mortem filii* (To the victor, upon the death of the son); and indeed the Hebrew words, as they are now read, so sound. But because this Psalm is a Psalm of gladness, and David was affected not with gladness, but with very great sorrow, from the death of his son, therefore some will have it that that word, לבן *laben*, is to be read with the order of the letters turned about, namely, נבל *nabal*, so that the sense is, *pro morte Nabal* (for the death of Nabal). But the Psalm speaketh not of the death of one man only, but also of the destruction of some great army. Therefore others say that in this Psalm thanks are given to God for the death of Goliath, and the flight of the whole army of the Philistines. But Goliath was not the son of David, that he should have inscribed the Psalm, *pro morte filii* (for the death of the son). Most true therefore is the reading of the Seventy and of the Latin Vulgate edition, *pro occultis* (for the hidden things), or *arcanis filii* (the secret things of the son). For those letters, עלמות *almuth*, can be read *alamoth*, which signifieth *hidden things*, or *secret things*, and it is credible that the preposition *al* hath fallen out, and that it is to be read *al alamoth, pro occultis*, as it is read in the title of Psalm xlv. Certainly the Seventy Interpreters, who rendered each title in the same manner, namely, *pro occultis*, without doubt read in the same manner, namely, *al alamoth*; nor can it be doubtful that their books were more correct than ours. But because the word *filii* (of the son) is set in this place absolutely, and there goeth before it the word *arcani* (secret) or *occulti* (hidden), that is, of a mystery laid up, we rightly understand that the Psalm treateth of that only Son of God, who, lying hid in human flesh, and fighting with a new kind of warfare, overcame the devil and his army; which the Apostle, *Col.* i, calleth the mystery hidden from ages. To which sense the Hebrew reading can be brought back, *pro morte filii*, that is, for the death of Christ the Son of God, in so far as that death was the glorious vanquishing of the prince of darkness.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. I WILL GIVE PRAISE TO THEE, O LORD, WITH MY WHOLE HEART: I WILL RELATE ALL THY WONDERS.**

He setteth forth the matter of the Psalm, which will be the praise of God for his wonderful works. That, *with my whole heart*, signifieth that the thing which he is about to praise is the greatest of all, and therefore to be praised with all his strength and with all his affection. That also, *all thy wonders*, signifieth that the thing to be praised is so universal that it comprehendeth, as in a kind of abridgment, all the wonderful works of God. And truly such was the redemption of mankind, a work of the highest mercy, in which all the benefits of God are recapitulated, according to that of the Apostle, *Ephes.* i, 10: "To re-establish all things in Christ," that is, to recapitulate, as the Greek word ἀνακεφαλαιώσασθαι soundeth, and to bring all things as it were into one sum.

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. I WILL BE GLAD AND REJOICE IN THEE: I WILL SING TO THY NAME, O THOU MOST HIGH.**

He signifieth the selfsame thing in other words, or rather explaineth it, as if he said: I will give praise to thee, being glad and rejoicing, so that there be gladness in the heart, rejoicing in the outward motion, and thus I will give praise with my whole heart. I will relate thy wonders, singing to thy name, O thou most High, that is, singing those wonders unto thy glory.

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. WHEN MY ENEMY SHALL BE TURNED BACK: THEY SHALL BE WEAKENED AND PERISH BEFORE THY FACE.**

He beginneth to relate the victory of Christ against the devil and his attendants, and speaketh in the person of the whole Church: "When my enemy," saith he, "shall be turned back," that is, when my enemy the devil shall be turned back, beginning, to wit, to flee from thy face; then all his soldiers shall be weakened and shall perish, that is, the flight of their leader being known, they shall be made feeble, and shall slip away, and vanish, as if they had perished. Of this flight of the devil the Lord speaketh in the Gospel: "Now is the judgment of the world: now shall the prince of this world be cast out," *John* xii, 31.

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. FOR THOU HAST MAINTAINED MY JUDGMENT AND MY CAUSE: THOU HAST SAT ON THE THRONE, WHO JUDGEST JUSTICE.**

[Margin: He hath ended the suit of mankind with the devil.]

He giveth the cause why it shall come to pass that the devil fleeth, and his whole army is scattered: "For," saith he, thou, O Lord, Son of God, "hast maintained my judgment and my cause," that is, thou hast ended the judgment and suit (for that the Hebrew word דיני *dini* properly soundeth), and the controversy which mankind, or the Church, had with the devil. For the devil pretended that mankind was justly held captive by him, and he vexed it tyrannically; but Christ on the cross ended this suit, making satisfaction for man; whence it is added: "Thou hast sat on the throne, who judgest justice," where he calleth the cross a throne, according to that of St. Leo, sermon 8 *On the Passion of the Lord*: "O unspeakable glory of the passion, in which is both the tribunal of the Lord, and the judgment of the world, and the power of the crucified!" And these are truly the hidden things of the Son of God, as it is in the title of the Psalm. For he who in the eyes of men seemed guilty, and to be paying the penalty with the utmost ignominy, at that very time, sitting on the throne, judged justice, that is, most justly judged, the price being now fully paid, that man was to be set free, and the devil to be stripped of his dominion; and in very deed "the handwriting of the decree, which was contrary to us," as the Apostle writeth, *Col.* ii, 14, "he hath taken out of the way, fastening it to the cross, and despoiling the principalities and powers."

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. THOU HAST REBUKED THE GENTILES, AND THE WICKED ONE HATH PERISHED: THOU HAST BLOTTED OUT THEIR NAME FOR EVER AND EVER.**

After the devil was vanquished by the cross, the Lord Christ through the Apostles rebuked the Gentiles, convicting "the world of sin, of justice, and of judgment" (as the Lord himself foretold would be, *John* xvi, 8), and in that manner the wicked one perished, that is, the wickedness of idolatry perished, and man, of wicked, was made godly. Nor did the wicked one only perish, who then was; but Christ blotted out their name, that is, the name of heathenism and idolatry, for ever, so that they shall return no more, being as it were plucked up by the roots. Which we now see so fulfilled, that not even the Jews worship idols any more, who were most prone to worship them. As touching the words, in the Greek there is not *nomen eorum* (their name), but *nomen ejus* (his name), that is, of the wicked one; but in the Hebrew there is שמם *schemum, nomen eorum*, and the Latin interpretation hath the same, either because it was once so read in the Greek, and was afterwards changed by the fault of the copyists; or because the Hebrew reading pleased the Latin interpreter more; but in the meaning there is no diversity of any moment. Now that, *in æternum, et in sæculum sæculi* (for ever and ever), is in the Hebrew לעולם ועד, *in sæculum et in æternum* (for an age and for ever): and by this addition true eternity is signified, that is, a duration never to be ended, lest any should take *for ever* in this place for a long time, but one that shall at some time have an end.

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. THE SWORDS OF THE ENEMY HAVE FAILED UNTO THE END: AND THEIR CITIES THOU HAST DESTROYED.**

He giveth the cause why idolatry shall not return: because the arms of the devil have perished, and the cities where he dwelt, so that he hath neither wherewith to assail, nor where to defend himself. That, *inimici* (of the enemy), is of the genitive case, as is plain from the Greek: for from the Hebrew it might also be translated *o inimice!* (O enemy!) but most right is the Greek

<!-- p. 53 (PDF 64) -->

and Latin version, and it is not repugnant to the Hebrew. For *frameæ* (swords), there is now read in the Hebrew *solitudines* (desolations); but the word חרבות *charaboth* signifieth *swords*, and *choraboth*, *desolations*: and either can be read, the points only being changed. And so the Seventy read *charaboth*; therefore they rightly translated *frameæ*, or *gladii* (swords), so that the sense is: The swords of our enemy the devil have failed, even unto the end, that is, none remaining, or for ever. That also, *civitates eorum* (their cities), is in the Hebrew and the Greek without the pronoun *eorum* (their): *Et civitates destruxisti* (And cities thou hast destroyed), namely, the enemy's, as if he said: The enemy hath lost his swords and his cities. By *the swords of the enemy* can be understood temptations, or suggestions, and as it were the words of the devil, as the Apostle calleth the word of God *the sword of the Spirit*: whence also the same Apostle saith that the temptations of the devil are *fiery darts*, *Ephes.* v.[^2] Now these swords are said to have failed, because they can in no wise hurt those who are armed with the faith of Jesus Christ. In which sense St. Anthony alleged this place of the Psalm, in Athanasius, in his Life, wishing to prove that the temptations of the devil are most easily driven back by the sign of the cross. And by the cities of the devil can be understood unbelieving men, in whom the devil reigneth freely. Christ destroyed these cities, when through the whole world he took away the idols. Of this thing the Lord seemeth to speak, when he saith, *Luke* xi, 21: "When a strong man armed keepeth his court, those things are in peace which he possesseth. But if a stronger than he come upon him, and overcome him; he will take away all his armour," etc. When the devil kept the court, all things which he possessed were in peace, that is, when he preserved unbelief, he possessed all his own securely; because while a man is an unbeliever, he is always in the hand of the devil, even though he seem to live morally well, as many philosophers and heathens did. But the court being taken by Christ, that is, unbelief being taken away, and the knowledge of the true God brought in, the devil lost all that was his.

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. THEIR MEMORY HATH PERISHED WITH A NOISE. BUT THE LORD REMAINETH FOR EVER.**

[Margin: Christ and his memory shall never perish.]

In the Hebrew, as it is now read, there is not *cum sonitu* (with a noise), but *cum ipsis* (with them), so that the sense is: the wicked have perished, and with them hath perished also their memory. But the Hebrew word can be read *hemmah*, and signifieth *with them*; it can also be read המה *hamah*, and signifieth *a noise*: and so the Seventy Interpreters read, and added for the sake of clearness the preposition *cum* (with), and therefore they translated *sonitu* (a noise); and the sense is, that the memory of idolaters, and of the idols themselves, and of the whole kingdom of Satan, hath perished with a great din and noise. For the whole world resisted, and the very highest kings and emperors strove with all their strength to defend the idols: but the more the world raged, the more idolatry fell, and its memory was abolished. Lastly, the din of the persecutions being ended, the memory also of idolatry perished: but the Lord Christ and his memory shall never perish.

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. HE HATH PREPARED HIS THRONE IN JUDGMENT: AND HE SHALL JUDGE THE WORLD IN EQUITY, HE SHALL JUDGE THE PEOPLE IN JUSTICE.**

After the death and resurrection of Christ, "power is given to him in heaven and in earth," *Matt.*, last chap. This David recounteth in this place, as if he said: After the combat with the devil the Lord "hath prepared," or, as the Hebrew word חנן *chonen* soundeth, *stabilivit* (hath established), "his throne in judgment," that is, for judging, and he, who is the prince of the kings of the earth, "shall judge the world," that is, all the peoples of the whole world, "in equity and justice." Now equity and justice are the same in this place; for as well in the Hebrew as in the Greek, for *æquitate* (equity) there is *justitia* (justice), for *justitia* there is *rectitudo* (uprightness). The Latin interpreter took not equity properly, but, as it is wont sometimes to be put, for justice; and as in the Hebrew and the Greek the same thing is twice repeated in different words, so it is to be believed that the Latin interpreter would have the same understood by equity and justice. Now Christ is said to judge the world, although there are in the world many unbelieving and wicked princes who fight against Christ, because their hearts are in the hand of Christ, nor do they any thing but what he by a just judgment suffereth them to do.

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. AND THE LORD IS BECOME A REFUGE FOR THE POOR: A HELPER IN DUE TIME IN TRIBULATION.**

From this, that henceforth the Lord Christ shall reign, and shall judge justly, David gathereth a great consolation of the poor, who otherwise are wont to be oppressed by the mighty, as if he said: Let the poor fear no more, because the Lord, sitting in heaven, is become a refuge of the poor; and again he repeateth the selfsame thing in other words: "A helper," saith he, of the poor "in due time" (*in opportunitatibus*), that is, at seasonable times, when, to wit, necessity requireth it, namely, in tribulation. For then is the divine help seasonably given, when we are pressed by tribulation, and human consolation faileth. Now this promise is fulfilled in all who truly seek and fear God, and therefore he subjoineth:

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. AND LET THEM TRUST IN THEE WHO KNOW THY NAME: FOR THOU HAST NOT FORSAKEN THEM THAT SEEK THEE, O LORD.**

The change of person is frequent in the Psalms, and often springeth from a new affection which was inspired into David while he sang the praises of God. Deservedly, saith he, can all trust in thee in their straits who know thy name, that is, who not only sound with the voice the name of God, but well understand how great a thing it signifieth, and from this name rightly understood know the power and mercy of God. Therefore deservedly can thy friends trust in thee, "for thou hast not forsaken," that is, thou hast never forsaken, them that seek thee. Now they are said to seek God who desire his grace, and study with their whole heart to please him.

<!-- p. 54 (PDF 65) -->

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. SING YE TO THE LORD, WHO DWELLETH IN SION: DECLARE HIS WAYS AMONG THE GENTILES:**

[Margin: Why is God said to dwell in Sion?]

After the apostrophe to God, out of the same fervour of spirit he maketh another to men, exhorting them that they also praise God, and stir up others to praise him. The Lord is said to dwell in Sion, because there was the ark of the testament, and the place of prayer; and this periphrasis is added, that the true God may be distinguished from the false gods, who dwell in the shrines and temples of the heathens. Now that, *studia ejus* (his ways), signifieth the thoughts, counsels, endeavours, devices, wonderful works of God, which shine forth in the redemption of mankind. For the same Hebrew word עלילות *hhaliloth*, which is here translated *studia*, is in *Ps.* civ translated *opera* (works), and in *Isa.* xii, in the canticle of Isaias, is translated *adinventiones* (devices). And so the sense is: Sing a Psalm to the true God, praising him, and declare among the Gentiles his wonderful thoughts, and wonderful industry, and even his wonderful works, that all the Gentiles hearing these things may praise him.

[Margin: Verse 12.]

**12. FOR REQUIRING THEIR BLOOD HE HATH REMEMBERED THEM: HE HATH NOT FORGOTTEN THE CRY OF THE POOR.**

The Prophet returneth to that which he was saying, that the Lord, the just judge, is the refuge of the poor in tribulation; and he answereth a silent objection which could be made, because we see that the godly poor are sometimes oppressed by the more mighty, and even innocently slain. Praise God, saith he, because, although he seem sometimes to forget his poor, yet it is not so, but "requiring," that is, inquiring into the life of men, and examining the deeds of each, "he hath remembered the blood" of the innocent unjustly shed, and "hath not forgotten the cry" of them who, while they were oppressed, cried to him. Now that God hath remembered the blood of his own will be plain in its own time, when both the punishment of the oppressors and the glory of the oppressed shall be made manifest.

[Margin: Verse 13.]

**13. HAVE MERCY ON ME, O LORD: SEE MY HUMILIATION WHICH I SUFFER FROM MY ENEMIES.**

He hath praised God for past benefits; now he imploreth help against present and future straits. And it is the voice of the Church against persecutors both visible and invisible. "Have mercy on me," saith he, "O Lord: see my humiliation," that is, my abasement (for that the Greek word ταπείνωσις soundeth), or affliction (which the Hebrew word עני *hhani* is wont to signify), "from my enemies," that is, proceeding from my enemies.

[Margin: Verse 14.]

**14. THOU THAT LIFTEST ME UP FROM THE GATES OF DEATH, THAT I MAY DECLARE ALL THY PRAISES IN THE GATES OF THE DAUGHTER OF SION.**

[Margin: What do the gates of death signify?]

The first part of this verse is joined with the verse above. For the sense is: "Have mercy on me, and see my humiliation," thou, O Lord, "that liftest me up from the gates of death," that is, who art wont to set me far from the gates of death; and because those gates are understood to be in a lower place, and the same with the gates of hell, since the Prophet speaketh not of the death of the body, but of the death of the soul through sin, or of eternal death; therefore he saith that he is lifted up, that he may be set far from them. By the gates of death, or of hell, the multitude of our infernal enemies seemeth to be understood. For because the multitude of the people was wont among the Hebrews to come together at the gates, whether for judgment or for other public businesses, therefore a multitude is signified by the name of gates, *Matt.* xvi, 18: "The gates of hell shall not prevail against it"; *Ecclus.*, last chap.: "From the gates of afflictions, which compassed me about." And beautifully are the gates of death set against the gates of the daughter of Sion, that is, the gates of Jerusalem: because the gates of death, or of hell, are in the lowest place, the gates of Jerusalem on a high mountain; in the gates of death the infernal people is gathered together, in the gates of Jerusalem the people of God. The gates of death send forth temptations and wars, by which men go to death; the gates of Jerusalem are founded in peace. For Jerusalem hath placed peace in her borders, and she herself is called the vision of peace. And so the Church is lifted up from the gates of death, that she may declare the praise of God "in the gates of the daughter of Sion," that is, she is delivered from temptations, lest she fall into eternal death, that she may acknowledge the grace of her deliverer, and praise him before the angels of God, who are in the gates of the heavenly Jerusalem.

[Margin: Verse 15.]

**15. I WILL REJOICE IN THY SALVATION: THE GENTILES HAVE STUCK FAST IN THE DESTRUCTION WHICH THEY HAVE PREPARED.**

I will rejoice, saith he, being delivered from the gates of death, "in thy salvation," that is, in the salvation given me by thee. Because (for that is understood, as often elsewhere in the Psalms) the Gentiles who made, that is, prepared, destruction for me, have stuck fast in that very destruction, as in a most clinging mire, whence they shall not be able to escape: that is, the persecution of the enemies hath hurt not me, but themselves.

[Margin: Verse 16.]

**16. THEIR FOOT HATH BEEN TAKEN IN THE VERY SNARE WHICH THEY HID.**

Nor was the open persecution only, but the hidden also, which was laid as a snare, hurtful to its authors, not to me. Perhaps the open persecutions of Diocletian and the like are here touched upon, and the hidden ones of Julian the apostate and of the heretical emperors.

[Margin: Verse 17.]

**17. THE LORD SHALL BE KNOWN WHEN HE EXECUTETH JUDGMENTS: THE SINNER HATH BEEN CAUGHT IN THE WORKS OF HIS OWN HANDS.**

From this wonderful judgment of God, who turneth the arms and frauds of wicked men against themselves, David gathereth that it shall come to pass that God is known: "The Lord shall be known when he executeth judgments," that is, he shall be known

<!-- p. 55 (PDF 66) -->

to be the true and supreme God, while he executeth judgments so wonderful; for through his providence "the sinner hath been caught in the works of his own hands," namely, when he falleth into the destruction which he had prepared for others, and into the snare which he had laid for others. Like things are found in *Ps.* vii, verse 15, and they have the same sense. After this verse there is added in the Hebrew הגיון סלה *higgaion selah*, which can be translated *meditatio semper* (meditation always), which signifieth that the judgments of God aforesaid are to be meditated unceasingly and continually.

[Margin: Verse 18.]

**18. THE WICKED SHALL BE TURNED INTO HELL, ALL THE NATIONS THAT FORGET GOD.**

[Margin: The root of all sins is forgetfulness of God.]

It is not an imprecation, but a prophecy, that the wicked shall go down to hell. *Convertantur* (let them be turned) seemeth to be put for *revertantur* (let them return), as the Hebrew word ישובו *iashubu* soundeth, and *revertantur* for *revertentur* (they shall return). Now sinners are said to be about to return to hell, because they came thence, in so far as they are sinners. For God made man right, but the devil made him a sinner. "You," saith the Lord, *John* viii, "are of your father the devil." That which followeth: "All the nations that forget God," declareth who the sinners are that shall return into hell, namely, all those who forget God. For forgetfulness of God is the root of all sins; for every one who sinneth turneth away from God, and turneth to the creature.

[Margin: Verse 19.]

**19. FOR THE POOR MAN SHALL NOT BE FORGOTTEN TO THE END: THE PATIENCE OF THE POOR SHALL NOT PERISH FOR EVER.**

In the Hebrew there is no negation in the second member, but it is read: *Patientia pauperum peribit in æternum* (The patience of the poor shall perish for ever); but the negation of the former member is, by the Hebrew phrase, drawn also to the latter member, and therefore the Seventy Interpreters rightly translated *non peribit* (shall not perish). Now this verse signifieth that sinners, who are wont to oppress the poor, are to be cast down into hell for this reason, that God will at some time take vengeance for the oppression of his poor. For although for a time he seem not to remember them, yet he will not forget them to the end; but at some time he will remember, and so the patience, or expectation, of the poor shall not perish "for ever" (*in finem*, to the end), that is, *in æternum* (for ever), as the Hebrew word לעד soundeth. Now the patience of the poor is said not to be about to perish for ever, not as to itself, but as to its effect, as if he said: It shall not be disappointed for ever, but at some time shall obtain its effect.

[Margin: Verse 20.]

**20. ARISE, O LORD, LET NOT MAN BE STRENGTHENED: LET THE GENTILES BE JUDGED IN THY SIGHT.**

After he hath foretold the destruction of the wicked, he asketh God to begin to restrain the wicked: "Arise," saith he, "O Lord, let not man be strengthened," that is, let not man, who is dust, prevail against God his maker. "Let the Gentiles be judged in thy sight," that is, let judgment against them proceed from thy face. Which we read in another Psalm: "Judge them, O God."

[Margin: Verse 21.]

**21. APPOINT, O LORD, A LAWGIVER OVER THEM: THAT THE GENTILES MAY KNOW THEMSELVES TO BE BUT MEN.**

The judgment against the Gentiles who persecuted the Church was manifest when they were made subject to a Christian prince. For then the Gentiles understood that they were men, weak, to wit, and not able to prevail against Christ. This the Prophet foretelleth after the manner of one praying. The Hebrew word מורה *morah*, or *moreh*, signifieth *a teacher*, and also *terror*, as St. Jerome translateth. And because the Prophet speaketh of a terrible teacher, who commandeth and teacheth with authority, therefore the Seventy Interpreters rightly said *legislatorem* (a lawgiver). Many by this lawgiver understand Christ, many Antichrist. In a matter most obscure let every man abound in his own sense. I understand Constantine, Jovinian,[^3] Valentinian, Theodosius, and the like.

[Margin: Verse 22.]

**22. WHY, O LORD, HAST THOU RETIRED AFAR OFF? WHY DOST THOU SLIGHT US IN OUR WANTS, IN THE TIME OF TROUBLE?**

At this verse the Hebrews begin the tenth Psalm, but the Seventy Interpreters had not this division; and it is credible that the division was devised by later men, because the matter of this part of the Psalm seemeth altogether different from the matter of the former part, since in the former part the Church rejoiceth for the victory against the enemies of God and her own, in the latter she mourneth for the prosperous successes of the same enemies against the Church. But the matter is not so much different, as the times are different which David runneth through in prophesying. For in the beginning of the Psalm David rejoiced in spirit for the hidden mysteries of the Son of God, who by dying vanquished the powers of the air and heathenism, and destroyed the idols; then in the end of the former part, and in this latter, he foretelleth the persecutions of the Church, stirred up both by the Gentiles and by other evil men, which sometimes so increase that the Lord seemeth to have forgotten his people, whom he had so gloriously delivered; and because he had said a little before: "The Lord is become a refuge for the poor: a helper in due time in tribulation," therefore now, looking to another time, in which, God permitting, the poor are oppressed by the more mighty, he saith: "Why, O Lord, hast thou retired afar off," that is, dost thou so permit the wicked to rage against the just, as if thou wert not present, but hadst retired afar off? "Why dost thou slight us," thy poor, namely, "in our wants" (*in opportunitatibus*), when it would be seasonable to help them, namely, "in the time of trouble."

[Margin: Verse 23.]

**23. WHILST THE WICKED MAN IS PROUD, THE POOR IS SET ON FIRE: THEY ARE CAUGHT IN THE COUNSELS WHICH THEY DEVISE.**

An altogether difficult verse; this seemeth to be the meaning: "Whilst the wicked man is proud," that is, whilst the wicked man prospereth, and appeareth glorious, then

<!-- p. 56 (PDF 67) -->

"the poor is set on fire," that is, the just poor man is scandalized, and burneth with the heat of his thoughts. "They are caught in the counsels which they devise," that is, each of them, as well the glorious wicked man as the just poor man, is caught and ensnared by his own counsel, that is, by his own thought. For the wicked man thinketh that that felicity is due to him, and he deceiveth himself: but the just man, seeing the wicked man prosper, thinketh what this may mean, and entangleth himself more and more. As touching the grammar, when it is said, *in consiliis, quibus cogitant* (in the counsels which they devise), it is a Grecism, and the Latin interpreter would translate to the word. For we should say: *in consiliis, de quibus cogitant* (in the counsels of which they think), or *quæ cogitant* (which they think). And by counsels we understand thoughts, as the Hebrew word more properly signifieth. Moreover this seemeth to be the reason of the verse above; for the Prophet asked God not to slight the poor man in tribulation for this cause, that the prosperity of the wicked hurteth both the wicked and the just, while those are proud, and these are scandalized.

[Margin: Verse 24.]

**24. FOR THE SINNER IS PRAISED IN THE DESIRES OF HIS SOUL: AND THE UNJUST MAN IS BLESSED.**

The Hebrew reading, as it now standeth, would have to be translated to the word thus: *Quoniam laudavit impius desiderium animæ suæ, et avarus benedicens exacerbavit Dominum* (For the wicked man hath praised the desire of his soul, and the covetous man, blessing, hath provoked the Lord). But the points and divisions being removed, which in the time of the Seventy Interpreters were not there, the meaning is most clearly seen which the Seventy most rightly expressed. For the word הלל *hillel*, which signifieth *laudavit* (hath praised), if it be read *hullal*, as the Seventy read, signifieth *laudatus est* (is praised); and the word ברך *berech, benedicens* (blessing), if it be read *baruch*, will signify *benedictus est* (is blessed). But that, *exacerbavit Dominum* (hath provoked the Lord), pertaineth to the following verse, which beginneth: "The sinner hath provoked the Lord." The Prophet therefore giveth the cause why the wicked man is proud from his prosperity, and the poor man burneth: namely, because the sinner doth evil, and yet, because he is rich and mighty, he is praised by many, as though he did well, and the desires of his soul, that is, his own desires (for it is a Hebrew phrase, *desiderium animæ meæ* (the desire of my soul), for *desiderium meum* (my desire)), although iniquitous and unjust, are approved, and hence it cometh that the unjust man is blessed, when he rather deserved to be cursed.

[Margin: Verse 25.]

**25. THE SINNER HATH PROVOKED THE LORD: ACCORDING TO THE MULTITUDE OF HIS WRATH HE WILL NOT SEEK HIM.**

He goeth on to explain the malice of the proud sinner: "The sinner," saith he, "hath provoked the Lord," and whereas he ought with all diligence to seek reconciliation, "according to the multitude of his wrath he will not seek him," that is, on account of the greatness of his wrath against the afflicted poor, he will not seek God, or reconciliation with God. For arrogance so blindeth his mind, that the sinner thinketh not how great an evil it is to provoke almighty God.

[Margin: Verse 26.]

**26. GOD IS NOT BEFORE HIS EYES: HIS WAYS ARE FILTHY AT ALL TIMES.**

The sinner, being blinded, thinketh not of God. In the Hebrew it is read with greater emphasis: *Non est Deus in omnibus cogitationibus ejus* (God is not in all his thoughts), that is, in no thought of his is God, that is, whereas he thinketh of so many things, God never cometh into his thought. So utterly hath he forgotten God. "His ways are filthy at all times." This followeth from the foregoing, because he never thinketh of God; at no time doth he direct his way to God, but to the fulfilling of carnal desires: and therefore all his ways, that is, all his works, are made filthy with the uncleanness of concupiscence.

[Margin: Verse 27.]

**27. THY JUDGMENTS ARE REMOVED FROM HIS SIGHT: HE SHALL RULE OVER ALL HIS ENEMIES.**

That which could turn him from the evil way, namely, the thought of thy terrible judgments, is far from his heart; and therefore without any fear of God he ruleth over all his enemies, that is, he tyrannically oppresseth all whom he thinketh his enemies.

[Margin: Verse 28.]

**28. FOR HE HATH SAID IN HIS HEART: I SHALL NOT BE MOVED FROM GENERATION TO GENERATION, AND SHALL BE WITHOUT EVIL.**

The vain confidence of the wicked man is here noted; for he thinketh within himself that nothing can hurt him: "He hath said," saith he, "in his heart: I shall not be moved," that is, no man will be able to move me, nor to cast me down from my state; "from generation to generation without evil," supply, I shall be. For both in the Hebrew and in the Greek a stop is set after the word *movebor* (I shall be moved), and in the Hebrew that sense plainly appeareth: from generation to generation I shall be without evil, as St. Jerome also translateth.

[Margin: Verse 29.]

**29. HIS MOUTH IS FULL OF CURSING, AND OF BITTERNESS, AND OF DECEIT: UNDER HIS TONGUE ARE LABOUR AND SORROW.**

The Prophet hath described what the heart of the wicked man is, namely, that it thinketh not of God, nor of his judgments, and hopeth that no evil will befall it: now he describeth what his mouth is, being about to describe afterwards what his works are. He saith therefore that the mouth of the wicked man is full of cursing, bitterness and deceit. To cursing pertain blasphemies against God, and revilings against men; to bitterness, detractions, murmurings, contentions, and other things that witness hatred and rancour; lastly, to deceits, calumnies, lies, perjuries. That which is added, *under his tongue are labour and sorrow*, signifieth the effect of the foregoing evils. For all the words of the wicked man tend to this, that they may bring labour and sorrow to the afflicted. "Under his tongue are labour and sorrow," that is, that which is under the tongue of the wicked man, that is, the matter in which the tongue of the wicked man is exercised, is the labour and sorrow of wretched men, who are in any way made subject to him.

<!-- p. 57 (PDF 68) -->

[Margin: Verse 30.]

**30. HE SITTETH IN AMBUSH WITH THE RICH IN PRIVATE PLACES, THAT HE MAY KILL THE INNOCENT.**

Here now the evil works are described, that is, the oppressions of the poor. And the speech is metaphorical, the similitude being drawn from those who lie hid within the porches of a house, that they may watch when those pass by whom they desire to kill. Now the Prophet signifieth by this metaphor that wicked and mighty men, having joined to themselves as companions other rich and mighty men, circumvent the poor by divers arts and deceits, and lead them away to utter ruin. In the Hebrew there is not *in insidiis cum divitibus* (in ambush with the rich), but *in insidiis atriorum*, or *vestibulorum* (in the ambush of the courts, or of the porches). But the Seventy read not חצרים *chatserim*, as it now is in the Hebrew copies, but עשרים *hhasirim*. For that signifieth *courts*, this *the rich*. And so it is probable that after the times of the Seventy the Hebrew text was changed.

[Margin: Verse 31.]

**31. HIS EYES ARE UPON THE POOR MAN: HE LIETH IN WAIT IN SECRET LIKE A LION IN HIS DEN.**

[Margin: Verse 32.]

**32. HE LIETH IN AMBUSH THAT HE MAY CATCH THE POOR MAN: TO CATCH THE POOR, WHILST HE DRAWETH HIM TO HIM.**

[Margin: Verse 33.]

**33. IN HIS NET HE WILL BRING HIM DOWN, HE WILL CROUCH AND FALL, WHEN HE SHALL HAVE POWER OVER THE POOR.**

What he said in the thirtieth verse he expoundeth in these three verses, varying the metaphors. For in verse 30 he compared the oppressor of the poor to a man lying in wait for another man; in verse 31 he compareth him to a lion lying in wait for other and weaker beasts; in verses 32 and 33 he compareth him to a man laying snares for beasts, and taking them. The two last verses are much clearer in the Hebrew. For the Hebrew reading can be rendered to the word thus, as St. Jerome translateth: *Insidiatur, ut rapiat pauperem; rapiet pauperem cum attraxerit eum ad rete suum; et confractum subjiciet, et irruet viribus suis valenter* (He lieth in ambush that he may catch the poor man; he will catch the poor man when he hath drawn him to his net; and he will subdue him, being broken, and will rush upon him mightily with his strength). But our reading is to be explained thus: "He lieth in ambush that he may catch the poor man"; and he lieth in ambush to catch the poor man then, when he draweth him to himself, alluring him unawares. But "in his net," him being now taken, "he will bring him down," that is, will oppress him, and "he will crouch" over him, and "fall" upon him, that is, will rush upon him, "when he shall have power over the poor," that is, when he shall have fully got them into his power. And at the same time these words beautifully allude to the inward fall of the wicked man: for then doth he fall horribly into the bondage of the devil, when he seemeth to himself to have power over wretched men.

[Margin: Verse 34.]

**34. FOR HE HATH SAID IN HIS HEART: GOD HATH FORGOTTEN, HE HATH TURNED AWAY HIS FACE NOT TO SEE TO THE END.**

He repeateth the cause of so great wickedness, namely, because the wicked man thinketh within himself that God careth not for human things, nor ever will care.

[Margin: Verse 35.]

**35. ARISE, O LORD GOD, LET THY HAND BE EXALTED: FORGET NOT THE POOR.**

He asketh God to restrain the wicked: "Arise," saith he, as from sleep, and "let thy hand be exalted," to strike: for the hands of one sleeping, or of a man at rest, are wont to be let down, and often folded. In the Greek two words are added: after *Domine Deus* (O Lord God) there is added *meus* (my), and after *ne obliviscaris pauperum* (forget not the poor) there is added *in finem* (to the end). But they are not in the Hebrew, nor in the more correct Latin copies.

[Margin: Verse 36.]

**36. WHEREFORE HATH THE WICKED PROVOKED GOD? FOR HE HATH SAID IN HIS HEART: HE WILL NOT REQUIRE IT.**

Again he repeateth the cause why the wicked man provoketh God by sinning, that it is this, that he thinketh that God requireth not these things.

[Margin: Verse 37.]

**37. THOU SEEST IT, FOR THOU CONSIDEREST LABOUR AND SORROW: THAT THOU MAYST DELIVER THEM INTO THY HANDS.**

The Prophet refuteth that cause, as if he said: Nay, thou seest, O God! and requirest, which appeareth from this, that thou considerest the labour and sorrow of the wretched, that, when the measure shall be filled up, thou mayst deliver the wicked into thy hands to be punished. In the Hebrew and the Greek it is: *Quoniam tu laborem et furorem consideras* (For thou considerest labour and rage), and so also St. Augustine readeth, and St. Jerome; and if we so read, the sense will be: For thou considerest the labour of the oppressed and the rage of the oppressors.

[Margin: Verse 38.]

**38. TO THEE IS THE POOR MAN LEFT: THOU WILT BE A HELPER TO THE ORPHAN.**

Deservedly dost thou consider these things, because upon thee, the Father of all, lieth the care of them, chiefly, who are destitute of all other help.

[Margin: Verse 39.]

**39. BREAK THOU THE ARM OF THE SINNER AND OF THE MALIGNANT: HIS SIN SHALL BE SOUGHT, AND SHALL NOT BE FOUND.**

"Break thou the arm," that is, the power and strength, of the sinner, and so, being humbled, he will come to a better mind, and will sin no more: therefore thou wilt afterwards seek his sin, and wilt not find it. For, as Isaias saith, chap. xxviii, 19: "Vexation alone shall make you understand what you hear"; and *Ps.* lxxxii, 17: "Fill their faces with shame; and they shall seek thy name."

[Margin: Verse 40.]

**40. THE LORD SHALL REIGN TO ETERNITY, YEA, FOR EVER AND EVER: YE GENTILES SHALL PERISH FROM HIS LAND.**

He foretelleth that that shall be which he had prayed might be done. "The Lord," saith he, "shall reign" always, his enemies being unwilling; nay, the enemies themselves shall perish, and shall be driven out "from his land," that is, from the whole round of the earth, "for the earth is the Lord's and the fulness thereof," *Ps.* xxiii, 1.

[Margin: Verse 41.]

**41. THE LORD HATH HEARD THE DESIRE OF THE POOR: THY EAR HATH HEARD THE PREPARATION OF THEIR HEART.**

That which is to come he setteth down as past,

<!-- p. 58 (PDF 69) -->

[Margin: Desire is a preparation for praying.]

on account of its certainty; nor doth he say the prayer, but "the desire of the poor," to show the speedy hearing, as if he said: God, who searcheth hearts, will not wait for the prayer, but will hear the desire itself, which is wont to go before prayer. Now the desire and the preparation of the heart are the same: for desire is a preparation for praying.

[Margin: Verse 42.]

**42. TO JUDGE FOR THE FATHERLESS AND FOR THE HUMBLE, THAT MAN MAY NO MORE PRESUME TO MAGNIFY HIMSELF UPON EARTH.**

The Lord hath heard the desire of the poor, "to judge for the fatherless and for the humble," that is, he hath heard to this end, that he may judge the cause of the fatherless and of the humble against their very oppressors, lest man should henceforth dare to be proud, "man," I say, who is "upon earth," against God, who is in heaven. In the Hebrew it is said more significantly: *Ut nequaquam ultra superbiat homo de terra* (That man of the earth may by no means be proud any more), that is, man composed of earth, as if he said: That the thing formed may not be proud against him that formed it. All these things which are said in this Psalm against the wicked oppressor of the poor are attributed to Antichrist by Saints Jerome and Augustine; and without doubt all things which are said in general of the wicked agree to him by antonomasia: but if these things be said to the letter of the oppressors of the poor in general, as we judge, assuredly it manifestly appeareth how mighty a crime that is which is reproved by the Holy Ghost in so many words, and words so significant.
