BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM XX.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*Unto the end, a Psalm for David.*[^1]

Psalm xx is connected with the preceding, xix. That Psalm asked of God that he would protect Christ the King, who was about to fight with the enemy of mankind. This one giveth thanks, that he hath protected, exalted and crowned him.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. IN THY STRENGTH, O LORD, THE KING SHALL JOY; AND IN THY SALVATION HE SHALL REJOICE EXCEEDINGLY.**

The victory being won, our king Christ "shall joy in thy strength," that is, because of thy strength and power, through which he hath happily triumphed over his enemies. "And in thy salvation," that is, he shall be glad of the salvation bestowed on him by thee, and with the voice also "he shall rejoice exceedingly." And so one part of the verse declareth the other.

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. THOU HAST GIVEN HIM HIS HEART'S DESIRE: AND HAST NOT WITHHOLDEN FROM HIM THE WILL OF HIS LIPS.**

These words answer to those which had been said in the Psalm above: "May he give thee according to thy own heart," etc. It is a Hebrew phrase, to give a petition, for that which is to give the thing asked, as in Book I of *Kings*, chap. i, 17, Heli the priest saith to Anna: "The God of Israel grant thee thy petition, which thou hast asked of him." So therefore in this place it is said: "Thou hast given him his heart's desire," that is, thou hast given what he desired. "And hast not withholden from him the will of his lips," that is, thou hast not withholden from him that thing which his lips willed, that is, which he showed, by pouring forth prayer with his lips, that he willed and desired. In sum he saith that there was given to Christ what he had desired with his heart, and had sought with his lips.

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. FOR THOU HAST PREVENTED HIM WITH BLESSINGS OF SWEETNESS: THOU HAST SET ON HIS HEAD A CROWN OF PRECIOUS STONES.**

Great indeed was the cause of joy to Christ, that he not only had what he asked, but that God prevented his desires, and gave him the greatest gifts before he asked. "Thou hast prevented him," saith he, "with blessings of sweetness": in the Hebrew there is *tob*, *bonitatis* (of goodness), and likewise in the Greek, χρηστότητος. And so the sense is, that Christ was prevented with the gifts of the goodness of God, which were the conception by the Holy Ghost, the union to the person of the Word, the infusion of all knowledge and virtues, and the very vision of God: all which were given to him in the first instant of conception; therefore he was prevented with blessings of sweetness. But that which followeth, *thou hast set on his head a crown of precious stones*, or, as it is in the Hebrew, *de auro* פז, *perfecto* (of perfect gold), seemeth to be referred to the kingdom and the priesthood, which also he had from his very conception, on account of which he received the surname of Christ; for a golden crown is a kingly and priestly ensign.

[Margin: The gifts of God with which Christ was prevented.]

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. HE ASKED LIFE OF THEE: AND THOU HAST GIVEN HIM LENGTH OF DAYS FOR EVER AND EVER.**

The foregoing benefits he received being prevented: but the glory of the body, and immortality, and other things thereafter he asked and obtained. "He asked life," when, the passion being at hand, "he offered up prayers and supplications to him that was able to save him from death," as the Apostle said, *Heb.* v, 7. And God gave him "length of days for ever and ever," that is, life everlasting, "that rising again from the dead he may die now no more, and death may no more have dominion over him," *Rom.* vi, 9. By this place they are manifestly refuted, who expound this Psalm to the letter of David, as Jansenius doth; or of Ezechias, as Euthymius doth, and before him Theodoret: for neither David nor Ezechias had length of days for ever and ever.

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. HIS GLORY IS GREAT IN THY SALVATION: GLORY AND GREAT BEAUTY SHALT THOU LAY UPON HIM.**

Not only did God give him life for ever and ever, but he also "hath exalted him, and hath given him a name which is above all names," *Philip.* ii, 9. For this is truly the "great glory which he had in the salvation of God," that is, in the salvation whereby God saved him: and hence "glory and great beauty," that is, immense honour, "shalt thou lay upon him," in return for this, that there was laid upon him by his enemies a crown of thorns of so great ignominy, that at that time "there was no beauty in him, nor comeliness," as Isaias sang, chap. liii, 2.

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. FOR THOU SHALT GIVE HIM TO BE A BLESSING FOR EVER AND EVER: THOU SHALT MAKE HIM JOYFUL IN GLADNESS WITH THY COUNTENANCE.**

After the exaltation to the right hand of the Father, and the name above all names, there shall follow the universal

<!-- p. 115 (PDF 126) -->

blessing "of those that are in heaven, on earth, and under the earth;" which shall never fail, of which blessed Paul speaketh, *Philip.* ii, and John, *Apoc.* v. Those words, *thou shalt give him to be a blessing*, signify: thou shalt set him as the common matter of blessing, that is, thou shalt cause all to bless him. The Greek copy is clearer; for it hath, *dabis ei benedictionem* (thou shalt give him a blessing); but in the Hebrew there is, *pones eum benedictionem* (thou shalt set him a blessing); the sense is the same. Now that which is added, *thou shalt make him joyful in gladness with thy countenance*, signifieth the joy that followeth all those good things already spoken of; for joy always followeth the attaining of a good. That phrase, *with thy countenance*, signifieth[^2] *before thee*, and *in thy presence*.

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. FOR THE KING HOPETH IN THE LORD: AND THROUGH THE MERCY OF THE MOST HIGH HE SHALL NOT BE MOVED.**

He foretelleth that the good things already spoken of shall be altogether stable, and firm for ever: "For," saith he, "the king hopeth in the Lord," that is, he trusteth in the infinite power of God, not in human strength; "and through the mercy of the most High," that is, through the goodness of him who is above all, and to whom all things are subject; therefore "he shall not be moved," or shall not waver, but shall abide secure for ever.

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. LET THY HAND BE FOUND BY ALL THY ENEMIES: LET THY RIGHT HAND FIND OUT ALL THEM THAT HATE THEE.**

He proveth that Christ is not to be moved, nor his kingdom, because all his enemies shall perish. "Let thy hand be found by all thy enemies," that is, thy hand shall be found upon all thy enemies, namely that thou mayest punish them. He repeateth the same, saying: "Let thy right hand find out," *inveniat* (let it find) for *inveniet* (it shall find), "all them that hate thee." The Hebrew text is most clear: *Inveniet manus tua omnes inimicos tuos, dextera tua inveniet odientes te* (Thy hand shall find all thy enemies, thy right hand shall find them that hate thee). But the Interpreters chose to vary. Now the Prophet seemeth here to speak to Christ, not to the Father: for it is properly Christ whom the Jews and the other persecutors hate, and of him it is said in *Ps.* cix, 1: "Sit thou at my right hand: until I make thy enemies thy footstool."

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. THOU SHALT MAKE THEM AS AN OVEN OF FIRE, IN THE TIME OF THY ANGER: THE LORD SHALL TROUBLE THEM IN HIS WRATH, AND FIRE SHALL DEVOUR THEM.**

He describeth the punishment of the enemies: "Thou shalt make them," saith he, namely thy enemies, "as an oven of fire," that is, thou shalt make them to glow on every side, as a burning oven gloweth, "in the time of thy anger" (*in tempore vultus tui*, in the time of thy countenance), that is, in the day of thy wrath, which shall be made manifest in the last judgment. He repeateth the same in the third person, as if he said: Truly have I said: "Thou shalt make them as an oven of fire, in the time of thy anger." For the Lord Christ "shall trouble his enemies in his wrath," and then at the command of Christ eternal fire shall devour them, and so they shall be made as a burning oven. The Hebrew word בלע *balah* signifieth properly *to swallow down*, or *to destroy*, and *to scatter*. Therefore St. Jerome: *Dominus in furore suo præcipitabit eos* (The Lord in his fury shall cast them down headlong). Perhaps *conturbabit eos* (shall trouble them) is put for *exturbabit eos* (shall thrust them out), namely from his face into hell fire, and fire shall devour them. For so the Hebrew reading will agree with the Greek and the Latin. For perhaps the Hebrew word בלע *balah* signifieth not only *to swallow down* and *to destroy*, but also *to trouble*; for the force of the Hebrew words was better known to the Seventy Interpreters than to the later Rabbins. But the sense is always good.

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. THEIR FRUIT SHALT THOU DESTROY FROM THE EARTH: AND THEIR SEED FROM AMONG THE CHILDREN OF MEN.**

Lest perchance any one should object that the posterity of the enemies of Christ would at some time rise up in the place of their fathers, and bring trouble upon Christ, the Prophet addeth that not only the enemies of Christ, but all their sons and grandsons, and indeed all their seed shall perish. "Their fruit," saith he, that is, their sons and posterity, "shalt thou destroy from the earth," and cast out, "and their seed from among the children of men," namely thou shalt destroy.

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. FOR THEY HAVE INTENDED EVILS AGAINST THEE: THEY HAVE DEVISED COUNSELS WHICH THEY HAVE NOT BEEN ABLE TO ESTABLISH.**

Justly shalt thou punish them, because they unjustly endeavoured to hurt thee. David seemeth to have said very properly and subtly: "They have intended evils against thee" (*Declinaverunt in te mala*, they have turned evils aside upon thee), because the evils of punishment did not directly belong to Christ, in whom there was no fault; but wicked men wrested and turned aside the evils, that is, reproaches, wounds, stripes, death itself, as it were from their own road upon the innocent Christ. "They have devised counsels which they have not been able to establish," that is, there was in them an evil will of destroying Christ, and of hindering his kingdom; but they could not accomplish it, because God turned all the evils into the good of Christ himself, and of his faithful servants.

[Margin: The evils of punishment did not directly belong to Christ.]

[Margin: Verse 12.]

**12. FOR THOU SHALT MAKE THEM TURN THEIR BACK: IN THY REMNANTS THOU SHALT PREPARE THEIR FACE.**

Here is described the utmost calamity of the wicked, namely that they are appointed to stripes alone, and, to their greater calamity, are to see the elect of God in the highest glory and felicity. "Thou shalt make them turn their back" (*Pones eos dorsum*, thou shalt make them a back), that is, thou shalt cause them to be nothing but a back, namely the part exposed to stripes: so, to wit, shalt thou scourge them wholly, as if they were wholly a back. "In thy remnants thou shalt prepare," or *thou shalt direct*, as the Hebrew word תכנן soundeth, "their face," that is, thou shalt direct their face upon thy remnants, that is, to consider thy elect, whom thou hast left to thyself, of whom it is written, *Rom.* ix: "A remnant shall be saved." The Hebrew word מיתריך St. Jerome rendereth *funes tuos* (thy cords), but the Seventy Interpreters have rightly rendered *reliquiis tuis* (thy remnants), because that word signifieth both. This place is very obscure; if our interpretation please any one less, let him hear others. Theodoret and Euthymius expound thus: "Thou shalt make them turn their back," that is, thou shalt turn them to flight, causing

<!-- p. 116 (PDF 127) -->

them to turn their backs. "In thy remnants thou shalt prepare their face," that is, against their remnants, that is, against their children, "thou shalt prepare thy face," that is, thy wrath. For these authors will have it that there is a hyperbaton in the words of the Prophet, and that therefore the words are to be inverted in the manner we have said. Whether this interpretation cleaveth to the letter and the words, let the readers judge. St. Jerome and St. Augustine, and also the moderns, have each devised his own interpretation.

[Margin: Verse 13.]

**13. BE THOU EXALTED, O LORD, IN THY OWN STRENGTH: WE WILL SING AND PRAISE THY POWER.**

The Psalm is concluded with a holy acclamation to Christ the king, and at the same time with a prediction of those things which shall be after the last destruction of all the wicked. "Be thou exalted, O Lord, in thy own strength," that is, thou who once didst appear lowly, and as one weak didst suffer thyself to be crucified, now in strength and power, the enemies being vanquished and thrust down into hell fire, "be thou exalted" even to the highest heaven; meanwhile we thy elect "will sing" with the voice, and with instruments of music will sound forth thy power, that at length we may come to thee, and praise thee for ever and ever. Here it is only to be observed, that by *virtus* (strength, power) in either part of the verse are not understood the moral virtues, but power and might, as is manifestly gathered from the Hebrew and the Greek.

[^1]: Translator's note: Bellarmine does not number the title, so that his verses 1 to 13 are verses 2 to 14 of the Douay-Rheims Bible.

[^2]: Translator's note: the print has *siginificat* here, a misprint for *significat*.
