BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM XXXVII.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*A psalm for David, for a remembrance of the sabbath.*

The title of this Psalm in the Hebrew copy maketh no mention of the *sabbath,* but of the *remembrance* alone; yet it pleased the Seventy Interpreters, most grave men, for explanation's sake, to add the word *sabbath.* Now the explanation of St. Augustine, which St. Gregory also followeth, seemeth fit enough, that this Psalm, which is one of those that properly pertain to penance, was composed by David doing penance, for a remembrance of the *sabbath,* that is, of the rest lost in the state of innocence, or promised in the resurrection of the just. It might also be understood of the sabbath which a good conscience keepeth, while it enjoyeth the grace of God; and which he loseth, who sinneth; and which he remembereth with groaning, who beginneth to do true penance. The argument of the Psalm is the prayer of a penitent, like to that which is had in Psalm VI, which hath altogether the same beginning, and it is very probable that this prayer was composed at that time when David fled from Jerusalem by reason of the persecution of Absalom.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. REBUKE ME NOT, O LORD, IN THY INDIGNATION; NOR CHASTISE ME IN THY WRATH.**[^1]

David doing penance prayeth that God would not punish him in his wrath and indignation, as a judge doth with the guilty, but in mercy, as a physician dealeth with the sick. See what we have said at the beginning of *Ps.* vi, where we shewed that there is no difference in this place between indignation and wrath, and between rebuking and chastising. Yet if it please thee to admit a difference, thou mayest say with St. Augustine and St. Gregory, that they are rebuked in indignation, who are condemned to hell fire; that they are chastised in wrath, who after death are tormented in the fire of purgatory: and that the Prophet prayeth God to punish him here for the sins committed, not in purgatory, or hell fire. And St. Augustine warneth that the fire of purgatory is not to be contemned, because that fire is more grievous than whatsoever a man can suffer in this life. This also is to be added, that although in this place, and in *Ps.* ii, verse 5, and *Ps.* vi, verse 1, the wrath of God is taken for condemnation out of justice, yet elsewhere it is sometimes taken for the zeal of a father, who is angry with his children, not that he may destroy, but that he may keep them.

[Margin: The fire of purgatory not to be contemned.]

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. FOR THY ARROWS ARE FASTENED IN ME: AND THY HAND HATH BEEN STRONG UPON ME.**

Because the Prophet knew that nothing is more profitable for obtaining the pardon of sins, than fully to know the misery of sin, and to bewail it with the Lord; he bewaileth in this verse and some that follow the infelicity which mortal sin bringeth with it. He saith therefore: "Rebuke me not in thy wrath," for it is most grievous, as I have now learnt by experience. For "thy arrows are fastened in me," that is, many tribulations, thou punishing my sin, have befallen me; "and thy hand hath been strong upon me," that is, thy arrows are fastened in me, and have not only touched me lightly, because thou hast made thy hand heavy upon me, that the arrows might pierce the deeper. Now by these tribulations and punishments he seemeth to understand the death of the son born of Bethsabee, the violation of his daughter done by her own brother, the slaying of his son Amnon, the incest and adultery of his wives committed by his own son, his own driving out from the kingdom, and other things of that kind, which for his sin, the Lord taking vengeance, he suffered. Perhaps also he calleth arrows those words of rebuke and threatening, which he heard from Nathan the messenger of God, II *Kings* xii: "Thus saith the Lord the God of Israel: I anointed thee king over Israel, and I delivered thee from the hand of Saul, and gave thee thy master's house and thy master's wives into thy bosom, and gave thee the house of Israel and Juda. Why therefore hast thou despised the word of the Lord, to do evil in my sight? Thou hast killed Urias the Hethite with the sword, and hast taken his wife to be thy wife. Therefore the sword shall never depart from thy house," etc. Surely that upbraiding of benefits, and threatening of punishments, without doubt pricked the mind of David most grievously, and filled him with shame, fear and most vehement sorrow.

[Margin: How many and how great things David suffered for his adultery.]

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. THERE IS NO HEALTH IN MY FLESH, BECAUSE OF THY WRATH: THERE IS NO PEACE FOR MY BONES, BECAUSE OF MY SINS.**

He describeth the effect of the arrows of the Lord, and saith that he is vehemently troubled, and cannot rest, while he thinketh again on the wrath of God and his own sins, which have deserved

<!-- p. 241 (PDF 252) -->

that wrath. "There is no health, saith he, in my flesh, because of thy wrath (*a facie iræ tuæ*, from the face of thy wrath)," that is, the sight of thy wrath, which is ever before the eyes of my mind, maketh my flesh to mourn and waste away. For sorrow of mind afflicteth and weakeneth the flesh also, and forceth it to grow pale and languish. "There is no peace for my bones, because of my sins," that is, the sight of the deformity and grievousness of my fault so troubleth me, that I have no rest, and even my very bones tremble.

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. FOR MY INIQUITIES ARE GONE OVER MY HEAD: AND AS A HEAVY BURDEN ARE BECOME HEAVY UPON ME.**

He giveth the reason why he is so greatly troubled, while he looketh upon his sins, and saith the reason is, because the sins are many and great. Of the multitude he saith: "For my iniquities are gone over my head," that is, they have grown to so great a heap, that they as it were overwhelm me: as he is overwhelmed with waters, who entereth a river so deep, that the waters go over his head. Of the greatness he saith: "And as a heavy burden are become heavy upon me," that is, my sins as an insupportable weight press upon the shoulders of my soul, because it is above my strength to make satisfaction for so heavy a debt. This is clearer in the Hebrew text, where that, *super me* (upon me), signifieth properly, *super vires meas* (above my strength), or more heavily than I can bear. The sin of David was adultery joined with murder, but the true penitent considereth very many deformities in it. For David offended a faithful servant, from whom he took away wife and life: he offended Bethsabee, whom he led to sin, and spiritually slew: he offended his own wives, to whom he kept not faith: he offended the whole people, nay even the unbelievers themselves, by evil example, whence Nathan said, III *Kings* xii: "Thou hast made the enemies to blaspheme the name of the Lord;"[^2] lastly he offended God, whose laws he openly transgressed. Therefore if so many offences and so many faults be numbered, as were the persons whom David offended by that his sin, not without cause may the same David say: "My iniquities are gone over my head." Next the grievousness of the sin may be known from the circumstances; for David slew Urias, first, an innocent man; secondly, a most faithful one; thirdly, one who was then fighting for David; fourthly, because he had defiled his wife by adultery, as though he would, because he had done him a wrong, do him a yet greater wrong; fifthly, because he would have him himself to be the minister of his own death; sixthly, because when he wrote to Joab, that he should slay Urias, he would have it believed that he was guilty of some great crime, and in that manner stained his good name. But ingratitude towards God is the chief cause of the grievousness of David's sin. For God bestowed most abundantly upon David alone well-nigh all gifts temporal and spiritual. For he made him a great king, an excellent prophet, a most valiant commander; he endowed him with prudence, might, beauty, riches, all the goods which can be desired by men: all which surely made his sin most grievous, which when he himself weighed in the balance of most diligent consideration, he said with good cause: "My iniquities as a heavy burden are become heavy upon me." And this is the cause why few perceive from their sins the sense of sorrow which were meet, because few weigh the greatness of sin in the balance of serious consideration.

[Margin: How grievous the sin of David was.]

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. MY SORES ARE PUTRIFIED AND CORRUPTED, BECAUSE OF MY FOOLISHNESS.**

These things pertain to that time which passed between the sin of adultery, and the admonition of Nathan the prophet, which was more than nine months. For Nathan corrected David, after the child conceived of that adultery had been born, as is said II *Kings* xii. All that time therefore David put off healing the wounds of his sin by penance. But meanwhile there had been drawn over them a certain scar, as it were, of forgetfulness, which suffered not the wound to be known; but in truth those scars were putrifying, and the wounds were becoming more grievous, and more incurable, and this is what he now bewaileth, saying: "My sores (*cicatrices*, scars)," not drawn over by the art of a physician healing, but by negligence and forgetfulness, "are putrified and corrupted, because of my foolishness," that is, my foolishness, which was the cause that I heeded not my wounds, the same was also the cause that the wounds themselves, being putrified, spread the stench of scandal far and wide. By scars in this place are not understood the marks which remain after the healing of a stripe, but there is understood a stripe not healed, which is wont to close after the manner of a scar, and because the corrupted blood hath not been pressed out of it, "nor is it fomented with oil, or bound up," as is said *Isa.* i; therefore it putrifieth, and is corrupted. This is gathered from the original text, where there is the word חבורות *chaburoth,* which signifieth not *cicatrices* (scars) properly so called, but *livorem* (a bruise), and a swelling *plagam* (stripe): wherefore either in the Latin edition scars are taken for stripes, or, as we have said, for scars which are formed of themselves, while the wound is not healed, but closeth of itself, and the stripe is neglected and corrupted.

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. I AM BECOME MISERABLE, AND AM BOWED DOWN EVEN TO THE END: I WALKED SORROWFUL ALL THE DAY LONG.**

From that corruption it came to pass that David became miserable and bowed; which may be understood in two ways, first of the fault, secondly of the punishment. For he who sinneth grievously, especially by the sin of luxury, by this very thing becometh miserable, because, God being forsaken, in whom is our beatitude, he is bowed down to the earth, and becometh like to the beasts, and by this miserable, and very miserable: and this is what that, *even to the end,* signifieth, namely, I am become miserable and bowed down so far, that I could not become more miserable and more bowed down, since from the delectation of

[Margin: The sin of luxury maketh a man bowed and very miserable.]

<!-- p. 242 (PDF 253) -->

angels I have been bowed down to the delights of cattle. This is gathered from the Hebrew text, where there is שחותי עד מאוד, *miser et curvatus sum usque valde* (I am miserable and bowed down even exceedingly). Wherefore that, *in finem* (to the end), signifieth not, *usque ad finem vitæ* (even to the end of life), or *usque ad finem temporis* (even to the end of time), or *usque in œternum* (even for ever), but *usque ad finem curvationis* (even to the end of bowing down), so that I cannot be bowed down more. For in the eighth verse, where we read: "I am humbled exceedingly," there is the same Hebrew word, which the Interpreter translateth now, *usque in finem,* now *nimis* (exceedingly), or *valde* (very much). It may also be understood of the punishment; for he who sinneth by the sin of luxury is made miserable, and bowed down exceedingly by reason of the remorse of conscience, the fear of the divine wrath, and a shame humbling and confounding, so that such a man dareth not lift up his eyes to heaven. But both understandings may also be joined together, so that the sense is: I am become miserable with the misery of fault and of punishment, and bowed down exceedingly, both because the face of my soul, which I ought to have had fixed on God, I have turned to carnal and lowest delights; and because for shame I cannot look upon heaven, and humble and abject am forced to look upon the earth; and for all these things, "I walked sorrowful all the day long," that is, my conscience always biting and accusing, I went sad and mourning: for what gladness can there be to a miserable man, if he know his misery?

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. FOR MY LOINS ARE FILLED WITH ILLUSIONS; AND THERE IS NO HEALTH IN MY FLESH.**

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. I AM AFFLICTED AND HUMBLED EXCEEDINGLY: I ROARED WITH THE GROANING OF MY HEART.**

The Prophet passeth from his own particular sin to the general corruption, which he experienced in his flesh from the sin of the first parent, whence that particular sin of his had flowed as from a fountain: and from that corruption he saith that he is afflicted and humbled, and continually groaneth and roareth. "For, saith he, my loins," that is, that bestial and concupiscent part, the bridle of original justice being broken, bringeth forth perpetually shameful and filthy desires, and therefore is filled with the illusions of wicked spirits; "and there is no health in my flesh," because good dwelleth not in it, but the evil of divers passions, which make it infirm; therefore "I am afflicted and humbled exceedingly," because I am ashamed that I, a man endowed with reason, cannot be free from such bestial motions; and therefore "I roared" for sorrow, provoked "by the inward groaning of my heart" to outward roaring and crying. I know that the Rabbins, and they who follow them, fable I know not what of a bodily and stinking disease, with which David laboured in the shameful parts of the body; but our holy Fathers Ambrose, Jerome, Augustine and Gregory in their commentaries on this place refer these verses to carnal concupiscence, so that this complaint of David is like to that which is read in the Apostle, *Rom.* vii: "I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and captivating me in the law of sin. Unhappy man that I am, who shall deliver me?" etc. St. Ambrose and St. Augustine read, *anima mea impleta est illusionibus* (my soul is filled with illusions), where we read, *lumbi mei impleti sunt illusionibus* (my loins are filled with illusions). But this happened, because with the Greeks loins are called ψυχι,[^3] and souls are called ψυχαι, a single letter being added. Wherefore it was easy for the Greek text to be corrupted, by reason of the likeness of the letters, of which there is none in the Hebrew text. Therefore that is the reading which agreeth with the Hebrew and Latin text. And that the Hebrew word, for which we have *illusionibus,* seemeth to signify properly *ignominiam* (ignominy), as Jerome translateth, changeth not the sense: for he who is filled with ignominy is exposed to derision. But that St. Jerome translateth *evigilavi* (I awoke), for that which we have *afflictus sum* (I am afflicted), I see not what he followed. For the word which we now have in the Hebrew text signifieth properly *debilitari* (to be weakened); and to be weakened and to be afflicted may be taken for the same.

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. LORD, ALL MY DESIRE IS BEFORE THEE, AND MY GROANING IS NOT HIDDEN FROM THEE.**

Because he had said that the groaning of his heart stirred up roaring, he declareth to whom he groaneth in heart, and saith that he groaneth to him who searcheth hearts, and knoweth what the spirit desireth. "Lord, saith he, all my desire is before thee," that is, thou, Lord, alone seest all my desire, which is nothing else, but that at length I may be delivered from evil concupiscence, that I may come to the sabbath of perfect rest; and concerning this thing "my" inward "groaning is not hidden from thee." The Apostle saith something like, when he saith, *Rom.* viii: "Even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption of the sons of God, the redemption of our body."

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. MY HEART IS TROUBLED, MY STRENGTH HATH LEFT ME, AND THE LIGHT OF MY EYES ITSELF IS NOT WITH ME.**

He goeth on to describe the corruption of human nature, and saith: "My heart is troubled," which signifieth the intestine war from the rebellion of the inferior part against the superior, and he addeth: "My strength hath left me," because from that rebellion is born so great infirmity, that a man, will he, nill he, suffereth evil desires, and saith with the Apostle, *Rom.* vii: "To will is present with me; but to accomplish, I find not." He addeth lastly: "And the light of my eyes itself is not with me," because not only infirmity in the will, but also ignorance in the mind hath arisen from the same rebellion. For often we judge of things, not as they are, but as they seem to us being ill affected, as they who have a fever judge what is sweet to be bitter, and what is bitter sweet. Therefore he saith not: "The light of my eyes is put out," but "is not with me;" for the light of understanding and of reason[^4] is indeed in the rational soul; but because the body which is corrupted is a load upon the soul, being often hindered by carnal passions, we cannot use it, therefore he saith: "And the light of my eyes, itself,

[Margin: The evils which follow the sin of luxury.]

<!-- p. 243 (PDF 254) -->

I say, the light of my eyes," namely the inward, "is not with me," while I work, though it be in me. Wherefore it is in me as to essence, it is not with me as to use.

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. MY FRIENDS AND MY NEIGHBOURS HAVE DRAWN NEAR, AND STOOD AGAINST ME.**

After he hath described the intestine war, which is continually waged within man, he describeth also outward wars, persecutions and detractions, all which are punishments of sins. And first he complaineth that friends and neighbours were made his enemies, which happened chiefly in the rebellion of his son Absalom, with whom many of the friends and neighbours of David conspired against him. But the modern expositors expound from the Hebrew text far otherwise than the truth of the Vulgate edition hath; the Hebrew text now hath, אהבי ורעי מנגד נגעי יעמדו, *amici mei et proximi mei e regione lepræ meæ steterunt* (my friends and my neighbours stood over against my leprosy). In which manner also St. Jerome almost translateth, from which many gather that in this place it is only signified that the friends and neighbours of David were not indeed made his enemies, but durst not come to him to help, or comfort him; and therefore stood afar over against him. But since the version of the Seventy Interpreters hath ἤγγισαν, and the ancient Latin Interpreter hath rendered, *appropinquaverunt* (have drawn near), surely it seemeth too hard to say, not *appropinquaverunt,* but *longe steterunt* (stood afar off); nor doth the Hebrew word hinder: for that, נגעי, which truly signifieth *lepræ meæ* (of my leprosy), or *plagæ meæ* (of my stripe), the Seventy Interpreters, who had more correct copies, read נגעו, *appropinquaverunt.* For the letters are the same, except the last, which is most easily changed into the other, since they differ not, save that one is a little greater than the other. For *iod,* if it be a little lengthened, becometh *vau,* and therefore by the fault of copyists it most easily happened that one was put for the other. But, say they, in the following verse it is said, "stood afar off." It is true, but the speech is not of the same persons, as we shall presently say.

[Margin: Verse 12.]

**12. AND THEY THAT WERE NEAR ME STOOD AFAR OFF: AND THEY THAT SOUGHT MY SOUL USED VIOLENCE.**

He hath said that friends and neighbours, as Absalom and his companions, were made enemies, and drew near and stood against him, that they might hurt him; now he saith that others, who were wont to be near him, as soldiers, or servants, withdrew far off, and would not be present in the time of persecution; and that meanwhile his enemies endeavoured with great violence to take away his life, I *Kings* ii.[^5] Nor had David only those "that sought his soul," that is, who sought to slay him, as the soldiers of Absalom; but he had also those who detracted from his honour and good name, as Semei, "who cursed him with a grievous curse," and others, who gave deceitful counsels against him, as Achitophel and others; therefore he subjoineth:

[Margin: Verse 13.]

**13. AND THEY THAT SOUGHT EVILS TO ME SPOKE VAIN THINGS, AND STUDIED DECEITS ALL THE DAY LONG.**

[Margin: Verse 14.]

**14. BUT I, AS A DEAF MAN, HEARD NOT: AND AS A DUMB MAN NOT OPENING HIS MOUTH.**

[Margin: Verse 15.]

**15. AND I BECAME AS A MAN THAT HEARETH NOT: AND THAT HATH NO REPROOFS IN HIS MOUTH.**

These things are plain and most true, which may chiefly be understood from book II of *Kings,* chap. xvi, where when Semei cursed David, and called him a man of Belial, and an invader of the kingdom, David bore with him with incredible patience, nor permitted that any should hurt him or reprove him; so "he became as a deaf man, and one that heareth not, and as a dumb man, and one that hath no reproofs in his mouth."

[Margin: Verse 16.]

**16. FOR IN THEE, O LORD, HAVE I HOPED: THOU WILT HEAR ME, O LORD MY GOD.**

He giveth three reasons why he was thus deaf and dumb: the first is, because he judged it more profitable for himself to hope in the Lord, than in his own defence. He saith therefore: I was dumb, "for in thee, O Lord, have I hoped;" I heeded not how many false and vain things they cast upon me, because I knew that all things are heard and known by thee, in whom I have always hoped, who art a just judge, and renderest to every one according to his works; and because "in thee have I hoped, thou wilt hear me, O Lord my God," and wilt deliver me from wicked lips, and a deceitful tongue.

[Margin: Verse 17.]

**17. FOR I SAID: LEST AT ANY TIME MY ENEMIES REJOICE OVER ME: AND WHILST MY FEET ARE MOVED, THEY SPEAK GREAT THINGS AGAINST ME.**

This is the second reason why he chose to be deaf and dumb: "For I said," saith he, with myself: "Lest at any time my enemies rejoice over me," that is, it is good for me to have patience, and to hope in the help of the Lord, lest perchance, if I be impatient, and render cursing for cursing, God forsake me, and my enemies rejoice over me, that is, be glad over my ruin. "And whilst my feet are moved, they speak great things against me," that is, not without cause do I fear lest my enemies be glad of my destruction; for "whilst my feet are moved," that is, whilst my feet begin to totter, and I incline to a fall (which came to pass when he was in danger of losing the kingdom), "my enemies spoke great things against me," threatening me, and foretelling utter destruction. Now it is to be observed in this place, that the particle *et* (and) is often put in the Scriptures for *quia* (because), as *Isa.* lxiii: "For thou art our father, and Abraham hath not known us, and Israel hath been ignorant of us," that is, thou alone, O Lord, art our father: for Abraham and Jacob, who seemed to be our fathers, acknowledge us not for children. So also chap. lxiv, 5: "Thou art angry, and we have sinned against thee,"[^6] that is, because we have sinned against thee. So therefore in this place it is said: "And whilst

[Margin: *Et* for *quia* frequent in Scripture.]

<!-- p. 244 (PDF 255) -->

my feet are moved," that *et* is put for *quia,* so that the sense is: lest at any time my enemies glory over me. "And whilst my feet are moved," that is, because whilst my feet are moved, etc. That also, *dum commoventur* (whilst they are moved), is put for *dum commoverentur* (whilst they were moved). For in the Hebrew and Greek text it is had by the infinitive, *in commoveri pedes meos* (in my feet being moved), which may be translated, *dum commoventur,* and *dum commovebantur,* and *dum commoverentur;* wherefore it ought to be expounded according to the requirement of the verb following, that the speech may hang together.

[Margin: Verse 18.]

**18. FOR I AM READY FOR SCOURGES: AND MY SORROW IS CONTINUALLY BEFORE ME.**

He addeth a third reason why he would be deaf and dumb[^7] before his adversaries: "For, saith he, I" for my sins "am ready to be scourged," not only with the scourges of words and detractions, but also with the scourges of blows and stripes. For the *sorrow* which I have deserved is ever before the eyes of my mind, or because *my sorrow,* that is my sin, which is to me the cause of continual sorrow, never departeth from the eyes of my heart. In the Hebrew is the word צלע, which some have translated *claudicationem* (halting); others think that צרע ought to be read, that is *lepram* (leprosy); we have *flagella* (scourges), from the Greek; but there is no difficulty: for that word צלע commonly signifieth *latus* (side), or *costam* (rib); but it is transferred to signify *claudicationem,* because the lame, while they walk, lean to one side, and go not upright. It is transferred also to signify *plagam* (a stripe), or *attritionem* (a bruising), and by this also *flagellum* (a scourge), because stripes are received from scourges. Therefore St. Jerome translateth: *Ego ad plagas paratus sum* (I am ready for stripes), and the Seventy Interpreters: *Ego autem in flagella paratus sum* (But I am ready for scourges).

[Margin: Verse 19.]

**19. FOR I WILL DECLARE MY INIQUITY: AND I WILL THINK FOR MY SIN.**

He giveth the reason why he said he was ready for scourges: "For, saith he, I will declare my iniquity," that is, because I acknowledge and confess that I have sinned, and have deserved scourges; "and I will think for my sin," namely to expiate it. St. Jerome translateth more expressively from the Hebrew: *Et sollicitus ero pro peccato meo* (And I will be solicitous for my sin), that is, I will think diligently and anxiously, how I may make satisfaction to the Lord and obtain full pardon. Which indeed is a wholesome lesson for penitents, that they be solicitous for the expiation of sin, and gladly accept the occasions offered by God of exercising patience.

[Margin: A wholesome lesson for penitents.]

[Margin: Verse 20.]

**20. BUT MY ENEMIES LIVE, AND ARE STRONGER THAN I: AND THEY THAT HATE ME WRONGFULLY ARE MULTIPLIED.**

After the Prophet hath set forth the reasons why he was deaf and dumb before his adversaries, that by patience he might appease God, he opposeth to his own patience the contrary malice of his adversaries. For whereas he himself rendered not evil for evil, they on the contrary repaid evil for good: and yet they lived, and exulted, and were strengthened. Which things are mentioned by David to this end, that he may the more easily incline God to mercy. "But my enemies live, and are stronger than I (*confirmati sunt super me*)," that is, I indeed am humbled and afflicted, and yet I keep patience, as if I were deaf and dumb: but meanwhile "my enemies live," that is, they are cheerful and exult, "and are stronger than I," that is, they are become stronger and mightier, "and are multiplied," that is, and have grown also as to number, "they that hate me wrongfully," that is, they live, and are strengthened, "and multiplied, that hate me," without any just cause. In the Hebrew is שנאי שקר, *qui oderunt me mendaciter* (who hate me lyingly), that is, for a false and feigned cause. For these things seem to be referred to the conspiracy of Absalom, who by his lie had persuaded the people that the king had not appointed any to judge justly; but that he himself would judge most justly, if he were appointed king. For hence it came to pass that the people rebelled, and with their whole heart followed Absalom, as is said, book II of *Kings,* chap. xv.

[Margin: Verse 21.]

**21. THEY THAT RENDER EVIL FOR GOOD, HAVE DETRACTED ME, BECAUSE I FOLLOWED GOODNESS.**

He proveth what he said, that he was hated by his enemies without just cause, saying: "They that rendered evil for good detracted me," as if he said: Truly my enemies hate me wrongfully; for they detracted me, who render evil for good, such as were Absalom his son, and Achitophel his counsellor. For Absalom had received very many good things from David, and a little before had obtained pardon of fratricide, and yet he detracted David his father as negligent and unjust, saying to those who came to the king's judgment: "Thy words seem to me just and good, but there is no man appointed by the king to hear thee," II *Kings* xv. So also Achitophel had received from David great honour, to be the chief counsellor of the kingdom, and yet, forgetful of the benefit, he fell away from David to Absalom, and gave to Absalom most iniquitous counsels against the same David, II *Kings* xvi and xvii. Nor did they only detract me, who render evil for good, but also (which is more grievous) "they detracted me, because I followed goodness," that is, because I dealt with all men with a sincere heart and a good will, and in that manner was contrary to their iniquitous desires and works.

[Margin: Verse 22.]

**22. FORSAKE ME NOT, O LORD MY GOD: DO NOT THOU DEPART FROM ME.**

[Margin: Verse 23.]

**23. ATTEND UNTO MY HELP, O LORD, THE GOD OF MY SALVATION.**

He concludeth from those things which he hath said, and prayeth God to be present to him with his protection, and as it were repeateth the same which he said in the beginning of the Psalm: for then

<!-- p. 245 (PDF 256) -->

doth God punish any man most grievously in his wrath, or indignation, when he leaveth him destitute of his grace, and departeth as from an enemy, and setteth him among foes naked of his help. As therefore he had said in the beginning of the Psalm: "Rebuke me not, O Lord, in thy indignation," so now in the end he concludeth: "Forsake me not, O Lord my God," that is, let not thy grace forsake me, seeing that thou art my Lord, who didst create me, and my God, who didst make me for thee, the supreme good. "Do not thou depart from me," as from an enemy; but rather, as a father, "attend unto my help," that is, apply thyself diligently to my aid, thou "O Lord, the God of my salvation," that is, thou who art the author of my salvation, from whom alone I expect salvation, and in whom alone I trust. And this indeed hath seemed to me the literal sense of this Psalm: yet because the holy Fathers Chrysostom, Augustine, Jerome and Gregory expound either the whole Psalm, or at least from the middle to the end, of Christ; it is to be known that the whole Psalm may be explained of David: first, as it properly agreeth to the person of David, then as David bore the person of Christ. For the Holy Ghost can intend several things together in the same words, namely to relate the tribulations of David, and at the same time to foretell the sufferings of Christ, who was to be the son of David according to the flesh. Since therefore we have expounded the Psalm of the person of David, let us also briefly shew how the same Psalm may be applied to Christ.

[Margin: This Psalm agreeth to David as he bore the person of Christ.]

## ANOTHER EXPLANATION OF THE SAME PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. REBUKE ME NOT, O LORD, IN THY INDIGNATION; NOR CHASTISE ME IN THY WRATH.**

Christ speaketh for his body, which is the Church, and asketh deliverance for it.

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. FOR THY ARROWS ARE FASTENED IN ME: AND THY HAND HATH BEEN STRONG UPON ME.**

He saith that he justly asketh the deliverance of the Church, because he hath taken upon himself the arrows of the wrath of God, which pertained to her.

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. THERE IS NO HEALTH IN MY FLESH, BECAUSE OF THY WRATH: THERE IS NO PEACE FOR MY BONES, BECAUSE OF MY SINS.**

He describeth his passion in general, by reason of which from the sole of the foot unto the top of the head there was no health in him. And whereas he saith: because of *my* sins, it is understood of sins, not which he himself did, but which he took upon himself to be punished in him.

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. FOR MY INIQUITIES ARE GONE OVER MY HEAD: AND AS A HEAVY BURDEN ARE BECOME HEAVY UPON ME.**

He saith that therefore there was no health in his flesh from the sole of the foot unto the top of the head, because the sins which he took to be expiated are so many, and so grievous, that they have gone over his head by their multitude, and were like lead by reason of their weight.

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. MY SORES ARE PUTRIFIED AND CORRUPTED, BECAUSE OF MY FOOLISHNESS.**

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. I AM BECOME MISERABLE, AND AM BOWED DOWN EVEN TO THE END: I WALKED SORROWFUL ALL THE DAY LONG.**

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. FOR MY LOINS ARE FILLED WITH ILLUSIONS; AND THERE IS NO HEALTH IN MY FLESH.**

These things he saith for his body, bewailing the corruption of mankind, as when one saith: I have pain in my feet, hands, or stomach; it is the head that speaketh, but it speaketh not of pain which it suffereth in itself, but which it hath in its members, and in its body.

[Margin: Verse 8.]

**8. I AM AFFLICTED AND HUMBLED EXCEEDINGLY: I ROARED WITH THE GROANING OF MY HEART.**

Here he beginneth to recount his passion in order. For these words pertain to the prayer made in the garden.

[Margin: Verse 9.]

**9. LORD, ALL MY DESIRE IS BEFORE THEE, AND MY GROANING IS NOT HIDDEN FROM THEE.**

[Margin: Verse 10.]

**10. MY HEART IS TROUBLED, MY STRENGTH HATH LEFT ME, AND THE LIGHT OF MY EYES ITSELF IS NOT WITH ME.**

They pertain to the same prayer, in which he desired the chalice to pass, and began to be troubled, to fear, to be heavy, and to be sad. And that he might experience the bitterness of the passion at hand, he would not that the strength and light of divine consolation should flow into the inferior part, so that even an angel from heaven appeared strengthening him.

[Margin: Verse 11.]

**11. MY FRIENDS AND MY NEIGHBOURS HAVE DRAWN NEAR, AND STOOD AGAINST ME.**

This was fulfilled in Judas the friend, and the Jews the neighbours, when they apprehended him.

[Margin: Verse 12.]

**12. AND THEY THAT WERE NEAR ME STOOD AFAR OFF.**

This was fulfilled in the Apostles, who fled, and in Peter, who followed afar off.

**AND THEY THAT SOUGHT MY SOUL USED VIOLENCE.**

It was fulfilled in the council of the priests, carefully seeking false witness, that they might destroy him.

[Margin: Verse 13.]

**13. AND THEY THAT SOUGHT EVILS TO ME SPOKE VAIN THINGS, AND STUDIED DECEITS ALL THE DAY LONG.**

They pertain to the same council.

<!-- p. 246 (PDF 257) -->

[Margin: Verse 14.]

**14. BUT I, AS A DEAF MAN, HEARD NOT: AND AS A DUMB MAN NOT OPENING HIS MOUTH.**

[Margin: Verse 15.]

**15. AND I BECAME AS A MAN THAT HEARETH NOT: AND THAT HATH NO REPROOFS IN HIS MOUTH.**

They were fulfilled to the letter in Christ, who before Caiphas first, then before Pilate and Herod, brought no defence, but as a lamb before his shearer was dumb, as Isaias also foretold, chap. liii.

[Margin: Verse 16.]

**16. FOR IN THEE, O LORD, HAVE I HOPED: THOU WILT HEAR ME, O LORD MY GOD.**

It pertaineth to the same silence; for therefore he was silent before men, because he was not silent before God, from whom he expected the recompense, the salvation of his people.

[Margin: Verse 17.]

**17. FOR I SAID: LEST AT ANY TIME MY ENEMIES REJOICE OVER ME: AND WHILST MY FEET ARE MOVED, THEY SPEAK GREAT THINGS AGAINST ME.**

Christ kept unconquered patience, lest his enemies should rejoice at his impatience, who, whilst his feet were moved, that is, whilst his infirmity was for a time shewn, spoke great things against him, saying, *John* xix,[^8] *Luke* xxiii: "If he were not a malefactor, we would not have delivered him up to thee;" and: "We have found this man perverting our nation."

[Margin: Verse 18.]

**18. FOR I AM READY FOR SCOURGES: AND MY SORROW IS CONTINUALLY BEFORE ME.**

We know it fulfilled to the letter in the scourging, and buffeting, and crowning with thorns.

[Margin: Verse 19.]

**19. FOR I WILL DECLARE MY INIQUITY: AND I WILL THINK FOR MY SIN.**

He saith that he will declare his sin, which, as hath been said, he did not, but took upon himself to be expiated: and that he will think, that is, will be solicitous for the utter blotting out of it; which he did when "he bore our sins in his body upon the tree," as St. Peter writeth in *Epist.* I, chap. ii.

[Margin: Verse 20.]

**20. BUT MY ENEMIES LIVE, AND ARE STRONGER THAN I: AND THEY THAT HATE ME WRONGFULLY ARE MULTIPLIED.**

This was fulfilled, when the princes and priests, thinking that they had now obtained the victory, exulted, and insulted him crucified.

[Margin: Verse 21.]

**21. THEY THAT RENDER EVIL FOR GOOD, HAVE DETRACTED ME, BECAUSE I FOLLOWED GOODNESS.**

Namely when they said to him hanging on the cross: "Vah, thou that destroyest the temple of God;" and: "Let him now come down from the cross," etc., *Matt.* xxvii.

[Margin: Verse 22.]

**22. FORSAKE ME NOT, O LORD MY GOD: DO NOT THOU DEPART FROM ME.**

This the Lord spoke in express words on the cross, when he said: "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" *Matt.* xxvii. As if he said: If thou hast forsaken me for a time, as to consolation, forsake me not as to the resurrection.

[Margin: Verse 23.]

**23. ATTEND UNTO MY HELP, O LORD, THE GOD OF MY SALVATION.**

"Leave not my soul in hell, nor give thy holy one to see corruption;" but "attend unto my help, making known to me the ways of life, and filling me with joy with thy countenance," *Ps.* xv.
