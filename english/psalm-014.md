BELLARMINE'S EXPLANATION.

PSALM XIV.

## TITLE AND ARGUMENT.

*Unto the end, a psalm of David.*[^1]

The title hath nothing new. But the argument is the knowledge of salvation: for in this Psalm is described the way to eternal life.

## EXPLANATION OF THE PSALM.

[Margin: Verse 1.]

**1. LORD, WHO SHALL DWELL IN THY TABERNACLE? OR WHO SHALL REST IN THY HOLY HILL?**

From the Hebrew some render: *Quis peregrinabitur in tabernaculo tuo, aut quis habitabit in monte sancto tuo?* (Who shall sojourn in thy tabernacle, or who shall dwell in thy holy hill?) But the Hebrew word גור *gur* signifieth both, *to sojourn* and *to dwell*, as also שכן *shacan* signifieth *to dwell* and *to rest*. Therefore neither

<!-- p. 73 (PDF 84) -->

the Greek version nor the Latin can be blamed. The Prophet alludeth to mount Sion, and to the tabernacle of God, which was on that mount; but in truth by these names he signifieth the heavenly Jerusalem, where is the tabernacle of God not made with hands. For the Prophets preached the kingdom of heaven by figures of this kind. Whence Blessed Paul, *Heb.* viii and ix, often maketh mention of the heavenly tabernacle, and in chap. xii calleth the very dwelling-place of heaven mount Sion; and Blessed John in the *Apocalypse*, chap. xiv, maketh mention of the heavenly mount Sion, and in chap. xxi saith: "Behold the tabernacle of God with men, and he will dwell with them," etc. The Prophet therefore asketh who it is that shall dwell, that is, shall enjoy a stable abode in the heavenly tabernacle, or shall rest on the top of the most high mountain, on which all the labour of the ascent is ended, since above it there is nothing? For here on earth there can be neither a stable abode, nor true rest.

[Margin: Verse 2.]

**2. HE THAT WALKETH WITHOUT BLEMISH, AND WORKETH JUSTICE.**

[Margin: Venial sin doth not properly leave a blemish.]

This is a compendious and summary answer, as if he said: "He that turneth away" from evil, and doth good, or he that offendeth God neither by the commission of a fault, nor by the omission of justice. He is said to walk without blemish, who walketh in the way of this life, that is, who liveth without mortal fault. For venial sin doth not properly leave a blemish. He is said to work justice, who doth all the things to which he is bound, not from fear of punishment, but from love of justice.

[Margin: Verse 3.]

**3. HE THAT SPEAKETH TRUTH IN HIS HEART, WHO HATH NOT USED DECEIT IN HIS TONGUE.**

He explaineth more in particular who shall dwell in the tabernacle of the Lord, and saith that it is he who worketh evil neither with heart, nor with mouth, nor with deed. Of the heart he saith: "He that speaketh truth in his heart." For every one who esteemeth temporal things at more than they are worth doth not speak truth in his heart, and whosoever consenteth to any sin speaketh falsehood in his heart, because he judgeth that to be expedient for him which in truth is not expedient for him. Thus all the sins of the heart can be reduced, as to their root, to a false judgment. Of the mouth he saith: Who "hath not used deceit in his tongue"; for detractions as well as flatteries, and other sins of the tongue of that kind, can be referred to deceit. Of the deed he saith in the following verse:

[Margin: Verse 4.]

**4. NOR HATH DONE EVIL TO HIS NEIGHBOUR: NOR TAKEN UP A REPROACH AGAINST HIS NEIGHBOURS.**

[Margin: Verse 5.]

**5. IN HIS SIGHT THE MALIGNANT IS BROUGHT TO NOTHING: BUT HE GLORIFIETH THEM THAT FEAR THE LORD.**

He that shall dwell in the tabernacle of the Lord not only hath not himself done evil; but neither hath he permitted, as far as in him lay, that others should do evil. Therefore he hath not taken up a reproach against his neighbours, that is, he hath not admitted to his ears revilings, detractions, whisperings, calumnies against his neighbours, and hath never favoured the malignant, but hath despised them, and held them for nothing: on the other hand he hath glorified, honoured and helped good men, who fear God. Great is this praise, to hate evil not only in oneself, but also in others.

[Margin: Verse 6.]

**6. HE THAT SWEARETH TO HIS NEIGHBOUR, AND DECEIVETH NOT; HE THAT HATH NOT PUT OUT HIS MONEY TO USURY, NOR TAKEN BRIBES AGAINST THE INNOCENT.**

He hath set forth in general the virtues of the upright man; now in particular he toucheth one vice, namely avarice, from which he ought chiefly to be free who desireth to become an heir of the heavenly kingdom. And why he toucheth this particular vice, the cause is, either because "the desire of money (*cupiditas*) is the root of all evils," as the Apostle saith, *to Timothy*, chap. vi,[^2] and by *cupiditas* (desire) he understandeth avarice, which he calleth *philargyria*, that is, the love of money; or because this vice always was, and is, familiar to the Jews. Further, avarice hath place either in frauds, which are committed in contracts otherwise lawful, or in contracts unlawful in their own kind, or in bribes. Of the first kind he saith: "He that sweareth to his neighbour, and deceiveth not." Of the second he saith: "He that hath not put out his money to usury." Of the third he saith: "Nor taken bribes against the innocent," that is, to oppress the innocent. From the Hebrew St. Jerome rendered *qui jurat, ut se affligat* (he that sweareth to afflict himself), for that which we have, "he that sweareth to his neighbour"; but the Hebrew word can be read להרע *leharah*, and it signifieth *to do evil*, namely to himself, that is, to afflict himself, and so St. Jerome read; it can also be read *lehareah*, and it signifieth *to his neighbour*, and so read the Seventy, from whose version the Latin Vulgate edition came forth, and certainly our reading cohereth better with the words above than that of St. Jerome. From this verse it is plainly gathered that the Jews err, who think usury to be lawful.

[Margin: Verse 7.]

**7. HE THAT DOTH THESE THINGS SHALL NOT BE MOVED FOR EVER.**

He answereth the question proposed in the first verse, and saith that they who so live as hath been set forth above shall have a most firm abode in the heavenly tabernacle. Therefore he saith: "He that doth these things," which have been said above, "shall not be moved for ever," that is, he shall dwell secure in the tabernacle of God, and shall rest in his holy hill, without any fear of falling from so happy a place.

[^1]: Translator's note: the Douay-Rheims title is "A psalm of David", without "Unto the end"; Bellarmine's title is translated as he gives it. He does not number the title, and divides the Psalm into seven verses. The Douay-Rheims Bible has five: its verse 1 holds the title and his verse 1; its 2 is his 2; its 3 holds his 3 and 4; its 4 holds his 5 and the first half of his 6; and its 5 holds the rest of his 6 and his 7.

[^2]: Translator's note: Bellarmine does not say which Epistle to Timothy; the words are at I Tim. vi, 10.
