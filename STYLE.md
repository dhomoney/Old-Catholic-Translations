# Style rules for the English translation

Owner: the Translator. Critics check the English against these rules. Fixed word choices are in `GLOSSARY.md`.

## 1. Source

- Translate from the Scribe's cleaned Latin (`latin/psalm-NNN.md`; for other works `latin/<work>/<unit>-NNN.md`) only. The English file has the same path under `english/`.
- The 1866 O'Sullivan translation is not consulted by the Translator.
- Translate what Bellarmine wrote. Do not correct, shorten, soften or expand him.

## 2. Register

- Traditional English in the manner of the Douay-Rheims Bible.
- Second person singular: thou, thee, thy, thine. Plural: ye (subject), you (object).
- Third person singular verbs end in -eth: teacheth, saith, hath, doth.
- Pronouns for God are not capitalised (he, his, him), as in the Douay-Rheims.
- Keep Bellarmine's long periods where English can bear them. Break one only where it would otherwise be unclear, and use a semicolon or colon before a full stop.
- British spelling: honour, labour, judgment (the Douay-Rheims form).
- No modern idiom, no contractions.

## 3. Scripture

- Quote Scripture in the Douay-Rheims (Challoner) wording.
- Psalm numbers and verse numbers follow Bellarmine (Vulgate numbering). Where his verse division differs from the Douay-Rheims, keep his and say so in a note.
- Where Bellarmine quotes a text in words that differ from the Vulgate, translate his words and give the Vulgate reading in a note.
- A quotation he shortens, or fits to the grammar of his own sentence, is translated as he gives it, in the Douay-Rheims wording of the words he keeps, and gets no note.
- A change of word order alone ("founded and rooted" for the Vulgate's "rooted and founded") is translated as he has it and gets no note.
- Where he explains a verse by changing one word of it, change only that word in the Douay-Rheims wording.
- Allusions he does not mark as quotations are worded after the Douay-Rheims but get no quotation marks and no reference.
- Where his argument turns on a Latin word that the Douay-Rheims renders differently in the text quoted, the quotation keeps the Douay-Rheims wording and the Latin word is given beside it in italics, or in a note.
- References: book names in the Douay-Rheims forms, abbreviated and in italics (*Exod.*, *Deut.*, *Ps.*, *Matt.*, *Rom.*, *James*, *Wis.*, *Mal.*, *Acts*, *Heb.*, *Apoc.*, *Mich.*, *Isa.*, *Num.*, *Tim.*, *John*, *Luke*, *Eccles.*, *Ephes.*, *Pet.*, II *Kings* for II *Reg.*); chapter in lower-case Roman numerals; verse in Arabic numerals only where Bellarmine gives one. A wrong reference in the source is kept and corrected in a note.

## 4. Words discussed as words

- When Bellarmine discusses a Latin word as a word (a reading, a spelling, a grammatical form), keep the Latin in italics and give the English in round brackets at its first mention in that passage: *non resurgent* (shall not rise again).
- When he merely repeats words of the verse to explain their meaning, give them in English, in italics.
- The test: if he says what a text, edition or language has or lacks, it is a reading, and the Latin is kept. If he says what the words mean, they go into English.
- Hebrew and Greek are copied exactly as the Latin file gives them, with Bellarmine's own transliteration in italics. They are not corrected; a doubtful form gets a note.
- Latin verse quoted from a classical author is kept in Latin, in italics, with the English in round brackets.

## 5. Layout

- One English paragraph for each Latin paragraph, in the same order.
- Page markers (`<!-- p. N (PDF N) -->`) stand at the same point as in the Latin, moved only to the nearest word boundary.
- Headings, running titles and verse lemmas match the Latin file. Verse lemmas are bold capitals with Bellarmine's verse number.
- Marginal glosses are translated and kept as `[Margin: ...]` in the same place.
- Bellarmine's guillemets (« ») become double quotation marks. His unmarked paraphrases of a verse begin with a capital letter and take no quotation marks, as in the Latin.
- Titles of works are translated and set in italics.

## 6. Translator's notes

- Notes are Markdown footnotes that begin "Translator's note:". They are few.
- A note is allowed for: a difference in verse numbering; a quotation that differs from the Vulgate; a wrong or doubtful reference; a doubtful reading or misprint in the source; a Hebrew or Greek form that needs a word of explanation.
- Notes never argue doctrine and never comment on Bellarmine's opinions.

## 7. Names

- Saints and Fathers in their usual English forms: Jerome, Augustine, Basil, Hilary, Ambrose.
- Biblical names in the Douay-Rheims forms: Esdras, Moses.
- *Septuaginta* is "the Seventy"; *Septuaginta Interpretes* is "the Seventy Interpreters".
- *Propheta* for the Psalmist and *Apostolus* for St. Paul take a capital: the Prophet, the Apostle.
- Capitals otherwise follow the Latin file (Saints, Angels, Psalm, Scripture).

## 8. Works other than the Psalms

- The same rules hold for every work. "Bellarmine" in the rules above means the author in hand.
- Address in the second person singular is "thou", also to the Pope and to the reader (*Sanctitas tua*, thy Holiness; *benevole lector*, kind reader ... thou).
- Quotations the Latin file gives without quotation marks or italics (the Liguori edition prints them so) take double quotation marks in English.
- References to authors and law are put into English words with the numbers kept: Part III, quest. 9, art. 4; Book I, no. 55. Titles of works are translated and set in italics; a title better known in Latin (the *Medulla* of Busembaum) is kept.
- The author's own notes, where the Latin file gives them as a block, are translated in the same place with the same mark: (1). A later editor's note is translated only when god has named it as a needed reading key, and always with the Latin file's label that it is the editor's: `(\*) [Editor's note, Marietti 1879]` in Liguori's *Monitum*. Everything else of an editor stays out.
- A slip of the Scribe is not noted in the English: it is reported to the Scribe, and the English follows the scan. A misprint of the printed edition is translated by the sense and gets a note.
